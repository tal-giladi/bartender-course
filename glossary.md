# Glossary

Bar terms used in this course, A–Z, with the lesson where each is introduced. Hebrew equivalents are on [Hebrew at the bar](references/hebrew-bar-terms.md).

## 0–9

- **100% agave** — tequila whose sugars all come from blue Weber agave; without it, tequila may contain up to 49% other sugars ([02.3](lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md))
- **100% agave tequila** — tequila made only from agave sugars ([05.4](lessons/module-05/lesson-04.md))
- **3-2-1** — Aperol's ratio: 3 parts Prosecco, 2 Aperol, 1 soda (90/60/30 ml) ([05.7](lessons/module-05/lesson-07.md))
- **86 (eighty-six)** — bar and restaurant slang for an item that is finished for the night (also for refusing service to a guest) ([08.7](lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md))

## A

- **ABV (alcohol by volume)** — the percentage of a drink's volume that is pure ethanol, measured at 20 °C; written "% vol" on labels ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **Accuracy** — how close a pour is to its target ([03.2](lessons/module-03/lesson-02.md))
- **Achmash** — Hebrew acronym for the shift manager, the person in charge of the shift ([01.1](lessons/module-01/lesson-01.md))
- **Active recall (testing effect)** — remembering from memory and then checking, which fixes memory better than rereading ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))
- **Aeration** — beating tiny air bubbles into a drink, which makes it lighter, frothier and cloudier; strongest when shaking ([04.1](lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md))
- **Ageing (maturation)** — resting a spirit or wine in wooden barrels so it gains colour, softness and aromas such as vanilla and spice ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **Alcohol-free (UK)** — alcohol removed to no more than 0.05% ABV ([07.5](lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md))
- **Ale / lager** — beer fermented with top-fermenting yeast at warmer temperatures / bottom-fermenting yeast at cool temperatures ([02.8](lessons/module-02/lesson-08.md))
- **Amargo bitters** — Peruvian aromatic bitters dropped on the foam of a Pisco Sour; Angostura is the usual substitute ([06.2](lessons/module-06/lesson-02.md))
- **Amaro** — a bittersweet Italian herbal liqueur, such as Amaro Nonino in the Paper Plane ([06.5](lessons/module-06/lesson-05.md))
- **Aperitif** — a drink served before a meal to open the appetite ([02.7](lessons/module-02/lesson-07.md))
- **Aquafaba** — the liquid from a can of chickpeas, used as a vegan foaming agent instead of egg white ([04.4](lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md))
- **Arak** — dry, usually grape-based anise spirit of the Levant, often 50% ABV or more ([02.5](lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md))
- **Aroma** — what the nose detects; most of what we call flavour ([03.3](lessons/module-03/lesson-03.md))
- **Aromatised wine** — wine flavoured with herbs and spices, with added alcohol; vermouth is one ([02.7](lessons/module-02/lesson-07.md))
- **Astringency** — the dry, gripping feeling from tannin, e.g. strong tea ([03.3](lessons/module-03/lesson-03.md))

## B

- **Back bar** — shelves and fridges behind the bartender for display and premium bottles, wine and beer ([01.2](lessons/module-01/lesson-02.md))
- **Balance** — no element of a drink sticks out unpleasantly ([03.3](lessons/module-03/lesson-03.md))
- **Balance (draught system)** — the setting of gas pressure, temperature and line length so beer pours with the right carbonation ([07.2](lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md))
- **Barback** — the bartender's assistant who keeps the station supplied with ice, glass, bottles and garnish ([01.1](lessons/module-01/lesson-01.md))
- **Bar convention (oz)** — treating 1 US fl oz (29.57 ml) as 30 ml behind the bar ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Bar manager** — manages ordering, costing, menus, schedules and compliance ([01.1](lessons/module-01/lesson-01.md))
- **Bar mat (rail)** — ribbed rubber mat or drip tray where drinks are built and served ([01.2](lessons/module-01/lesson-02.md))
- **Barspoon** — long twisted-stem spoon; as a measure about 5 ml ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Bartender** — the person who takes orders, prepares and serves drinks, handles payment and keeps the station clean and safe ([01.1](lessons/module-01/lesson-01.md))
- **Base** — the main spirit of a drink, usually the largest amount ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))
- **Basic tastes** — sweet, sour, bitter, salty and umami, detected by the tongue ([03.3](lessons/module-03/lesson-03.md))
- **Batch** — a quantity of a drink mixed ahead of time ([03.1](lessons/module-03/lesson-01.md))
- **Beer-clean glass** — a glass free of grease, detergent and rinse-aid residues that break beer foam; checked with the sheeting, salt and lacing tests ([07.1](lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md))
- **Bitters** — concentrated, unsweetened infusions of roots, barks and spices in strong alcohol, used by the dash ([02.6](lessons/module-02/lesson-06.md))
- **Blanco, reposado, añejo, extra añejo** — tequila age classes: unaged, at least 2 months, at least 1 year, at least 3 years in oak ([02.3](lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md))
- **Blend** — mix a drink with crushed ice in an electric blender to make a frozen texture ([04.6](lessons/module-04/lesson-06.md))
- **Body** — how heavy a wine or drink feels in the mouth; rises with alcohol and sugar ([07.3](lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md))
- **Boston shaker** — two-piece shaker: large tin with a small tin (tin-on-tin) or a mixing glass ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Botanicals** — plant flavourings in gin, such as juniper, coriander seed, citrus peel and angelica ([02.2](lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md))
- **Bottle-conditioned** — beer that has had a second fermentation in the bottle and has yeast at the bottom ([07.2](lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md))
- **Bourbon** — US whiskey from at least 51% corn, aged in new charred oak, distilled to no more than 80% ABV ([02.4](lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md))
- **Briefing** — a few minutes before opening when the team hears bookings, specials, the 86 list, allergen news and roles ([08.1](lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md))
- **Brut** — sparkling wine label term; drier than "extra dry" ([02.8](lessons/module-02/lesson-08.md))
- **Buffer** — extra prep on top of the forecast for spills, remakes and a busier night; 10% in this course as a starting point ([08.2](lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md))
- **Build** — make a drink directly in its serving glass, usually over ice, with the carbonated part added last ([04.1](lessons/module-04/lesson-01.md))
- **Burning the ice** — discarding and melting all ice in a well after glass breakage or contamination ([01.6](lessons/module-01/lesson-06.md))

## C

- **Cachaça** — Brazilian spirit distilled from fresh sugar-cane juice ([02.5](lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md))
- **Cage (muselet)** — the wire cage holding a sparkling-wine cork ([07.4](lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md))
- **Caipiroska / Caipirissima** — a Caipirinha made with vodka / with rum (IBA note) ([06.6](lessons/module-06/lesson-06.md))
- **Calibrate** — check a count or tool against a known measure and adjust it ([03.2](lessons/module-03/lesson-02.md))
- **Call-out** — a short standard phrase that warns colleagues what you are doing, such as "behind", "corner", "hot", "knife", "glass" ([08.7](lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md))
- **Carbonation** — carbon dioxide dissolved in a liquid under pressure ([03.6](lessons/module-03/lesson-06.md))
- **Carbonic acid** — weak acid formed by dissolved CO2; makes fizzy drinks taste slightly sour ([03.6](lessons/module-03/lesson-06.md))
- **Centilitre (cl)** — 10 ml; used on older European specs ([03.1](lessons/module-03/lesson-01.md))
- **Channel knife** — tool that cuts a long thin strip of peel ([01.4](lessons/module-01/lesson-04.md))
- **Check-back (closed-loop communication)** — the receiver repeats a message back and the sender confirms it ([08.7](lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md))
- **Churn** — mix a crushed-ice drink by moving a spoon up and down without the palm spin ([04.6](lessons/module-04/lesson-06.md))
- **Citrus vodka** — vodka flavoured with lemon or other citrus ([05.5](lessons/module-05/lesson-05.md))
- **Claw grip** — holding food with fingertips tucked and knuckles guiding the blade ([01.4](lessons/module-01/lesson-04.md))
- **Cleaning** — removing visible dirt and sugar with detergent and water ([01.7](lessons/module-01/lesson-07.md))
- **Clear ice** — ice without trapped air, made by directional freezing ([01.6](lessons/module-01/lesson-06.md))
- **Close (bar close)** — the routine from last orders to locked door: tabs closed, prep stored or discarded, cleaning, restocking, logs and handover note ([08.1](lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md))
- **Cobbler glass** — a small goblet named in the IBA Whiskey Sour spec ([05.5](lessons/module-05/lesson-05.md))
- **Cobbler shaker** — three-piece shaker with a built-in strainer and cap ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Cocktail family** — a group of drinks that share one structure: the same kinds of ingredients in similar proportions, the same method and usually the same glass ([06.1](lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md))
- **Cognac** — grape brandy from the Cognac region of France; VS, VSOP, Napoléon and XO mean at least 2, 4, 6 and 10 years ([02.4](lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md))
- **Collins** — a sour lengthened with soda water and served in a tall glass full of ice ([06.1](lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md))
- **Collins glass** — taller, narrower glass, about 300–410 ml ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **Consistency** — how close repeated pours are to each other ([03.2](lessons/module-03/lesson-02.md))
- **Cork taint ("corked")** — a fault that gives wine a musty, wet-cardboard smell ([07.4](lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md))
- **Coupe** — stemmed glass with a shallow rounded bowl ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **Cracked ice** — cubes broken into 2–4 pieces ([01.6](lessons/module-01/lesson-06.md))
- **Crema** — the fine foam on fresh espresso, made of coffee oils and gas ([05.7](lessons/module-05/lesson-07.md))
- **Cross-utilisation** — choosing menu drinks that share ingredients, to keep prep short and waste low ([06.8](lessons/module-06/lesson-08.md))
- **Crushed (pebble) ice** — small pieces of ice for juleps, swizzles and tiki drinks ([01.6](lessons/module-01/lesson-06.md))

## D

- **Dash** — a quick shake from a bitters bottle, about 0.8 ml in this course ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Date marking** — labelling prepared food with the date (and time) it was made or must be discarded ([08.2](lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md))
- **Dead drink** — a finished drink that waited too long before serving and is watery, warm or flat; in the service simulation, more than 30 seconds ([08.5](lessons/module-08/lesson-05.md))
- **De-alcoholised** — alcohol removed to no more than 0.5% ABV (UK guidance) ([07.5](lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md))
- **Degrees Brix (°Bx)** — grams of sugar per 100 g of solution ([03.4](lessons/module-03/lesson-04.md))
- **Density** — weight of 1 ml of a liquid; water 1.00, 1:1 syrup 1.23, 2:1 syrup about 1.33 g/ml ([03.2](lessons/module-03/lesson-02.md))
- **Dilution** — water added to a drink by melting ice ([01.6](lessons/module-01/lesson-06.md))
- **Dilution %** — (weight after − weight before) ÷ weight before × 100 ([03.5](lessons/module-03/lesson-05.md))
- **Distillation** — heating a fermented liquid so the alcohol evaporates before water and is collected as a stronger spirit ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **Double straining** — straining through a Hawthorne and a fine strainer together to remove ice shards and pulp ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Double straining (fine straining)** — straining through a Hawthorne and a fine mesh strainer at the same time, to catch ice shards, pulp, herbs and egg ([04.5](lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md))
- **Draught (draft) beer** — beer served from a keg through a tap ([07.2](lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md))
- **Draught line cleaning** — cleaning beer lines with a caustic cleaner, about every two weeks per the Brewers Association ([08.6](lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md))
- **Dry shake** — shaking without ice first, then with ice, to build a thicker foam from egg white or aquafaba ([04.4](lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md))
- **Dry / wet (Martini)** — less / more vermouth in proportion to gin ([05.2](lessons/module-05/lesson-02.md))

## E

- **Elderflower liqueur** — a sweet liqueur flavoured with elder blossoms, used in the Hugo Spritz ([06.5](lessons/module-06/lesson-05.md))
- **Error %** — (measured − target) ÷ target × 100 ([03.2](lessons/module-03/lesson-02.md))
- **Express** — squeeze or twist a citrus peel so its oils spray onto the drink's surface ([04.7](lessons/module-04/lesson-07.md))

## F

- **Fermentation** — yeast turning sugar into ethanol and carbon dioxide ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **Few dashes** — an unfixed small number of dashes, usually 2–3; follow the house spec ([05.3](lessons/module-05/lesson-03.md))
- **FIFO** — first in, first out: use the oldest stock or prep first ([01.7](lessons/module-01/lesson-07.md))
- **Fine strainer** — small conical mesh sieve for double straining ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Fizz** — a shaken sour topped with a splash of soda and served in a tall glass without ice ([06.1](lessons/module-06/lesson-01.md))
- **Float** — the last, lightest layer poured on top of a drink ([04.5](lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md))
- **Flor** — the layer of yeast that protects fino and manzanilla sherry from air ([02.7](lessons/module-02/lesson-07.md))
- **Forecast** — the expected number of each drink for a shift, usually based on past sales ([08.2](lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md))
- **Fortified wine** — wine with added grape spirit, such as sherry and port ([02.7](lessons/module-02/lesson-07.md))
- **Free pouring** — pouring from a bottle with a speed pourer, timed with a count instead of a jigger ([03.2](lessons/module-03/lesson-02.md))
- **French (Parisian) shaker** — two-piece metal shaker with no built-in strainer ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))

## G

- **Garnish** — anything added to the finished drink for aroma, flavour, look or identification ([04.7](lessons/module-04/lesson-07.md))
- **Ginger beer** — a fizzy, spicy ginger soft drink, hotter and less sweet than ginger ale ([05.6](lessons/module-05/lesson-06.md))
- **Glasswasher** — a machine that washes and sanitises racks of glasses with a very hot final rinse or a chemical sanitiser ([08.6](lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md))

## H

- **Half rim** — salt or sugar on the outside of half the glass rim ([04.7](lessons/module-04/lesson-07.md))
- **Half salt rim** — salt on the outside of half the glass rim, so the guest can choose ([05.4](lessons/module-05/lesson-04.md))
- **Handover note** — a short written note for the next shift: what ran out, what was ordered, what broke, what prep is in the fridge ([08.1](lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md))
- **Hand press (Mexican elbow)** — hinged citrus juicer; fruit goes in cut side down ([01.4](lessons/module-01/lesson-04.md))
- **Hawthorne strainer** — flat strainer with a spring around the edge, used on tins and mixing glasses ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Head bartender** — senior bartender who runs the bar during a shift, trains staff and sets specs ([01.1](lessons/module-01/lesson-01.md))
- **Head (beer)** — the collar of foam on a poured beer, ideally about 2–3 cm; it carries aroma and is partly beer ([07.1](lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md))
- **Heads, heart, tails** — the first, middle and last parts of a distillation run; only the heart is kept for drinking ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **Headspace** — empty space left at the top of a bottle so the contents can expand ([08.3](lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md))
- **Heat damage** — a fault from storing wine too warm: cooked, jammy taste ([07.4](lessons/module-07/lesson-04.md))
- **Hefeweizen** — German-style unfiltered wheat beer, served with its yeast ([07.2](lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md))
- **Henry's law** — the amount of gas dissolved in a liquid is proportional to that gas's pressure above it ([03.6](lessons/module-03/lesson-06.md))
- **Highball** — tall straight glass, 240–350 ml ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **Holdback** — liquid left clinging to the ice after straining ([03.5](lessons/module-03/lesson-05.md))
- **House spec** — a bar's own written version of a drink, used by every bartender there ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))

## I

- **IBA** — International Bartenders Association; publishes the official list of classic cocktail specs in ml ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))
- **Ice well** — insulated bin of ice built into the counter in front of the bartender ([01.2](lessons/module-01/lesson-02.md))
- **Interleaving** — mixing different kinds of items in one practice session ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))
- **Inventory** — all the stock a bar holds for sale ([08.4](lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md))

## J

- **Jigger** — double-ended measuring cup, e.g. 30/60 or 30/45 ml ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **John Collins** — the IBA's Collins spec (gin, lemon, syrup, soda, built on ice); with Old Tom gin it becomes a Tom Collins ([06.3](lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md))
- **Julep strainer** — perforated spoon-shaped strainer used in a mixing glass ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))

## K

- **Keg** — a pressurised metal container of draught beer ([07.2](lessons/module-07/lesson-02.md))
- **Keg dolly** — a small wheeled trolley for moving kegs without lifting them ([08.6](lessons/module-08/lesson-06.md))
- **Kosher wine** — wine made under kosher supervision, handled only by Jews from crushing onwards ([07.3](lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md))

## L

- **Lacing** — rings of foam left on the inside of a clean glass as a beer is drunk ([07.1](lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md))
- **Layering** — pouring liquids of different densities so they sit in separate bands; heaviest (most sugar, least alcohol) first ([04.5](lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md))
- **Leitner system** — flashcards in numbered boxes; a correct card moves up a box, a missed card returns to box 1 ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))
- **Lengthener** — a long mixer, usually fizzy, that tops up a drink (soda, ginger beer, Prosecco) ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))
- **Lift** — one stroke of the bar spoon from the bottom of a built drink upwards, to mix without losing fizz ([04.2](lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md))
- **Lime cordial** — sweetened, preserved lime juice concentrate; the classic Gimlet ingredient ([06.5](lessons/module-06/lesson-05.md))
- **Lined copper mug** — a copper mug with a stainless steel or nickel lining, safe for acidic drinks ([05.6](lessons/module-05/lesson-06.md))
- **Liqueur** — sweetened, flavoured spirit; in the EU at least 15% ABV and 100 g sugar per litre for most types ([02.6](lessons/module-02/lesson-06.md))
- **London dry gin** — gin re-distilled with natural botanicals, with nothing added afterwards except water and a trace of sugar; a method, not a place ([02.2](lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md))
- **Long drink** — topped with a mixer over ice in a tall glass ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **Louche** — the milky cloud that forms when water is added to an anise spirit ([02.5](lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md))
- **Low alcohol (UK)** — 1.2% ABV or less ([07.5](lessons/module-07/lesson-05.md))

## M

- **Mevushal** — kosher wine that has been heat-treated, so it stays kosher when anyone opens and pours it ([07.3](lessons/module-07/lesson-03.md))
- **Mezcal** — Mexican agave spirit from many agave species, often smoky; categories mezcal, artesanal and ancestral ([02.3](lessons/module-02/lesson-03.md))
- **Mint sprig** — a small stem of mint with its leaves ([05.6](lessons/module-05/lesson-06.md))
- **Mise en place** — French for "everything in its place"; the prep done before service ([01.1](lessons/module-01/lesson-01.md))
- **Mixer** — a non-alcoholic ingredient that lengthens a drink, such as soda, tonic, cola or juice ([04.2](lessons/module-04/lesson-02.md))
- **Mixing glass** — thick vessel of about 500–700 ml for stirring drinks ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Modifier** — a smaller ingredient that changes the base's flavour, such as vermouth or a liqueur ([05.1](lessons/module-05/lesson-01.md))
- **Muddle** — press fruit, herbs or sugar with a muddler, with a twist, to release juice, oils and flavour ([04.5](lessons/module-04/lesson-05.md))
- **Muddler** — stick for pressing fruit, herbs or sugar in a tin or glass ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))

## N

- **Navy strength** — gin bottled at about 57% ABV ([02.2](lessons/module-02/lesson-02.md))
- **Nick & Nora** — small stemmed glass with a tall rounded bowl ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **Nonic** — a pint glass with a bulge near the top ([07.1](lessons/module-07/lesson-01.md))
- **Nucleation site** — a scratch, ice surface or particle where bubbles form and gas escapes ([03.6](lessons/module-03/lesson-06.md))

## O

- **Old Tom gin** — an older, slightly sweeter and softer style of gin; the IBA uses it for the Tom Collins ([06.3](lessons/module-06/lesson-03.md))
- **On the rocks** — served over ice in a short glass ([01.5](lessons/module-01/lesson-05.md))
- **Opening (bar opening)** — the routine that takes the bar from locked and empty to ready for the first guest: checks, temperatures, stock pull, prep, setup and briefing ([08.1](lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md))
- **Orange curaçao** — an orange liqueur, close to triple sec, used in the Mai Tai ([06.4](lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md))
- **Order quantity** — par minus on hand, rounded up to whole units ([08.4](lessons/module-08/lesson-04.md))
- **Orgeat** — a sweet almond syrup used in the Mai Tai; contains tree nuts ([06.4](lessons/module-06/lesson-04.md))
- **Oxidation** — the slow change caused by air after opening; makes vermouth and wine flat or sour ([02.7](lessons/module-02/lesson-07.md))

## P

- **Par (par level)** — the amount of an item the bar wants on hand after each delivery or at the start of a shift ([08.1](lessons/module-08/lesson-01.md))
- **Parts** — a recipe written as a ratio, so it works at any size; ml per part = total ÷ sum of parts ([03.1](lessons/module-03/lesson-01.md))
- **Pass** — the spot where finished drinks wait for servers to collect them ([08.7](lessons/module-08/lesson-07.md))
- **Pasteurised egg white** — egg white heated enough to kill bacteria such as Salmonella, sold in cartons; safe to use raw ([04.4](lessons/module-04/lesson-04.md))
- **Peat** — decomposed plant matter burned to dry malted barley, giving smoky whisky ([02.4](lessons/module-02/lesson-04.md))
- **Perfect (Manhattan)** — vermouth split between sweet and dry ([05.2](lessons/module-05/lesson-02.md))
- **Pisco** — grape brandy from Peru and Chile ([02.5](lessons/module-02/lesson-05.md))
- **Pith** — the bitter white layer under citrus skin ([01.4](lessons/module-01/lesson-04.md))
- **Planned dilution** — water added to a batch served without ice-mixing, as a fraction of the undiluted volume ([03.1](lessons/module-03/lesson-01.md))
- **POS** — point of sale: the till or touchscreen where orders are entered and paid ([01.2](lessons/module-01/lesson-02.md))
- **Pot still / column still** — batch still that keeps more flavour / continuous tower still that reaches higher strength ([02.1](lessons/module-02/lesson-01.md))
- **Pour spout (speed pourer)** — spout fitted to a bottle for a steady stream ([01.3](lessons/module-01/lesson-03.md))
- **Pre-diluted batch** — a batch that already contains the water ice would have added, poured straight into a chilled glass ([08.3](lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md))
- **Pre-mix** — a batch of a drink's ingredients without water, still shaken or stirred on ice for each serving ([08.3](lessons/module-08/lesson-03.md))
- **Prep list** — a list of what to prepare and how much, calculated from the forecast plus a buffer minus what is already in date ([08.2](lessons/module-08/lesson-02.md))
- **Professional judgment** — a statement based on experience, not on a published source; labelled as such in this course ([06.7](lessons/module-06/lesson-07.md))
