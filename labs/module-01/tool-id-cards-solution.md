# Tool and glass identification cards — solution

| # | Name | One use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jigger (double / hourglass) | Measuring spirits and modifiers, e.g. 30 and 45 ml |
| 2 | Boston shaker, tin-on-tin | Shaking drinks with ice |
| 3 | Hawthorne strainer | Straining a shaken drink from the tin; push forward to close the gate |
| 4 | Fine strainer | Double straining to remove ice shards and pulp |
| 5 | Julep strainer | Straining a stirred drink from a mixing glass |
| 6 | Bar spoon | Stirring; measuring a barspoon (≈ 5 ml); layering |
| 7 | Mixing glass | Stirring spirit-only drinks such as a Martini or Negroni |
| 8 | Muddler | Pressing fruit, herbs or sugar in the tin or glass |
| 9 | Hand citrus press | Juicing lemons and limes |
| 10 | Y-peeler | Cutting wide citrus peels for twists |
| 11 | Paring knife | Cutting wheels, wedges and trimming peels |
| 12 | Channel knife | Cutting long thin citrus twists |
| 13 | Pour spout (speed pourer) | Steady pouring from a bottle; free pouring |
| 14 | Waiter's friend (wine key) | Opening wine bottles; cutting foil |
| 15 | Rocks / old fashioned glass | Spirit-forward drinks over ice, e.g. Old Fashioned, Negroni |
| 16 | Highball glass | Spirit + mixer over ice, e.g. gin and tonic |
| 17 | Coupe | Drinks served up (no ice), e.g. Daiquiri |
| 18 | Cocktail (martini) glass | Drinks served up, e.g. Martini |
| 19 | Ice scoop | Taking ice from the well; never use a glass |
| 20 | Cobbler shaker | Shaking at home; its top strains the drink |
