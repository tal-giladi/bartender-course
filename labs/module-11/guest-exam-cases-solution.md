# Guest scenarios exam — model answers (lesson 11.5)

Responses that would score 10/10 with the scenario rubric. Your words can differ; the decisions and the rules should not. Legal references as checked in Module 9 on 2026-10-18 (Penal Law on Nevo, consolidated to 2 July 2026).

## Case A — the birthday table

Serve the four pints to the four guests who showed valid ID. To the birthday girl, privately and warmly: "Happy birthday! I'm sorry, I need a physical ID with a date of birth to serve alcohol, and a student card doesn't show it. If your friend can bring your ID card from the car, I'll make your mojito straight away. In the meantime, can I make you a virgin mojito on the house?" Do not accept the cousin's word. Watch the table: if a pint or a mojito made for someone else is passed to her, take it back politely and tell the manager. If the group argues, call the manager. Log the refusal if the house asks.

Rule: Penal Law s. 193A — no sale or serving of alcohol to a minor; if a minor was served, you are presumed to have known unless they showed an ID saying they were adult; the bar's licence condition requires anyone who wants to drink to show ID (statutory + licence condition). Safety-critical: serving her (or letting her drink a friend's drink) without a physical ID showing 18+.

## Case B — one for the road

Tell the manager (achmash). Then, privately and calmly: "I can't pour you any more alcohol tonight. It's the law, and I want you to get home safely. Let me get you a big glass of water and something to eat, and I'll call you a taxi." About the keys: "Please leave the car tonight; I'll order the taxi now." No whisky, no "small one". If he insists on driving, the manager decides on calling the police (100). Keep an eye on him until the taxi comes; if he becomes unwell (vomiting, confused, hard to wake), do not leave him alone and call an ambulance (101). Write it in the incident log.

Rule: Penal Law s. 193(c) — the owner or a worker of a business that sells alcohol may not supply alcohol to a drunk person or encourage them to drink (statutory); signs of intoxication from official guidance (Liquor & Gaming NSW, lesson 09.4); people planning to drive should not drink (gov.il). Safety-critical: serving any alcohol; letting him drive; leaving him alone if unwell.

## Case C — the egg allergy

"Thank you for telling me — yes, our Pisco Sour has egg white, so I won't make it the usual way. Is a trace a problem for you, or only the egg itself?" If any trace matters, or you are not sure: "Our aquafaba is made in the same kitchen as the egg drinks, so I can't promise it's egg-free. I can make you a Pisco Sour without foam, or a Paloma, with a freshly washed shaker." If only the egg itself matters and the guest accepts: aquafaba from the closed can opened now (not the prepared tin), a freshly washed shaker set, fresh ice, clean hands. Mark the ticket "egg allergy", serve it yourself and check in. Never say "it should be fine".

Rule: egg is one of the 14 allergens (EU 1169/2011 Annex II, adopted in Israel; Ministry of Health guide 2025); never guess; prevent cross-contact with a clean set (professional standard, lesson 09.3); raw egg only pasteurised or replaced by aquafaba (course rule, lesson 04.4). Safety-critical: guessing; using the egg white; using an unwashed shaker that held egg.

## Scoring notes

- A response that is safe but forgets to involve the manager in case B scores 1 on Team (total 9): still passes.
- "I'll make it with aquafaba, no problem" without asking about traces or checking the kitchen is a guess: 0 in Safety and law, safety-critical.
- Serving the four pints is correct; refusing the whole table because one guest has no ID is not required by law (it may be house policy) and scores 1 on Guest care if done bluntly.
