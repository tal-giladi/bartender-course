# Model answers — ten scripted guests (lesson 07.6)

Open only after you have written all ten. Other answers that fit the guest's words and the rules also earn the point. Item numbers refer to `drinks-list.md`. Styles, temperatures and pairings follow lessons 07.1–07.5; specific choices are professional judgment.

## Guest 1 — light, not bitter
- **Questions:** "Draught or bottle?" / "Crisp like a lager, or a bit fruity?"
- **Options:** B1 house lager (crisp, light bitterness, 4.9%) or B2 wheat (fruity, low bitterness).
- **Say:** "Our house lager is crisp and easy, and the wheat beer is soft and fruity with almost no bitterness."
- **Serve:** B1 at 3–7 °C in a pint or tall glass; B2 in a tall glass with a bigger head. 45° pour, head 2–3 cm (07.2).
- **Avoid:** B3 IPA (clearly bitter).

## Guest 2 — sea bream, white, soft and round
- **Questions:** "Crisp and zesty, or soft and round?" (answer: soft) / "A glass each or a bottle?"
- **Options:** W3 oaked Chardonnay (full, creamy) or W2 Pinot Grigio (light and neutral, less zesty than the Sauvignon Blanc).
- **Say:** "The Chardonnay is round and creamy with a touch of oak; the Pinot Grigio is lighter and gentle."
- **Serve:** W3 lightly chilled (10–13 °C); W2 chilled (7–10 °C).
- **Why not W1:** zesty, high acidity, which the guest called "sour".

## Guest 3 — big red with steak
- **Questions:** "Big and structured, or big and spicy?" / "A glass or a bottle?"
- **Options:** W8 Cabernet Sauvignon (firm tannin, 14.5%) or W9 Syrah (spicy, dark fruit).
- **Say:** "The Cabernet is big and structured — great with the fat of the steak; the Syrah is rich with a peppery edge."
- **Serve:** 15–18 °C (chill briefly if the bar is warm, 07.4); fat in the meat softens the tannin (07.6).

## Guest 4 — pregnant, festive (safety-critical)
- **Questions:** "Bubbly or a cocktail-style drink?" / (confirm) "No alcohol at all, right?"
- **Options:** S3 alcohol-free sparkling (0.0%) in a flute, or Z3 bitter spritz (0.0%) in a wine glass.
- **Say:** "Our alcohol-free sparkling is 0.0% — I'll pour it in a flute for you. Or a 0.0 bitter spritz if you'd like something more like a cocktail."
- **Safety:** do not recommend B7 (0.4%); if asked about it, state the 0.4%. Ministry of Health: avoid alcohol completely in pregnancy (07.5).
- **Serve:** well chilled, two-stage pour (07.4).

## Guest 5 — IPA fan, stronger and darker
- **Questions:** "Dark and roasted, or rich and fruity?" / "How strong is OK — around 5%, or a strong one?"
- **Options:** B4 stout (dark, roasted, 5%) or B5 Belgian-style strong ale (9%, served in a 250 ml tulip).
- **Say:** "The stout is dark with coffee and chocolate; the Belgian strong ale is rich and fruity — it's 9%, so it comes in a smaller glass."
- **Serve:** B4 at 7–13 °C; B5 at 10–13 °C, poured to leave the yeast in the bottle (07.2). State the 9%.

## Guest 6 — tourist, something Israeli
- **Questions:** "Wine, or our local anise spirit, arak?" / "Something light, or a full drink?"
- **Options:** W5 Israeli dry rosé from the Golan, or arak and grapefruit (lesson 06.7).
- **Say:** "This rosé is from the Golan, one of Israel's eight official wine regions; or try arak with grapefruit, a very Israeli serve — it's strong anise, softened with juice."
- **Honesty:** say what is verified (region on the label) and what is your opinion; mention the arak's strength (06.7).

## Guest 7 — engagement, not too sweet, bottle for six
- **Questions:** "Very dry, or fruity but not sweet?" / "Opened at the table now?"
- **Options:** S1 cava brut (driest) or S2 Prosecco extra dry (fruitier, slightly sweeter than brut).
- **Say:** "The cava is brut — dry and elegant; the Prosecco is fruitier and a touch softer."
- **Serve:** 6–10 °C, ice bucket with water; open safely at the table — thumb on the cork, 45°, away from people, turn the bottle, no pop; two-stage pour into six flutes (07.4). A 750 ml bottle gives six glasses of 125 ml.
- **Note:** brut is drier than extra dry (02.8).

## Guest 8 — no alcohol, not sweet
- **Questions:** "Bitter, sour or both?" / "Long and fizzy, or short?"
- **Options:** Z1 grapefruit and salt highball (0.0%, bittersweet, salty) or Z3 bitter spritz (0.0%).
- **Say:** "The grapefruit highball is tart with a pinch of salt; the bitter spritz is dry and bitter-orange — both alcohol-free, 0.0."
- **Safety:** guest avoids alcohol completely, so not B7 (0.4%) without stating it; 0.0 options first (07.5).

## Guest 9 — intoxicated, "strongest beer" (safety-critical)
- **Do not** recommend or serve any alcohol, including B5.
- **Say (calm, private, not in front of the group if possible):** "I can't pour you another drink tonight, but I can get you water, something to eat, or a 0.0 beer."
- **Then:** tell the manager or shift lead; ask friends to help get him home safely (Module 9 covers refusal in detail).

## Guest 10 — observant friend, kosher red, smooth (safety-critical)
- **Questions:** "Smooth and fruity is good?" / "Glasses or a bottle?"
- **Options:** W7 Merlot (kosher, mevushal, soft) — the best match, since any staff member may open and pour it. W8 Cabernet is kosher but not mevushal and firmer, so less "smooth".
- **Say:** "Our Merlot is kosher and mevushal — soft and plummy. I'll show you the bottle."
- **Safety:** show the certification and the word mevushal on the label. If they choose a non-mevushal kosher wine, bring the bottle sealed and ask how they would like it opened and poured (07.3).
