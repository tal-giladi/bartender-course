# ABV and standard-drink worksheet — solution (lesson 02.1)

Standard drink = 14 g (Israeli Ministry of Health sheet, checked 2026-10-17). Small rounding differences (±0.1 g) are fine.

## Part A

| # | Serve | ml of alcohol | Grams | Standard drinks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 330 ml beer at 5% | 16.5 | 13.0 | 0.93 |
| 2 | 500 ml beer at 4.5% | 22.5 | 17.8 | 1.27 |
| 3 | 150 ml wine at 12.5% | 18.75 | 14.8 | 1.06 |
| 4 | 100 ml sparkling wine at 11% | 11.0 | 8.7 | 0.62 |
| 5 | 30 ml vodka at 40% | 12.0 | 9.5 | 0.68 |
| 6 | 45 ml anise spirit at 50% | 22.5 | 17.8 | 1.27 |
| 7 | 60 ml whisky at 43% | 25.8 | 20.4 | 1.45 |
| 8 | 50 ml vermouth at 16% | 8.0 | 6.3 | 0.45 |
| 9 | 75 ml port at 20% | 15.0 | 11.8 | 0.85 |
| 10 | 330 ml alcohol-free beer at 0.4% | 1.32 | 1.0 | 0.07 |

Point to notice: the 45 ml anise spirit (6) holds more alcohol than the large beer (2) would suggest by size, and a "0.4%" beer (10) is not zero. Tell a guest who must avoid alcohol completely.

## Part B

| # | Total ml of alcohol | Final volume | ABV | Standard drinks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 18 + 6 = 24 | 120 ml | 20.0% | 24 × 0.789 = 18.9 g → 1.35 |
| 12 | 20 | 200 ml | 10.0% | 15.8 g → 1.13 |
| 13 | 24 + 5.1 = 29.1 | 115 ml | 25.3% (32.3% before the water) | 23.0 g → 1.64 |

Point to notice: drink 13 is short and looks small, but it holds more alcohol than drinks 11 and 12.

## Part C

14. 100 proof ÷ 2 = 50% ABV.
15. 45% × 2 = 90 proof.
