# Unbalanced drinks — answer key (lesson 03.7)

For the partner, and for the taster after all six diagnoses are written.

| Card | Fault | Signature the taster should notice | Evidence | Best fix | Rescue possible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Too sweet | sweetness first and lingering, cloying finish | normal weight and dilution | remake with 22.5 ml syrup | yes, add lemon 5 ml at a time and taste, but the drink grows; remake is better |
| B | Too sour | sharp and puckering, sweetness hidden | normal weight and dilution | remake with 22.5 ml lemon | yes, add syrup 5 ml at a time and taste |
| C | Under-diluted | warm, thick, sweet and sharp at once | dilution well under 10%; warm (often above 10 °C) | shake 12–15 s with fresh ice | yes: shake again with fresh ice |
| D | Over-diluted | thin, watery, pale, all flavours weak | dilution far above the reference (often 45% or more) | remake with fresh dry cubes, strain at once | no: water cannot be removed |
| E | Flat | few or no bubbles, dull, tastes sweeter than the reference | fizz 1–2 of 5 at 0 min | rebuild: cold soda, cold glass full of cubes, gentle pour, one lift | no: lost CO2 does not come back |
| F | Too bitter / astringent | bitter finish, drying grip on the tongue | normal weight and dilution | 1–2 drops saline as a quick rescue; remake with tea brewed 4 minutes | partly: saline softens it, the tea is the real fix |

Common mix-ups: C diagnosed as "too sweet" (warm drinks taste sweeter; check temperature and dilution first); D diagnosed as "not enough syrup" (everything is weak, not just sweetness); F diagnosed as "too sour" (bitterness and astringency are different from sourness).

Expected dilution with this mix (lesson 03.5 reference): about 20–30% after a 15 s shake. Card C typically 3–8%, card D typically 45–60%.
