# Intoxication cards — reference answers (lesson 09.4)

The traffic-light levels are this course's working tool (professional judgment) built on the NSW categories of speech, balance, coordination and behaviour. Signs are not conclusive alone; look for patterns.

## Part A

| # | Level | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Green | Serve normally; keep an eye on pace. |
| 2 | Amber | No round of shots; offer water and food; one drink at a time; tell the team. |
| 3 | Green (watch) | Serve; she may have had more than she says, so watch the next drinks closely. |
| 4 | Red (coordination and confusion) | No more alcohol; refuse privately; water, food, taxi; manager. |
| 5 | Red, possibly unwell | No alcohol; check on her, ask friends what she drank; signs of alcohol poisoning → 101; do not leave her alone. |
| 6 | Green | Slow speech and a stick are not signs of alcohol here; serve normally and do not assume. |
| 7 | Red (slurred speech, leaning) | No more alcohol; refuse privately with the manager's support; water, food, a way home. |
| 8 | Red by association | Ask who the drinks are for; you may not supply the refused guest through a friend. Serve the friend only if the drink is for them. |
| 9 | Amber (fast drinking) | Slow the pace: water with the next round, food, no doubles or shots. |
| 10 | Red (aggression) | No alcohol; keep the bar between you; manager or security; police (100) if needed. |
| 11 | Green, but driving | Offer a 0.0% beer or a zero-proof drink and say the ABV of anything that is not 0.0%; the gov.il guidance says people planning to drive should avoid alcohol. If she insists on alcohol and shows no signs, follow house policy and tell the manager. |
| 12 | Red, possibly alcohol poisoning | No alcohol; check her with the manager; signs (pale, vomiting, confusion, slow breathing) → 101; stay with her. |

## Part C — what a good refusal contains

1. Private, calm, "I can't serve you more alcohol tonight; it's the law and our policy", water, food and a taxi; the manager knows; incident-log entry. (Full model answer in the lesson.)
2. "I'm glad you're feeling better. I still can't serve you alcohol tonight, but I can get you a soft drink or a coffee." Once refused, no alcohol again that night.
3. Serve no shots to the unsteady guest; tell the group privately and kindly: "I can make five, but not for him tonight. Can I get him some water?" Offer the group food. If the group is pushing him to drink, do not serve the round; involve the manager.
