# Legal cases and ID drill — reference answers (lesson 09.5)

Research date 2026-10-18. A learning summary, not legal advice.

## Legal cases

| # | Answer | Rule and type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not allowed (the son's beer) | Penal Law s. 193A(b): an owner or worker of a business serving alcohol for drinking on the premises may not sell or serve alcohol to a minor; no family exception in that subsection (statutory). Serve the son a soft drink. |
| 2 | Refuse alcohol (house policy and safe practice) | Penal Law s. 193A(c1): the defence is that the minor *showed a document*; a phone photo is easy to fake (professional judgment). Licence condition: anyone who wants to drink must show an ID. |
| 3 | Not allowed | Licensing of Businesses Law s. 2(a1): 23:00–06:00 ban; the First Schedule exemption covers drinking on the premises only (statutory). |
| 4 | Allowed | Same section: a bar is exempt for drinks served for drinking on the premises (statutory). |
| 5 | House policy decides | ID age thresholds are house policy. Apologise, explain it is the house rule for everyone, and back your colleague up. |
| 6 | Not allowed (the selling part) | Police licence conditions for items 4.8 and 4.2: no one under 18 employed selling alcohol (licence condition). Other work for a 17-year-old also falls under youth employment law, which this course does not cover: ask the employer. |
| 7 | Not allowed at the bar | Smoking Law, Schedule item 11: smoking only in the separate room; the holder must act (statutory). Ask the guest to move to the smoking room. |
| 8 | Not allowed | Smoking Law s. 2B: the holder may not place an ashtray in the non-smoking public area (statutory). |
| 9 | House policy decides | A 0.0% beer is below the 2% ABV "intoxicating drink" line of the Licensing of Businesses Law; this course did not confirm the Penal Law uses the same line, and many venues refuse as house policy. Never serve anything with alcohol. |
| 10 | Allowed to serve him (he is 18 and ID'd); a free shot is house policy | Not a minor. Free "birthday shots" are a house decision; if he shows signs of intoxication, Penal Law s. 193(c) forbids supplying or encouraging. |
| 11 | Not allowed: put it back up today | Penal Law s. 193A(d)–(e): not displaying the sign counts as breaching a licence condition; the police spec sets size, wording, height and lighting (statutory + licence condition). |
| 12 | Not allowed for the drunk guest | Penal Law s. 193(c): no supply to a drunk person; passing it through a friend is still supply (statutory). Serve the friend his own drink only. |
