# Model answers — complaint, substitution and unclear-order cases (lesson 09.2)

These are examples, not the only correct wording. A good answer avoids arguing or blaming, offers a clear solution or two concrete options, and sends money decisions to the manager.

1. **Too sour Margarita.** Listen; "Sorry it's not how you like it. I can make it a little sweeter, or make you something else, maybe a Paloma?" Bin, remake with about +5 ml syrup or a little less lime (not by topping up), taste-check with a straw, serve, "Thanks for telling me," check in after a few sips. Log the waste. If several guests say the same, check the lime juice and syrup.
2. **Flat, watery spritz.** "Sorry, that waited too long. I'll make you a fresh one right now." Remake at no charge, serve straight away. Afterwards: why did it wait? Fix sequencing (lesson 08.5) or tell the server it is ready.
3. **Chipped glass.** Take the glass away at once: "I'm so sorry. Are you OK, no cut?" Offer first aid if needed. Tell the manager. Make a fresh drink in a checked glass. Check other glasses from the same rack; if the chip may have fallen into the ice well, burn the ice (lesson 01.6). Log it.
4. **"Add a free shot."** "I pour the house measure so every drink is the same, sorry. I can make it as a double if you'd like a stronger one; that's charged as a double. Or I can make you something more spirit-forward." Offer the double only because he seems sober (lesson 09.4). No free alcohol unless the manager decides.
5. **Out of Cointreau, Cosmopolitan.** "We're out of Cointreau tonight. I can make it with another triple sec; it's slightly sweeter, so I'll balance it with the lime. Is that OK?" Pour only after "yes". If there is no triple sec at all: say so and offer a different drink by name.
6. **"Sweet but very dry, not too strong" Martini.** "A Martini can go two ways: drier means less vermouth and more spirit, which is stronger. Would you like a softer Martini with more vermouth, a bit sweeter and less boozy, or something fruity like a French Martini?" Check back the choice.
7. **"Mojito, no mint, no sugar, no soda."** That leaves rum and lime. "Without mint, sugar and soda it would be rum with lime. Would you like a Daiquiri, which is rum, lime and a little sugar, shaken? Or rum and lime on ice?" Check back.
8. **Twenty-minute wait, wants free drinks.** Listen to the end. "I'm really sorry about the wait and that you weren't served in order. Your drinks are my next ticket." Make them at once. "About the bill, let me ask my manager." Fetch the manager; do not promise. Afterwards, check why they were not acknowledged (lesson 09.1).
