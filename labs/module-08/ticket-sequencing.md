# Ticket sequencing worksheet (lesson 08.5)

Use the service simulation's time table from lesson 08.5. Short version: glass 2 s; ice 3 s per glass; pour 4 s (2 s for each next pour from the same bottle in a row); top 3 s; muddle 8 s; shake 12 s (one or two tins); stir in mixing glass 20 s (one or two); stir a built drink 5 s; strain 3 s per drink; garnish 4 s per drink; draught beer 8 s; wine 6 s; serve ticket 3 s. Four tins, two mixing glasses. A finished drink waiting more than 30 s before serve is dead.

## 1. Which ticket first?

Both tickets are waiting now. Ticket A: 4 Moscow Mules. Ticket B: 1 glass of house wine.
a) Work out the time of each ticket (Moscow Mule: highball glass, ice, vodka 45, lime 10, top ginger beer, lime slice; IBA build).
b) Average ticket time for A then B, and for B then A. Which order do you choose?

## 2. Order of work

Ticket: 1 Manhattan (stirred, strained, cherry), 1 Aperol Spritz (wine glass, ice, Prosecco, Aperol, splash of soda, orange slice), 1 Margarita (shaken, strained, optional half salt rim; leave it off here). Put these steps in a good order and explain why: ice the spritz glass, stir the Manhattan, shake the Margarita, top the spritz with soda, pour the Manhattan ingredients, pour the Margarita ingredients, pour Aperol and Prosecco into the spritz glass, garnish, serve.

## 3. Rounds

A table orders 4 Cuba Libres (highball, ice, white rum 50, lime 10, top cola; lime wedge). Time drink by drink and in passes. How much is saved?

## 4. Dead drink

You make a Mojito (muddle, then build) and a Daiquiri for one ticket. You finish the Mojito first, then start the Daiquiri from the beginning: glass, three pours, shake, strain. Does the Mojito die? What would you change?

## 5. Three tickets

Waiting, in order of arrival: T1 = 2 Negronis (old fashioned glass, ice, gin, Campari, vermouth, stir in glass, orange slice); T2 = 2 draught beers; T3 = 3 Espresso Martinis. Which order do you make them in, and what is each ticket's time if you start all three now?
