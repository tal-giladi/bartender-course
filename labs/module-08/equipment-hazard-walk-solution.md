# Equipment hazard walk — solution (lesson 08.6)

| # | Hazard | Why | What to do now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High-temperature glasswasher rinsing at 61 °C | Below the 82 °C (most machines) sanitising rinse in the U.S. Food Code: glasses may not be sanitised | Stop using it, tell the shift manager; rewash glasses once fixed or follow the venue's backup procedure |
| 2 | Steaming rack pushed next to the ice well | Hot glasses can crack in ice and warm drinks; steam and heat next to ice | Move the rack away; let glasses cool before use |
| 3 | Pint glass on the ice machine, used as a scoop | Glass can chip into the ice; hands touch the ice | Remove the glass; use the clean scoop from its holder |
| 4 | Ice bin door open | Dust, splashes and hands get into food ice | Close it |
| 5 | Wine bottles chilling in the ice bin | Bottles carry dirt into ice that goes into drinks; they can break | Remove them; chill in a fridge or a separate ice bucket; consider burning the bin ice if bottles were dirty or chipped |
| 6 | Sanitiser bucket next to an open descaler | Acid descaler mixed with a chlorine-based sanitiser can release toxic gas | Close and store the descaler; keep chemicals apart, labelled, away from food and ice |
| 7 | Soda gun with dried syrup and green growth | Contamination of every soft drink and mixer | Take it out of use; clean and sanitise nozzles and holster before service; report |
| 8 | Cloudy lager, line cleaned five weeks ago | Lines should be cleaned about every two weeks (Brewers Association); cloudy, off beer | Tell the manager; do not serve it until checked |
| 9 | Full keg at the top of the stairs to be carried | A full keg weighs about 61–73 kg; carrying it down stairs risks serious injury and falls | Do not carry it; use the venue's lift or dolly, or leave it for the delivery/cellar procedure; keep the stairs clear |
| 10 | CO2 cylinders unchained, one without a cap | A falling cylinder can break its valve and release gas fast | Report; trained staff chain them upright and replace the cap |
| 11 | Gas alarm panel shows "FAULT" | No warning if CO2 leaks into the cellar | Nobody enters the cellar until the alarm is fixed or the manager applies the venue's safe-entry procedure |
| 12 | Untrained bartender going alone to change the gas, with a faulty alarm | Gas change is a trained task; a leak in a cellar can make her collapse | Stop her; tell the manager; a trained person does it once the alarm works |

Also acceptable as extra findings: the blender cable across the wet floor and the rolled-up mat (trip, slip and electrical hazard: reroute the cable, dry the floor, put the mat back). If you listed these instead of one of the twelve, count them.

## Example daily-check cards

Glasswasher: 1. Display shows wash and final rinse in range (high-temperature: rinse at least 82 °C) or sanitiser level OK. 2. Detergent and rinse aid connected and labelled. 3. Filters clean and in place. 4. Out of range: stop, tell the shift manager, use the backup procedure. 5. At close: drain, clean filters, leave the door open to dry.

Ice machine: 1. Bin full or filling, door closed. 2. Ice clear, no smell, no specks. 3. Scoop clean, in its holder outside the ice. 4. Nothing stored in the bin. 5. Slime, smell or specks: stop using it, tell the manager; machine sanitised on the maker's schedule.

Soda gun: see the example card in lesson 08.6.
