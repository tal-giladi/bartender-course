# 05.4 · Daiquiri and Margarita

The Daiquiri and the Margarita are the two purest sours: a spirit, fresh lime juice and something sweet, shaken hard and served cold without ice. Learn these two well and you have learned the pattern behind dozens of other drinks, from the Whiskey Sour in the next lesson to the White Lady and the Sidecar in Module 6. Both are also drinks that many guests have only had as frozen, sweet versions, so a bartender who makes a sharp, balanced classic gets noticed. This lesson gives both IBA specs, the common variations and their sources, how to swap sugar for syrup, how to make a half salt rim, and a shaking practice with lime, syrup and water.

## Why it matters

A guest orders "a Daiquiri" and you reach for the blender, because that is what they serve at the beach. They wanted the classic: rum, lime, sugar, shaken, in a cold coupe. Or you make a Margarita with 30 ml of lime instead of 15 ml, and it comes back "too sour". Sours are where your Module 3 work on balance pays off: one wrong pour of lime or sugar is something every guest can taste.

## Core ideas

### The sour template

A **sour** is base spirit + citrus juice + sweetener, shaken with ice ([lesson 05.1](lesson-01.md)). The sweetener can be sugar, syrup, or a **liqueur** (a sweetened, flavoured spirit) such as **triple sec**, an orange liqueur ([lesson 02.6](../module-02/lesson-06.md)). You balanced sweet and sour with water drills in [lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md); the same rule applies here: the lime must be fresh and measured exactly.

### Why shake

Citrus drinks are shaken, not stirred: shaking chills fast, dilutes, and whips in tiny air bubbles that give a lively texture ([lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)). Shake hard for about 10–15 seconds with plenty of ice. Then strain into a chilled glass. Many bars **double strain** (through a Hawthorne and a fine strainer, [lesson 04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md)) to keep ice chips out of drinks served without ice; the IBA texts say only "strain".

### Sugar or syrup in the Daiquiri

The IBA Daiquiri uses 2 bar spoons of superfine (caster) sugar, stirred with the lime and rum in the shaker until dissolved, before the ice goes in. Most bars use syrup instead because it is faster and consistent. In this course a bar spoon is 5 ml, so 2 bar spoons are about 10 ml of sugar, roughly 8 g (professional judgment; sugar settles differently in different spoons). About 15 ml of 1:1 syrup or 10 ml of 2:1 syrup gives a similar sweetness ([lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)); taste and follow the house spec.

### The Margarita and its salt

The IBA Margarita is tequila, triple sec and lime, with an optional **half salt rim**: salt on half of the outside of the rim only, so the guest can sip with or without salt. Salt on the outside only matters: salt that falls into the drink makes it taste salty all the way through. Use **100% agave** tequila (made only from agave sugars, [lesson 02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)), as the IBA spec says.

| | Daiquiri | Margarita |
|---|---|---|
| Base | white rum 60 ml | 100% agave tequila 50 ml |
| Sour | fresh lime 20 ml | fresh lime 15 ml |
| Sweet | 2 bar spoons superfine sugar | triple sec 20 ml |
| Method | dissolve sugar, shake with ice, strain | shake with ice, strain |
| Glass | chilled cocktail glass, no ice | chilled cocktail glass, no ice |
| Garnish | none in the IBA spec | half salt rim (optional) |

> [!NOTE]
> Specs checked on iba-world.com and diffordsguide.com on 2026-10-17. Difford's Guide revises its own recipes from time to time; the linked page is the current version.

> [!WARNING]
> Seal the shaker fully before shaking and hold both parts: a shaker that opens mid-shake throws ice and liquid, and broken glass if you use a glass half. Shake away from people. Shaken sours taste easy but contain a full measure of spirit; if you taste a real one, make a half size, sip and spit, never before driving.

## Step by step

### Daiquiri

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| White rum | 60 | 2 |
| Fresh lime juice | 20 | ⅔ |
| Superfine (caster) sugar | 2 bar spoons | — |

- **Method:** put rum, lime and sugar in the shaker and stir until the sugar dissolves; add ice, shake hard 10–15 seconds, strain (or double strain) into the chilled glass.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass (coupe), no ice.
- **Garnish:** none in the IBA spec; a lime wheel or twist is common (professional judgment).
- **Profile and key technique:** sharp, fresh, dry-leaning, with the rum clearly there. The key is exact lime and sugar: 5 ml either way changes the drink.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Daiquiri](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/daiquiri/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Daiquiri](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/611/daiquiri-diffords-recipe) is 50 ml light white rum, 15 ml lime, 10 ml 2:1 syrup, optional bitters, plus 7.5 ml chilled water if the ice is dry; shaken, fine-strained into a chilled coupe, lime zest expressed and discarded, lime wedge on the rim. Frozen (blended) Daiquiris are a different serve, covered with blending in [lesson 04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md).
- **Substitutions:** 15 ml 1:1 syrup or 10 ml 2:1 syrup for the sugar. Any light, dry white rum. Never bottled lime cordial for fresh lime (that is a different drink, the Gimlet family).
- **Home-practice version:** 60 ml cold water + 20 ml fresh lime + 15 ml 1:1 syrup, shaken and double strained. This is a real, drinkable lime sour, so you can taste the balance.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** an alcohol-free rum alternative (or cold water with a few drops of vanilla) with 20 ml lime and 15 ml 1:1 syrup, shaken. Close in balance and texture, lighter in body.
- **Common mistakes:** undissolved sugar (gritty); blending when the guest wanted the classic; warm glass; old lime juice.

### Margarita

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Tequila (100% agave) | 50 | 1⅔ |
| Triple sec | 20 | ⅔ |
| Fresh lime juice | 15 | ½ |

- **Method:** shake hard with ice 10–15 seconds, strain into the chilled glass (prepare the rim first).
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass, no ice.
- **Garnish:** half salt rim (optional).
- **Profile and key technique:** bright, tangy, orange-sweet, with the green, peppery taste of agave. The key is the rim: salt on the outside of half the rim, nothing inside the glass.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Margarita](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/margarita/). Variations: [Difford's Guide Original Margarita](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/7884/the-original-margarita) is 50 ml blanco tequila, 30 ml triple sec, 20 ml lime, shaken and strained over ice in a salt-rimmed old fashioned glass with a lime wheel; the same page cites a 1937 "Picador" of 60 ml tequila, 30 ml Cointreau and 30 ml lime. On the rocks (over ice) is a very common serve.
- **Substitutions:** Cointreau or another triple sec; a non-salt rim on request. Mixto tequila (not 100% agave) is not the IBA spec; say so if it is all you have.
- **Home-practice version:** 50 ml cold water + 20 ml orange juice with 5 ml 1:1 syrup (the "triple sec") + 15 ml lime, shaken and strained into a half salt rimmed glass.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** 15 ml lime + 30 ml fresh orange juice + 10 ml agave or 1:1 syrup + 30 ml cold water and a tiny pinch of salt, shaken, half salt rim. Close in taste; no agave spirit character.
- **Common mistakes:** salt all round the rim and inside the glass; too much lime (the IBA spec has less lime than triple sec); over-dilution from a long shake with wet ice.

### Half salt rim

1. Pour a thin layer of salt (flaky or coarse) on a small plate.
2. Hold the chilled glass by the stem. Run a cut lime wedge along the **outside** of half the rim only.
3. Tilt the glass and roll the wet outside edge in the salt; do not dip it straight down.
4. Tap the glass gently to drop loose salt. Wipe any salt from the inside edge with a clean napkin.
5. Rim the glass before you shake, so the drink goes in the second it is strained.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A shaker (Boston, cobbler or a sealed jar), a jigger, a Hawthorne and a fine (tea) strainer, two stemmed glasses, ice.
- 4–6 limes, an orange, 1:1 syrup, superfine sugar, salt, water, a citrus press, a knife and board.
- A kitchen scale, a phone timer, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: a sealed jar with a tea strainer instead of shaker and Hawthorne.

> [!CAUTION]
> Cut limes on a board with the knife moving away from your fingers. Hold the glass by the stem while rimming; a cold, wet stemmed glass slips.

### Time

55 minutes: 10 to juice and set up, 20 for six Daiquiri-style shakes, 15 for three rimmed Margarita-style drinks, 10 to taste and record.

### Steps

1. Recite both specs. Check your cards.
2. Juice the limes and strain the juice ([lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)).
3. **Sugar Daiquiri (×2).** In the shaker: 60 ml water, 20 ml lime, 2 bar spoons sugar. Stir until no grains are visible; time it. Add ice, shake 12 seconds, double strain into a chilled glass. Check for ice chips.
4. **Syrup Daiquiri (×2).** Same with 15 ml 1:1 syrup instead of sugar. Taste a sugar version and a syrup version side by side: is the sweetness similar?
5. **Weighing rep (×2).** Weigh the shaker with the liquid before adding ice, and the strained drink after. Record the gain.
6. **Margarita-style (×3).** Rim half the glass with salt. Shake 50 ml water, 20 ml orange juice + 5 ml syrup, 15 ml lime. Strain. Time the rim and the whole drink.
7. Taste one Margarita-style drink from the salted side and from the clean side.
8. Run the simulation and pass both cards.

### What you should see

The sugar takes 20–40 seconds to dissolve in the shaker; the syrup version skips that step. Sugar and syrup Daiquiris taste very close if you used 15 ml of 1:1. A 12-second shake with fresh ice adds roughly 20–30% of the starting weight in water to these water-based drills (about 20–30 g); real spirit-based sours gain more because they chill below 0 °C (see the simulation and [lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md)). Double straining leaves no chips. A clean half rim takes about 20–30 seconds; the clean side tastes of lime and orange only, the salted side rounder and less sharp.

### What this practice cannot teach

Lime and syrup show the balance, but not how a real rum or tequila changes it, or how your shake feels when ten sours are waiting; that comes with small tastings and real service.

## Common mistakes

- **Too much lime.** Sour and sharp. Fix: jigger the lime every time; the Margarita has only 15 ml.
- **Undissolved sugar.** Gritty texture. Fix: stir until clear before adding ice, or use syrup.
- **A weak shake.** The drink is warm and flat. Fix: full ice, hard shake, 10–15 seconds.
- **Ice chips in an up drink.** They melt and water it down. Fix: double strain.
- **Salt in the drink.** Fix: rim the outside only, tap off loose salt, wipe the inside edge.
- **Confusing the classic with the frozen version.** Fix: ask "classic, shaken and up, or frozen?" if the guest is unsure.

## Key takeaways

- IBA Daiquiri: 60 ml white rum, 20 ml lime, 2 bar spoons superfine sugar, dissolved, shaken, strained into a chilled cocktail glass.
- IBA Margarita: 50 ml 100% agave tequila, 20 ml triple sec, 15 ml lime, shaken, strained, optional half salt rim.
- 2 bar spoons of sugar is close to 15 ml 1:1 or 10 ml 2:1 syrup.
- Shake hard 10–15 seconds and double strain drinks served without ice.
- Salt goes on the outside of half the rim, never inside.
