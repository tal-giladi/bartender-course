# 05.7 · Aperol Spritz and Espresso Martini

The last two drinks of this module are the two newest: the Aperol Spritz, an orange, bittersweet sparkling drink built in a wine glass, and the Espresso Martini, vodka and coffee shaken until a creamy foam forms on top. The IBA lists both as "New Era" drinks. They are very different, one long, light and fizzy, one short, strong and cold, but both are ordered constantly, both look simple, and both have one detail that decides whether they come out right: the order of pouring for the spritz, and the fresh, hot espresso for the Espresso Martini. This lesson gives both IBA specs on full recipe cards, the brand's own ratio for the spritz, the Espresso Martini's origin and variations, and a practice that needs no alcohol.

## Why it matters

A sunny afternoon, a table of eight orders Aperol Spritzes. If you pour the Aperol first and then the Prosecco and soda on top, the colour sits in a layer at the bottom and you have to stir hard, losing the bubbles. Later the same evening someone orders an Espresso Martini; you use coffee that has been sitting in a jug since lunch, and the drink arrives flat, with no foam, looking like cold coffee. Both mistakes cost you nothing in ingredients and a lot in the guest's opinion.

## Core ideas

### The Spritz

A **spritz** is a bitter aperitif lengthened with sparkling wine and soda over ice. The IBA lists it simply as "Spritz", with Aperol (a bright orange, gently bitter Italian aperitif, [lesson 02.7](../module-02/lesson-07.md)) and notes that other versions use Campari, Cynar or Select. Guests usually order it by brand: "an Aperol Spritz".

**Prosecco** is an Italian sparkling wine (more in Module 7). Keep it cold and closed; an opened bottle loses its fizz within hours unless it is sealed with a stopper (professional judgment; see [lesson 03.6](../module-03/lesson-06.md) for why cold liquids keep CO2 better).

### 3-2-1 and the brand's own version

The IBA gives 90 ml Prosecco, 60 ml Aperol and a splash of soda. Aperol's own recipe pages give the same as **3-2-1**: 90 ml Prosecco, 60 ml Aperol, 30 ml soda, poured in that order over ice in a wine glass, with an orange slice. Aperol's Ireland page also shows a smaller "official recipe" of 75, 50 and 25 ml in the same ratio. Bars pick one size and stick to it.

### Pour order and fizz

Prosecco first, then Aperol, then soda. The heavier, sweeter Aperol poured into the wine sinks and mixes on its way down, so one short, gentle stir is enough. If Aperol goes in first, it sits at the bottom under the light wine and needs more stirring, which knocks out bubbles ([lesson 03.6](../module-03/lesson-06.md)).

### The Espresso Martini

Despite the name, it is not a Martini: it has no gin or vermouth and is shaken, not stirred. It belongs to the **coffee** template ([lesson 05.1](lesson-01.md)): vodka, coffee liqueur, a little sugar and espresso. Difford's Guide records that bartender Dick Bradsell created it in London around 1983 as a "Vodka Espresso" on the rocks; it was later served straight up and became the Espresso Martini.

### Why fresh espresso foams

**Espresso** (אספרסו, *espreso*) is strong coffee made by forcing hot water through finely ground coffee under pressure. Fresh espresso has **crema**, a layer of fine foam made of oils and gas. Shaken hard with ice right away, those oils and the dissolved gas build a thick foam on the drink. Old, cold coffee has lost them, so the drink stays flat. Difford's Guide is blunt about it: let the espresso cool and the crema dies, and so does the drink.

> [!WARNING]
> Caffeine does not reduce the effects of alcohol on the body; it may only make a person feel less drunk, which can lead to drinking more (U.S. CDC). Never present an Espresso Martini as a way to "sober up" or "keep going", and watch for guests ordering several in a row.

## Step by step

### Aperol Spritz

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Prosecco | 90 | 3 |
| Aperol | 60 | 2 |
| Soda water | splash (Aperol: 30) | 1 |

- **Method:** fill the wine glass with ice; pour the Prosecco, then the Aperol, then a splash of soda; stir gently once.
- **Glass and ice:** large wine glass, filled with ice cubes.
- **Garnish:** orange slice.
- **Profile and key technique:** bittersweet orange, light and fizzy, low in alcohol compared with the other drinks in this module. The key is the order and a gentle stir, so the drink stays bright and sparkling.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Spritz](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/spritz/) (notes versions with Campari, Cynar or Select). Variation: [Aperol's own recipe](https://www.aperol.com/en-ie/aperol-spritz-recipe/) gives 3-2-1 (90 ml Prosecco, 60 ml Aperol, 30 ml soda) and a smaller 75/50/25 ml version. A Campari Spritz uses Campari for Aperol and is more bitter.
- **Substitutions:** another dry sparkling wine (Cava, an Israeli dry sparkling) for Prosecco; another bitter aperitif for Aperol, with a change of name.
- **Home-practice version:** 90 ml cold sparkling water (the "Prosecco") + 60 ml orange juice with 5 ml grapefruit juice (the "Aperol") + 30 ml soda, built in order on ice in a wine glass.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** an alcohol-free bitter orange aperitif with alcohol-free sparkling wine and soda, built the same way; or 60 ml blood orange or orange juice + 10 ml grapefruit + 120 ml soda with an orange slice. Close in look and freshness.
- **Common mistakes:** Aperol first; warm or flat Prosecco; a half-full glass of ice; hard stirring; a huge pour of soda that drowns it.

### Espresso Martini

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Vodka | 50 | 1⅔ |
| Coffee liqueur (Kahlúa) | 30 | 1 |
| Sugar syrup (1:1) | 10 | ⅓ |
| Strong espresso, fresh | 1 shot (about 30) | 1 |

- **Method:** make the espresso right before the drink; pour all ingredients into the shaker, add ice at once, shake hard 10–15 seconds, strain (double strain for a smoother foam) into the chilled glass.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass (coupe or martini glass), no ice.
- **Garnish:** 3 coffee beans on the foam.
- **Profile and key technique:** rich, roasted, bittersweet coffee, cold, with a creamy foam. The key is fresh espresso shaken immediately and hard.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Espresso Martini](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/espresso-martini/) (names Kahlúa; espresso volume not given, "about 30 ml" is this course's single shot, professional judgment). Variations: [Difford's Guide Espresso Martini](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/725/espresso-martini) is 45 ml vodka, 30 ml hot espresso, 20 ml coffee liqueur, 2 drops saline, no added sugar, lemon zest expressed and discarded, coffee beans; [Difford's simple version](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/4106/espresso-martini-simple-3-equal-parts-recipe) is 30 ml each of vodka, coffee liqueur and espresso.
- **Substitutions:** another coffee liqueur (taste: some are sweeter, so adjust the syrup); cold brew concentrate if there is no espresso machine (less foam; tell the guest).
- **Home-practice version:** 50 ml cold water + 30 ml strong sweetened coffee (the "liqueur") + 10 ml 1:1 syrup + a fresh shot of espresso or very strong moka-pot coffee, shaken hard on ice and strained.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** fresh espresso + 20 ml 1:1 or coffee syrup + 40 ml cold water or alcohol-free coffee "liqueur" alternative, shaken hard on ice, coffee beans. Close in taste and foam; contains caffeine, so tell the guest.
- **Common mistakes:** old or cold coffee (no foam); too short a shake; not chilling the glass; a weak, watery coffee; forgetting that the drink contains caffeine.

### A round of spritzes

1. Line up the wine glasses and fill each with ice.
2. Prosecco into each (90 ml), then Aperol into each (60 ml), then a splash of soda.
3. One gentle stir each, orange slice, serve.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A wine glass, a coupe or martini glass, a shaker (or a sealed jar), a Hawthorne and a fine (tea) strainer, a jigger, a bar spoon, ice.
- Cold sparkling water and soda, orange juice, a grapefruit or bottled grapefruit juice, an orange.
- An espresso machine, a moka pot, or strong instant coffee made with very little water; sugar or 1:1 syrup; a few coffee beans.
- A ruler, a kitchen scale, a phone timer, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: a jar shaker and strong instant coffee whisked hard (less foam, but it shows the idea).

> [!CAUTION]
> Fresh espresso is hot. Pour it into the shaker first and add plenty of ice before you close it, so the liquid cools at once; hold the shaker tightly and shake away from people. Coffee and caffeine: keep tasting small, and avoid it late in the evening if it keeps you awake.

### Time

50 minutes: 10 to set up and make coffee, 15 for three spritzes and the order test, 20 for four coffee shakes, 5 to record.

### Steps

1. Recite both specs. Check your cards.
2. **Order test.** Build one home-practice spritz the right way (sparkling water, then orange mix, then soda) and one the wrong way (orange mix first). Stir each once, gently. Look at the colour: even or layered? Taste the last sip of each for fizz.
3. **Spritz (×2).** Two more correct builds, timed.
4. **Coffee shake, fresh (×2).** Make a fresh shot. Within 30 seconds, shake the home-practice version hard for 12 seconds and double strain into a chilled coupe. After 1 minute, measure the foam height with a ruler against the glass.
5. **Coffee shake, old (×1).** Use coffee made 30 minutes earlier and left to cool. Shake and strain the same way. Measure the foam.
6. **Short shake (×1).** Fresh coffee, but shake only 4 seconds. Measure.
7. Place 3 coffee beans on the best foam: they should sit on top without sinking.
8. Run the simulation and pass both cards.

### What you should see

The correctly built spritz is evenly coloured after one stir; the wrong-order one keeps a darker layer at the bottom and needs more stirring, so it tastes flatter at the end. A spritz takes about 25–35 seconds. Fresh espresso shaken hard gives a foam of roughly 5–10 mm that holds the coffee beans; old coffee gives little or none; a 4-second shake gives a thin, bubbly foam that fades quickly. Instant coffee foams much less than espresso.

### What this practice cannot teach

The home versions show order, fizz and foam, but not how Aperol's bitterness or the vodka and coffee liqueur balance in the real drink, or how to keep up with espresso orders at a busy machine; small tastings and service teach that.

## Common mistakes

- **Aperol poured first.** Layered colour, hard stirring, lost fizz. Fix: Prosecco, then Aperol, then soda.
- **Flat Prosecco.** Fix: keep it cold, seal opened bottles, open a new one when it tastes flat.
- **No foam on the Espresso Martini.** Old coffee or a weak shake. Fix: fresh espresso, ice straight in, hard 10–15 second shake.
- **Warm Espresso Martini.** Hot coffee plus too little ice. Fix: fill the shaker with ice and chill the glass.
- **"It will wake you up."** Caffeine does not cancel alcohol. Fix: never sell it that way; follow responsible service rules (Module 9).

## Key takeaways

- IBA Spritz: 90 ml Prosecco, 60 ml Aperol, splash of soda, built on ice in a wine glass, stirred gently, orange slice.
- Aperol's own 3-2-1 is 90/60/30 ml, poured Prosecco, Aperol, soda.
- IBA Espresso Martini: 50 ml vodka, 30 ml coffee liqueur, 10 ml sugar syrup, 1 strong espresso, shaken hard, chilled cocktail glass, 3 coffee beans.
- Fresh, hot espresso shaken at once makes the foam; old coffee does not.
- Caffeine does not reduce alcohol's effects.
