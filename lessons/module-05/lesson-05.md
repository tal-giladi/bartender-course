# 05.5 · Whiskey Sour and Cosmopolitan

Two more sours, one old and one modern. The Whiskey Sour is bourbon, lemon and sugar, sometimes with egg white for a soft foam on top. The Cosmopolitan is citrus vodka, orange liqueur, lime and cranberry, pink, tart and served up. Both use the sour template from the last lesson, which means you already know most of the movement; what changes is the balance, the optional egg white and the garnish. This lesson gives both IBA specs on full recipe cards, the best-known variations and their sources, how to use egg white or aquafaba safely, and a shaking practice that builds foam without alcohol.

## Why it matters

A guest orders a Whiskey Sour. Your bar's spec has egg white. If you do not mention it, and the guest is allergic to egg or vegan, you have a serious problem, not a bad drink. If you mention it and offer aquafaba, you have a happy guest. The Cosmopolitan tests something else: four ingredients, two of them small, in a drink that shows every mistake in its colour and taste. Get the cranberry wrong and it is either pale and sharp or pink juice.

## Core ideas

### Two more sours

Both drinks follow the sour template ([lesson 05.1](lesson-01.md)): base + citrus + sweet, shaken. The Whiskey Sour uses lemon and sugar syrup; the Cosmopolitan uses lime, an orange liqueur and cranberry juice, which adds both sweetness and colour. Lemon is a little less sharp than lime, so the Whiskey Sour carries more of it (25 ml) than the Margarita carries lime (15 ml).

### Egg white and foam

**Egg white** (חלבון, *chelbon*) has proteins that trap air when shaken, giving a thick, silky foam (קצף, *ketsef*) on top of the drink and a smoother texture. The IBA spec says "few drops of egg white (optional)"; many bars use about 15–20 ml, the white of a small egg (Difford's Guide uses 15 ml). **Aquafaba** (the liquid from a can of chickpeas) foams in a similar way and is egg-free.

Foam needs extra air, so egg drinks get an extra shake ([lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)):

- **Dry shake**: shake without ice first to build foam, then add ice and shake again to chill.
- **Reverse dry shake**: shake with ice first, strain the liquid back into the empty shaker, then shake again without ice. Many bartenders find this gives a thicker foam (Difford's Guide uses it in its Whiskey Sour).

> [!WARNING]
> Raw egg can carry *Salmonella* bacteria, even in clean, uncracked shells. The U.S. FDA advises using pasteurised eggs or pasteurised egg products when eggs will not be cooked; children, older adults, pregnant women and people with weak immune systems are most at risk. Use pasteurised egg white or aquafaba in all practice. Egg is a major allergen: always tell the guest a drink contains egg before you make it (Module 9 covers allergens in Israel).

### Sugar syrup in the IBA spec

The IBA Whiskey Sour says "sugar syrup" without a ratio. This course reads it as 1:1. If your bar uses 2:1, pour about two thirds as much ([lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)).

### Cranberry, colour and the Cosmopolitan

**Cranberry juice** sold in shops is usually a sweetened "cranberry drink", not pure juice; it adds sweetness, a little tartness and the pink colour. The IBA spec uses **citrus vodka** (vodka flavoured with lemon) and **Cointreau** (a brand of triple sec). The Cosmopolitan should be pale pink, not red: too much cranberry turns it into juice.

| | Whiskey Sour | Cosmopolitan |
|---|---|---|
| Base | bourbon 45 ml | citrus vodka 40 ml |
| Sour | fresh lemon 25 ml | fresh lime 15 ml |
| Sweet | sugar syrup 20 ml | Cointreau 15 ml + cranberry 30 ml |
| Extra | few drops egg white (optional) | — |
| Glass | cobbler glass, or old fashioned glass on ice | large cocktail glass, no ice |
| Garnish | half orange slice + maraschino cherry | lemon twist |

## Step by step

### Whiskey Sour

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Bourbon whiskey | 45 | 1½ |
| Fresh lemon juice | 25 | ¾ |
| Sugar syrup (1:1) | 20 | ⅔ |
| Egg white, pasteurised (optional) | few drops (bars often use 15–20) | ½–⅔ |

- **Method:** shake hard with ice and strain. With egg white, shake harder or add a dry shake (or reverse dry shake) to build the foam, then fine strain.
- **Glass and ice:** cobbler glass (a small goblet; many bars use a coupe) with no ice, or an old fashioned glass filled with ice for "on the rocks".
- **Garnish:** half orange slice and a maraschino cherry; orange zest optional.
- **Profile and key technique:** sweet-sour and round, with vanilla and oak from the bourbon; with egg, a soft, creamy foam. The key is the foam: a proper dry shake and a fine strain.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Whiskey Sour](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/whiskey-sour/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Whiskey Sour](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/2083/whiskey-sour-diffords-recipe) is 60 ml bourbon, 30 ml lemon, 20 ml 2:1 syrup, 3 dashes Angostura, optional 2 drops saline, 15 ml pasteurised egg white (or an egg-free foamer); shaken with ice, strained back, dry shaken, fine-strained onto ice in an old fashioned glass, lemon wheel and cherry.
- **Substitutions:** aquafaba (15–20 ml) or a commercial cocktail foamer for egg white; rye or a blended Scotch for bourbon (Difford's lists a Scotch Sour). 2:1 syrup at about 13–15 ml.
- **Home-practice version:** 45 ml cold strong black tea + 25 ml lemon + 20 ml 1:1 syrup + 15 ml aquafaba, reverse dry shaken and fine strained. Drinkable, and it shows you the foam.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** the home-practice version with smoky or strong black tea, or an alcohol-free whiskey alternative, plus lemon, syrup and aquafaba. Close in balance and texture.
- **Common mistakes:** not telling the guest about egg; raw unpasteurised egg; weak dry shake (thin, quick-disappearing foam); egg shell in the drink; too much syrup.

### Cosmopolitan

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Citrus vodka | 40 | 1⅓ |
| Cointreau | 15 | ½ |
| Fresh lime juice | 15 | ½ |
| Cranberry juice | 30 | 1 |

- **Method:** shake hard with ice, strain (double strain if the bar does) into the chilled glass.
- **Glass and ice:** large cocktail glass, chilled, no ice.
- **Garnish:** lemon twist.
- **Profile and key technique:** tart, fruity and clean, citrus nose, pale pink. The key is the small pours: 15 ml lime and 15 ml Cointreau decide whether it tastes balanced.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Cosmopolitan](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/cosmopolitan/). Variation: [Difford's Guide, Dale DeGroff's Cosmopolitan](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/473/cosmopolitan-cocktail-dale-degroffs-recipe) is 45 ml citrus vodka, 22.5 ml triple sec, 30 ml sweetened cranberry, 7.5 ml lime, shaken and fine-strained into a martini glass, with a flamed orange zest. This course does not teach flaming garnishes: express the orange zest instead.
- **Substitutions:** plain vodka plus an extra lemon twist if there is no citrus vodka; another triple sec for Cointreau.
- **Home-practice version:** 40 ml cold water + 15 ml orange juice (the "Cointreau") + 15 ml lime + 30 ml cranberry drink, shaken and strained.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** 45 ml cranberry drink + 15 ml lime + 20 ml orange juice + 5 ml 1:1 syrup + 20 ml cold water, shaken, lemon twist. Close in taste and colour.
- **Common mistakes:** too much cranberry (red, juicy); bottled lime; plain vodka without telling the guest; a flamed garnish (unsafe near spirits, not taught in this course).

### Reverse dry shake, step by step

1. Ask: "This drink has egg white, is that OK?" Offer aquafaba if not.
2. Build the drink in the small tin: spirit, lemon, syrup, then the egg white or aquafaba.
3. Add ice, seal, shake hard 10 seconds.
4. Strain the liquid into the other (empty) tin. Discard the ice.
5. Seal again and shake hard without ice for 10 seconds. Hold the tins tightly: without ice, the seal is weaker and pressure builds.
6. Fine strain into the glass (with or without ice, as the spec says). Let the foam settle a few seconds, then garnish.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A shaker (two tins, or a sealed jar), a Hawthorne and a fine (tea) strainer, a jigger, a coupe and an old fashioned glass, ice.
- Lemons, limes, 1:1 syrup, cold strong black tea, cranberry drink, orange juice.
- A can of chickpeas (for aquafaba), or pasteurised liquid egg white from a supermarket chiller.
- A peeler, a phone timer, a ruler, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: a jar with a tight lid; aquafaba is the cheapest foamer.

> [!WARNING]
> Use pasteurised egg white or aquafaba only. Do not use raw shell eggs. Wash your hands and the shaker after handling egg. A shaker without ice can pop open: hold both parts firmly and shake away from people.

### Time

55 minutes: 10 to set up and juice, 25 for six foamed sours, 15 for three Cosmopolitan-style drinks, 5 to record.

### Steps

1. Recite both specs. Check your cards.
2. **Foam test (×2 each).** Make the home-practice Whiskey Sour four times: twice with a dry shake (no ice first), twice with a reverse dry shake (ice first). Strain into a coupe.
3. After 1 minute, measure the foam height with the ruler held against the glass. Check again at 5 minutes.
4. **Plain sour (×2).** The same drink without aquafaba, shaken once with ice. Compare texture and look.
5. **Cosmopolitan-style (×3).** Shake the home-practice version and strain into a chilled glass. Pour one finished drink into a measuring jug: is it close to the expected 100 ml plus the water the shake added?
6. Express a lemon twist over each Cosmopolitan.
7. Practise the guest line out loud: "Our Whiskey Sour has egg white. Is that OK, or would you like it without, or with a vegan foam?"
8. Run the simulation and pass both cards.

### What you should see

Aquafaba foam should reach roughly 1–2 cm in a coupe and hold for several minutes; the reverse dry shake usually gives a slightly thicker, finer foam than the dry shake, though your kit may differ. The plain sour has no foam and a thinner texture. The Cosmopolitan-style drink is pale pink; if yours is deep red, you poured too much cranberry. The strained volume is the 100 ml of ingredients plus 20–30 ml of meltwater.

### What this practice cannot teach

Aquafaba and tea show the foam and the balance, but not how bourbon and Cointreau taste in the drink, or how to manage a guest who is unsure about allergens while the bar is busy; Module 9 and real shifts cover that.

## Common mistakes

- **Not mentioning egg.** A guest with an egg allergy can get seriously ill. Fix: say it every time before making the drink; offer aquafaba.
- **Thin foam.** Spot it: a few big bubbles that vanish in a minute. Fix: dry or reverse dry shake, hard, 10 seconds; fine strain.
- **Raw shell eggs.** Fix: pasteurised egg white or aquafaba only.
- **Red Cosmopolitan.** Too much cranberry. Fix: measure 30 ml exactly.
- **Wrong syrup strength.** 20 ml of 2:1 instead of 1:1 makes the Whiskey Sour much sweeter. Fix: know which syrup your bar uses.
- **Flaming the zest.** Fire near spirits and guests. Fix: express the zest; no flames.

## Key takeaways

- IBA Whiskey Sour: 45 ml bourbon, 25 ml lemon, 20 ml sugar syrup, few drops egg white optional; shaken; cobbler glass or on the rocks; half orange slice and cherry.
- IBA Cosmopolitan: 40 ml citrus vodka, 15 ml Cointreau, 15 ml lime, 30 ml cranberry; shaken; large cocktail glass; lemon twist.
- Egg white or aquafaba needs a dry or reverse dry shake for good foam.
- Use pasteurised egg white or aquafaba, and always tell the guest a drink contains egg.
- Difford's Whiskey Sour (60/30/20 of 2:1 with bitters) and DeGroff's Cosmopolitan are the main variations.
