# 05.8 · Recipe recall and a twelve-drink practice session

This is the module's practical piece. First you write all twelve specs from memory on a blank grid and score yourself to the same standard as the final recipe exam. Then you make all twelve drinks, one after another, as home-practice or alcohol-free versions, with your station set up and a timer running, and you log what happened. The two parts test different things: the grid tests what is in your head; the session tests whether your hands can turn it into drinks in a sensible order, without hunting for tools. At the end you choose your three weakest drinks and plan how to fix them.

## Why it matters

On a trial shift nobody asks you to recite a spec; they hand you a ticket and watch. You need both the memory and the movement, and you need them for any drink at any moment, not for the one you studied last. A session that runs through all twelve shows you where the gaps are: the drink you always forget the garnish for, the method you hesitate over, the moment you realise the mint is still in the fridge. Finding these at home costs nothing. Finding them on a shift costs the job.

## Core ideas

### The recall standard

The final assessment's recipe recall exam (lesson 11.2) needs 80% and marks each drink on ml (exact to ±5 ml), method, glass and garnish. This lesson uses the same standard, so you know where you stand now:

| Part (per drink) | Point if… |
|---|---|
| Ingredients and ml | every ingredient named and every amount within ±5 ml (dashes, cubes, sprigs as in the spec) |
| Method | the right technique (build, stir, shake, muddle) and the key extra step (dissolve the sugar, dry shake, top with soda) |
| Glass and ice | the right glass and the right ice in it (or none) |
| Garnish | the spec garnish |

12 drinks × 4 points = 48. Pass mark: 39 points (80%).

### Planning a session like a shift

Order the drinks so that equipment is used in blocks and nothing waits: this is the same thinking as sequencing tickets in Module 8.

1. **Prep first** (mise en place): juice, syrup, mint in water, garnishes cut, glasses chilling, ice ready.
2. **Built and stirred drinks** (no shaker needed): Negroni, Old Fashioned, Manhattan, Martini.
3. **Shaken sours**: Daiquiri, Margarita, Cosmopolitan, then Whiskey Sour (the aquafaba foam is last so the tins are rinsed once).
4. **Espresso Martini**: when the coffee is fresh.
5. **Long drinks**: Mojito, Moscow Mule, Aperol Spritz, finishing with the fizz.

### Logging and reviewing

Log each drink: time from first pour to garnish, any spec error, and one accuracy check (pour the finished drink into a measuring jug for three of the twelve). Afterwards, pick the three weakest drinks, the ones with spec errors or the slowest times, and put their cards back into box 1 of your Leitner system ([lesson 05.1](lesson-01.md)). Testing yourself again a few days later is what makes the improvement stick.

> [!NOTE]
> Specs in this lesson are IBA, checked on iba-world.com on 2026-10-17. Your bar's house specs may differ; the method of practice stays the same.

## Step by step

### Part 1: blind recall (20 minutes)

1. Print [labs/module-05/recall-grid.md](../../labs/module-05/recall-grid.md) or copy its grid to paper. Put away your cards and this lesson.
2. Set a timer for 15 minutes. Fill in all twelve rows from memory: ingredients with ml, method, glass and ice, garnish.
3. Stop when the timer ends. Leave blanks blank.
4. Mark it with [labs/module-05/recall-grid-solution.md](../../labs/module-05/recall-grid-solution.md): one point per correct part, 48 in total.
5. Write your score and the parts you missed in your practice log.

### Part 2: the twelve-drink session (about 90 minutes including prep and clean-up)

1. Prepare your station as in [lesson 01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md), with the shopping list from [labs/module-05/twelve-drink-session.md](../../labs/module-05/twelve-drink-session.md).
2. Do the prep block: juice, syrup, mint, garnishes, chilled glasses, coffee ready to brew.
3. Make the twelve home-practice or alcohol-free versions in the planned order. Time each one.
4. For three drinks of your choice, pour the finished drink into a measuring jug and note the volume.
5. Record each drink in [labs/module-05/twelve-drink-log.csv](../../labs/module-05/twelve-drink-log.csv).
6. Reset the station: wash, dry and put away everything.
7. Pick your three weakest drinks and write a review plan.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- Your full home station from Modules 1 and 4: shaker, mixing glass or jar, jigger, bar spoon, Hawthorne and fine strainer, muddler, peeler, knife and board, citrus press.
- Glasses: two stemmed (coupe or martini), two short (rocks), one tall (highball), one wine glass. Wash and reuse between drinks.
- Ingredients for the home-practice versions (full list in [twelve-drink-session.md](../../labs/module-05/twelve-drink-session.md)): limes, lemons, oranges, mint, sugar and sugar cubes, 1:1 syrup, black and green tea, cranberry drink, orange juice, ginger beer, soda and sparkling water, chickpea can (aquafaba), coffee or espresso, salt, plenty of ice.
- A measuring jug, a phone timer, the [recall grid](../../labs/module-05/recall-grid.md), the [session log](../../labs/module-05/twelve-drink-log.csv) and your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md). Blank cards: [templates/recipe-card.md](../../templates/recipe-card.md).
- Low-cost alternative: one glass of each shape is enough if you wash it between drinks; any jar works as shaker and mixing glass.

> [!CAUTION]
> This session uses knives, a peeler, glass and hot coffee. Cut on a board away from your fingers, muddle only in thick glass, add ice to hot espresso before sealing the shaker, and use aquafaba or pasteurised egg white only. All twelve drinks are alcohol-free here; if you choose to taste a real drink another day, keep it to small sips, no more than about one standard drink in a session, and never before driving.

### Time

About 110 minutes: 20 for the recall grid, 15 for prep, about 60 for building twelve drinks, 15 for reset and the review plan. Split it over two days if you need to (recall one day, session the next).

### Steps

1. Do Part 1 (blind recall) and score it.
2. Do Part 2 (the session) in the planned order, timing each drink.
3. Measure the volume of three finished drinks.
4. Fill in the log for every drink: time, spec error yes/no, what went wrong.
5. Reset the station and time the reset.
6. Write your review plan: the three weakest drinks, what exactly went wrong, and when you will practise them again (put their cards back in Leitner box 1).
7. Run the simulation round and compare its score with your paper grid.

### What you should see

After a week of daily Leitner reviews most learners score 36–44 of 48 on the grid; the common misses are small pours (Margarita lime 15, Mule lime 10, Martini vermouth 10), the Daiquiri's sugar in bar spoons, the Whiskey Sour's cobbler glass and garnish, and the Espresso Martini's syrup. In the session, built and stirred drinks take about 30–75 seconds each, shaken sours 45–90 seconds, the Mojito the longest. Building time usually totals 15–25 minutes; the rest of the hour goes on washing glasses, re-icing and prep you forgot. Measured volumes should be within about 10% of the expected size including meltwater.

### What this practice cannot teach

A home session has no guests, no tickets arriving mid-drink and no real spirits; it trains memory, order and movement, and the Module 8 service simulation and real shifts add the pressure.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I filled the recall grid from memory in 15 minutes and scored it honestly.
- [ ] My recall score is at least 39 of 48 (80%), or I have scheduled a retest.
- [ ] I made all twelve drinks as home-practice or alcohol-free versions in one session.
- [ ] I logged time and errors for every drink and measured three volumes.
- [ ] I wrote a review plan for my three weakest drinks.

## Common mistakes

- **Peeking during recall.** It feels better and teaches nothing. Fix: cards away, timer on, blanks stay blank.
- **Generous marking.** "Close enough" on ml. Fix: ±5 ml or no point; the final exam uses the same rule.
- **No prep block.** You stop mid-session to juice limes. Fix: prep everything before the first drink.
- **Random order.** Shaker, mixing glass and mint in use at once. Fix: follow the block order: stirred, shaken, coffee, long.
- **Skipping the review plan.** The session shows the gaps; the plan closes them. Fix: write it the same day.

## Key takeaways

- The recall standard is per drink: ml within ±5 ml, method, glass and ice, garnish; 80% to pass, as in the final exam.
- Plan a session like a shift: prep first, then stirred, shaken, coffee and long drinks in blocks.
- Most of the time in a session goes on prep, washing and ice, not on the drinks themselves.
- Log every drink; your three weakest go back to Leitner box 1.
- Retest after a few days: spaced recall is what makes the specs stick.
