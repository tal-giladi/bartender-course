# 05.2 · Martini and Manhattan

The Martini and the Manhattan are the two most famous stirred cocktails. Each is a strong spirit softened by vermouth, stirred over ice until cold and silky, and served without ice in a chilled stemmed glass. They have almost nothing to hide behind: no juice, no sugar syrup, no soda. That makes them a test of measuring, stirring and temperature, and the reason experienced bartenders judge a new colleague by them. This lesson gives both IBA specs on full recipe cards, the main variations and where they come from, and a stirring practice you can do with water and cold tea.

## Why it matters

A guest at the bar says: "A Martini, very dry, with a twist." Another wants "a Manhattan, but with bourbon." If you know the spec, you know exactly what changes: less vermouth for "very dry", a lemon peel instead of an olive, bourbon instead of rye. If you do not, you guess, and in a drink this simple every guess shows. Stirred drinks are also slow: the 20–30 seconds you stir is time you must plan for when tickets are waiting.

## Core ideas

### What the two drinks share

Both follow the **spirit-forward stirred** template from [lesson 05.1](lesson-01.md): a base spirit plus a smaller amount of aromatised wine (vermouth), sometimes a dash of bitters. Neither contains citrus juice, so neither is shaken; stirring chills and dilutes them while keeping them clear and smooth ([lesson 04.3](../module-04/lesson-03.md)). Both are served "up", in a glass chilled beforehand with ice and water or in the freezer.

### Vermouth

**Vermouth** is wine flavoured with herbs and spices and fortified with a little spirit ([lesson 02.7](../module-02/lesson-07.md)). **Dry vermouth** (pale, low sugar) goes in a Martini; **sweet red vermouth** (rosso) goes in a Manhattan. Because it is wine, an opened bottle goes dull: keep it closed and refrigerated after opening.

### "Dry" and "wet"

In a Martini, **dry** means less vermouth and **wet** means more. The IBA spec is 6 parts gin to 1 part vermouth (60:10 ml), a dry Martini. Difford's Guide lists versions from 1:1 to 15:1 and gives a 4:1 recipe (60:15 ml). A guest who asks for "extra dry" wants even less vermouth; ask if unsure.

### Garnish changes the drink

The **garnish** (קישוט, *kishut*) is not decoration only; in a drink this simple it changes the aroma. The IBA Martini garnish is lemon oil: you squeeze a strip of lemon peel over the drink so its oil sprays on the surface ([lesson 04.7](../module-04/lesson-07.md)). An olive is the alternative "if requested". Lemon makes the drink smell fresh and bright; an olive adds a little salt and savoury aroma. The Manhattan gets a cocktail cherry.

### Rye and bourbon

**Rye whiskey** is American whiskey made mostly from rye grain; it tastes spicier and drier. **Bourbon** is made mostly from corn; it tastes rounder and sweeter ([lesson 02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). The IBA Manhattan uses rye. Bourbon Manhattans are very common; Difford's Guide's own version mixes bourbon and a strong rye.

| | Dry Martini | Manhattan |
|---|---|---|
| Base | gin 60 ml | rye whiskey 50 ml |
| Modifier | dry vermouth 10 ml | sweet red vermouth 20 ml |
| Accent | lemon oil | 1 dash Angostura bitters |
| Glass | chilled martini glass, no ice | chilled cocktail glass, no ice |
| Garnish | lemon peel oil, or olive if requested | cocktail cherry |

> Specs checked on iba-world.com and diffordsguide.com on 2026-10-17. Brand names on Difford's pages are their examples, not requirements.

> [!CAUTION]
> Both drinks are almost pure spirit: one Martini at the IBA spec holds about 60 ml of 40% gin. If you choose to taste the real drink, make a half-size version, sip a little and spit, and never taste before driving. All practice in this lesson works without alcohol.

## Step by step

### Dry Martini

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Gin | 60 | 2 |
| Dry vermouth | 10 | ⅓ |

- **Method:** stir with ice in a mixing glass for 20–30 seconds, strain with a Hawthorne or julep strainer.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled martini glass, no ice.
- **Garnish:** squeeze the oil from a lemon peel over the drink; green olive instead if the guest asks.
- **Profile and key technique:** dry, cold, aromatic, about as strong as a cocktail gets. The key is temperature: a well-chilled glass and a full, even stir. A warm Martini tastes harsh.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Dry Martini](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/dry-martini/). Variations: [Difford's Guide 4:1](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/4964/dry-martini-41-ratio) is 60 ml gin, 15 ml dry vermouth, optional dash of orange bitters, lemon twist and/or olive; Difford's [ratios guide](https://www.diffordsguide.com/g/1121/martini/dry-martini-ratios) covers 1:1 ("fifty-fifty") to 15:1. A vodka Martini (vodka instead of gin) is a common guest request (professional judgment).
- **Substitutions:** any London dry gin works; a softer, more floral gin changes the profile. No dry vermouth: do not use sweet vermouth (that is a different drink); serve gin stirred and tell the guest.
- **Home-practice version:** 60 ml cold water + 10 ml cold weak green tea, stirred in a jar with ice cubes for 25 seconds, strained into a glass chilled in the freezer. Weigh before and after to see dilution ([lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md)).
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** none that is faithful: the drink is mostly spirit, and its warmth and weight cannot be copied. The closest offer is an alcohol-free gin alternative stirred with a little dry alcohol-free aperitif and a lemon twist, described honestly as "Martini-style".
- **Common mistakes:** shaking it (cloudy, ice chips, more dilution); warm glass; old, oxidised vermouth; dropping a lemon wedge in instead of expressing peel oil.

### Manhattan

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Rye whiskey | 50 | 1⅔ |
| Sweet red vermouth | 20 | ⅔ |
| Angostura bitters | 1 dash | — |

- **Method:** stir with ice in a mixing glass for 20–30 seconds, strain.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass (coupe or martini glass), no ice.
- **Garnish:** cocktail cherry.
- **Profile and key technique:** rich, spicy and gently sweet, with herbal bitterness. The key is balance between whiskey and vermouth: measure the vermouth exactly, it is easy to over-pour.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Manhattan](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/manhattan/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Manhattan](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/1247/manhattan) is 45 ml bourbon + 15 ml 50% rye, 30 ml sweet vermouth, 1 dash Angostura, stirred, fine-strained into a chilled coupe, orange zest expressed and discarded, maraschino cherry. A "perfect" Manhattan splits the vermouth between sweet and dry (Difford's Guide lists it).
- **Substitutions:** bourbon for rye (sweeter, rounder; tell the guest if the menu says rye). Orange bitters if there is no Angostura changes the aroma; better to say so.
- **Home-practice version:** 50 ml cold water + 20 ml cold strong black tea (colour and some grip like vermouth) + 1 drop of vanilla or cinnamon water as the "dash", stirred 25 seconds and strained into a chilled glass.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** none that is faithful, for the same reason as the Martini. Closest: an alcohol-free whiskey alternative stirred with an alcohol-free red aperitif and a dash of non-alcoholic bitters, served as "Manhattan-style".
- **Common mistakes:** over-pouring vermouth (too sweet); shaking; forgetting the bitters; a bright red, syrupy cherry dropped in with its syrup.

### Service order for a stirred drink

1. Chill the glass first (ice and water, or freezer).
2. Measure into the mixing glass: smallest and cheapest ingredients first (bitters, vermouth), spirit last, so a mistake wastes less.
3. Fill the mixing glass about ¾ with fresh, dry ice cubes.
4. Stir 20–30 seconds, quietly, back of the spoon against the glass.
5. Empty the serving glass, strain the drink into it.
6. Garnish: express the peel over the drink or add the cherry. Serve at once.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A mixing glass or a large jar, a bar spoon or long teaspoon, a Hawthorne or tea strainer, a jigger, two stemmed glasses (any small wine glass works), ice cubes.
- Cold water, cold weak green tea, cold strong black tea.
- A lemon and a peeler; a kitchen scale (1 g steps); a phone timer.
- Your recipe cards, the [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: a jar and a teaspoon instead of a mixing glass and bar spoon.

> [!CAUTION]
> You use a peeler on citrus. Peel away from your hand on a cutting board, as in [lesson 01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md). Stemmed glasses break easily: chill them upright, not stacked.

### Time

50 minutes: 5 to set up and chill the glasses, 30 for six stirred reps, 10 for peel expression, 5 to record.

### Steps

1. Recite both specs from memory. Check against your cards. Fix any miss.
2. Chill two glasses in the freezer or with ice water.
3. **Martini reps (×3).** Weigh the empty mixing glass. Add 10 ml weak green tea and 60 ml water, weigh. Add ice, stir 25 seconds by the timer, strain into a chilled glass, weigh the strained drink. Write down the gain in grams (meltwater).
4. Cut a strip of lemon peel without white pith. Hold it shiny side down about 5 cm above the drink and squeeze. Look at the surface against the light for the fine oil spray.
5. **Manhattan reps (×3).** Same as step 3 with 20 ml black tea and 50 ml water and one drop of flavoured water as the dash. Add a cherry (or a red grape) on a pick.
6. For all six reps, record: total time from first pour to garnish, grams gained, and whether the liquid in the glass was clear with no ice chips.
7. Run one round of the simulation and try to pass both cards.

### What you should see

The liquid gains water as you stir: with water-only drills the gain is small and varies with your ice, because plain water cannot get colder than 0 °C the way a real spirit-based drink does ([lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md)). What matters here is consistency: your three gains should be within a few grams of each other. Each rep should take about 60–75 seconds from first pour to garnish. The strained liquid should be clear, without chips. The lemon oil shows as a faint mist and a fresh smell over the glass.

### What this practice cannot teach

Water and tea cannot show how a real Martini's texture and aroma change with dilution and temperature, or how fresh and old vermouth differ; that needs real ingredients, tasted in small amounts, or a working shift.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I can recite both specs with ml, method, glass and garnish.
- [ ] I stirred six reps and recorded time and gain for each.
- [ ] My three gains per drink were within a few grams of each other.
- [ ] I expressed lemon peel oil and saw the spray.

## Common mistakes

- **Shaking a Martini or Manhattan.** Spot it: cloudy drink, ice chips, foam. Fix: stir; shake only drinks with juice, egg or coffee.
- **Warm glass.** The drink warms in seconds. Fix: chill the glass before you start the drink, not after.
- **Over-pouring vermouth.** The drink gets sweet (Manhattan) or flabby (Martini). Fix: jigger the vermouth every time; it is the easiest pour to rush.
- **Old vermouth.** Dull, sour, flat. Fix: date the bottle when opened, keep it in the fridge.
- **Wrong garnish.** An olive in a Martini ordered "with a twist". Fix: ask "twist or olive?" when the guest does not say.
- **Stirring too long or too short.** Too short: warm and harsh; too long: watery. Fix: time 20–30 seconds until it is automatic.

## Key takeaways

- IBA Dry Martini: gin 60 ml, dry vermouth 10 ml, stirred, chilled martini glass, lemon oil (olive on request).
- IBA Manhattan: rye 50 ml, sweet vermouth 20 ml, 1 dash Angostura, stirred, chilled cocktail glass, cherry.
- Dry means less vermouth; wet means more. Difford's 4:1 is a common wetter Martini.
- Bourbon Manhattans are common; Difford's mixes bourbon and rye with 30 ml vermouth.
- Chill the glass first, measure small ingredients first, stir 20–30 seconds, strain clear.
