# 05.3 · Old Fashioned and Negroni

The Old Fashioned and the Negroni are the two great spirit-forward drinks served on ice in a short, heavy glass. The Old Fashioned is the oldest pattern in the cocktail book: spirit, sugar, bitters and a little water. The Negroni is three ingredients in equal parts, which makes it one of the easiest specs to remember and one of the easiest to get slightly wrong. Both are made in the glass the guest drinks from. This lesson gives both IBA specs on full recipe cards, the syrup method most bars use for the Old Fashioned, the Negroni's best-known relative, and a build practice with tea and water.

## Why it matters

Friday, 22:30. Four Negronis on one ticket. If you know it is 30, 30 and 30 built straight in the glass, you line up four glasses and pour them in a minute. If you stop to think, or shake them by habit, the ticket waits and the drinks come out cloudy. The Old Fashioned is the opposite challenge: a guest at the counter watches you make it, and an undissolved lump of sugar at the bottom of the glass is something they will notice on the last sip.

## Core ideas

### Built and stirred in the glass

Both drinks follow the **spirit-forward stirred** template ([lesson 05.1](lesson-01.md)) but are **built** in the serving glass on ice instead of stirred in a mixing glass and strained. You learned to build in [lesson 04.2](../module-04/lesson-02.md) and to stir in [lesson 04.3](../module-04/lesson-03.md); here you combine them: pour into the glass with ice, then stir gently for 10–15 seconds to mix and start chilling. The drink keeps chilling and diluting slowly as the guest drinks, which is why both are served over ice and why good, cold, dry ice matters ([lesson 01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)).

### The Old Fashioned: sugar, bitters, water, spirit

By the 1860s, drinkers who missed the simple early cocktails of spirit, sugar, water and bitters asked for them "the old-fashioned way", and the name stuck (Wikipedia, Old fashioned). The IBA spec keeps the original steps: soak a sugar cube with Angostura bitters and a few dashes of water, **muddle** (press and crush, [lesson 04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md)) until the sugar dissolves, add ice and whiskey, stir.

Most busy bars use **sugar syrup** instead of a cube, because it mixes at once ([lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)). Difford's Guide's recipe uses 12.5 ml of 2:1 syrup with 60 ml bourbon. Both methods are correct; follow the house spec.

**Angostura bitters** are a strong, spiced bitter tincture used by the dash ([lesson 02.6](../module-02/lesson-06.md)). A **dash** is one quick shake of the bottle, about 0.8 ml in this course. "Few dashes" in the IBA spec usually means 2–3 (professional judgment).

### The Negroni: equal parts

Gin, **Campari** (a red, bitter Italian aperitif, [lesson 02.7](../module-02/lesson-07.md)) and sweet red vermouth, 30 ml each. Bitter, sweet and strong at once. Equal parts make it easy to scale and to batch, but only if each pour is truly 30 ml: Campari and vermouth are sticky and cling to the jigger.

### Garnish: orange

Both drinks are garnished with orange. The IBA Negroni gets half an orange slice. The IBA Old Fashioned gets an orange slice or zest plus a cherry; many bars use only an expressed orange zest (Difford's Guide does).

| | Old Fashioned | Negroni |
|---|---|---|
| Base | bourbon or rye 45 ml | gin 30 ml |
| Modifier / sweet | 1 sugar cube | Campari 30 ml + sweet red vermouth 30 ml |
| Accent | few dashes Angostura + few dashes water | — |
| Method | muddle cube, add ice and whiskey, stir gently | build on ice, stir gently |
| Glass and ice | old fashioned glass, cubes | chilled old fashioned glass, cubes |
| Garnish | orange slice or zest + cherry | half orange slice |

> [!NOTE]
> Specs checked on iba-world.com and diffordsguide.com on 2026-10-17. Campari is a brand; other red bitter aperitifs exist and taste different.

> [!CAUTION]
> Both drinks are about two thirds or more spirit and liqueur by volume. Practice uses tea and water. If you taste a real one, make a half-size version, sip and spit, and never before driving.

## Step by step

### Old Fashioned

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Bourbon or rye whiskey | 45 | 1½ |
| Sugar cube | 1 cube | — |
| Angostura bitters | few dashes | — |
| Plain water | few dashes | — |

- **Method:** place the cube in the glass, soak it with the bitters, add a few dashes of water, muddle until the sugar dissolves, fill the glass with ice, add the whiskey, stir gently 10–15 seconds.
- **Glass and ice:** old fashioned (rocks) glass, ice cubes (a large cube if the bar has them).
- **Garnish:** orange slice or zest, and a cocktail cherry.
- **Profile and key technique:** whiskey first, then soft sweetness and spice from the bitters, rounder as the ice melts. The key is fully dissolving the sugar before the ice goes in: sugar hardly dissolves in cold spirit.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Old Fashioned](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/old-fashioned/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Old Fashioned](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/4782/old-fashioned) is 60 ml bourbon, 12.5 ml 2:1 sugar syrup, 3 dashes Angostura, stirred with ice and strained onto fresh ice (a large cube if possible) in a chilled glass, orange zest twist. Difford's Guide also lists rum, gin and other spirit versions.
- **Substitutions:** 7.5–10 ml 2:1 syrup or about 10–15 ml 1:1 syrup for the cube (taste and follow the house spec; professional judgment). Bourbon and rye are both IBA-correct.
- **Home-practice version:** cube + 3 dashes of cold black tea from a small dasher bottle + 3 dashes water, muddle until dissolved, ice, 45 ml cold strong black tea, stir 10–15 seconds. Then repeat with 10 ml 1:1 syrup instead of the cube and compare the time.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** none that is faithful: the drink is almost all whiskey. Closest: an alcohol-free whiskey alternative with a little syrup and bitters on ice, or strong black tea with demerara syrup, orange zest and a few dashes of bitters (a tiny amount of alcohol from the bitters; say so to the guest).
- **Common mistakes:** sugar left undissolved; muddling an orange slice and cherry into a pulp (a habit that spread much later, not the IBA spec); too much water; using crushed ice (melts too fast).

### Negroni

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Gin | 30 | 1 |
| Campari | 30 | 1 |
| Sweet red vermouth | 30 | 1 |

- **Method:** pour all three straight into a chilled old fashioned glass filled with ice, stir gently 10–15 seconds.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled old fashioned glass, ice cubes.
- **Garnish:** half an orange slice.
- **Profile and key technique:** bitter, sweet and herbal, strong and very aromatic. The key is accurate 30 ml pours of two sticky liquids; a short pour of vermouth makes it harsh, an over-pour of Campari makes it bitter.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Negroni](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/negroni/). Variations: [Difford's Guide Negroni](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/1392/negroni-cocktail) uses the same equal parts and allows an orange wedge, slice or zest twist. [Difford's Guide Negroni Sbagliato](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/2515/negroni-sbagliato) swaps the gin for 60 ml Prosecco (30 ml sweet vermouth, 30 ml red bitter, 60 ml Prosecco, built on ice, brief stir). The Boulevardier, with whiskey instead of gin, comes in Module 6.
- **Substitutions:** another red bitter aperitif for Campari (tell the guest; taste differs); any London dry gin.
- **Home-practice version:** 30 ml cold water (gin) + 30 ml cold hibiscus tea or diluted red grape juice with a drop of grapefruit (the red bitter) + 30 ml cold strong black tea (vermouth), built in an iced glass and stirred. Pour the "sticky" parts with a syrup-thickened liquid sometimes to practise draining the jigger.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** equal parts of an alcohol-free gin alternative, an alcohol-free red bitter aperitif and an alcohol-free red vermouth alternative over ice with orange. Close in look and bitterness, lighter in body; describe it as "Negroni-style".
- **Common mistakes:** shaking it; under-filling with ice (it warms and dilutes fast); not draining the jigger; a full orange wheel that blocks the drink.

### Four Negronis on one ticket

1. Line up four chilled glasses and fill each with ice.
2. Pour gin 30 ml into each, then Campari, then vermouth: one bottle at a time across all glasses, so you pick each bottle up only once.
3. Stir each 10–15 seconds, garnish, serve together.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- Two old fashioned glasses (any short, heavy glass), ice cubes, a jigger, a bar spoon or long teaspoon, a muddler or the handle of a wooden spoon.
- White sugar cubes, 1:1 syrup from [lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md), cold strong black tea, cold hibiscus tea or diluted red grape juice, water.
- A small dropper or dasher bottle (a clean, small sauce bottle works) filled with cold black tea as "bitters".
- An orange, a peeler or paring knife, a cutting board, a phone timer, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: any short glass and a teaspoon; a jar lid as a muddler base is not needed, the back of a spoon works.

> [!CAUTION]
> Muddle gently in a thick-bottomed glass only: a thin glass can crack and cut your hand. Cut orange on a board with the knife moving away from your fingers ([lesson 01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md)).

### Time

45 minutes: 15 for three cube Old Fashioneds, 10 for three syrup versions, 15 for a four-glass Negroni round twice, 5 to record.

### Steps

1. Recite both specs. Check your cards.
2. **Cube Old Fashioned (×3).** Start the timer. Cube in the glass, 3 dashes "bitters", 3 dashes water, muddle until you cannot feel grains under the muddler. Note the time. Add ice, 45 ml black tea, stir 10–15 seconds, garnish with orange zest. Stop the timer.
3. Tip the glass at the end: any sugar on the bottom? Record yes or no.
4. **Syrup Old Fashioned (×3).** Same build with 10 ml 1:1 syrup instead of cube and water. Record the time.
5. **Negroni round (×2).** Four glasses with ice. Pour 30 ml of each liquid into all four, one bottle at a time, stir, garnish with half orange slices. Time the whole round.
6. Check one glass: pour its liquid into a measuring jug before the ice melts much. Is it about 90 ml?
7. Run the simulation and pass both cards.

### What you should see

Muddling a cube with a little liquid takes about 30–60 seconds; the syrup version is ready in half the time or less, which is why bars use syrup. If you added the ice before the sugar was fully dissolved, you find grains on the bottom at the end. A four-Negroni round should drop under 2 minutes by the second try. The measured volume should be close to 90 ml; less means you did not drain the jigger.

### What this practice cannot teach

Tea cannot show how the real bitters, whiskey and vermouth taste together or how the drink opens up as the ice melts; only small tastings of the real drink or service at a bar teach that.

## Common mistakes

- **Undissolved sugar.** Spot it: grains on the bottom, the last sip too sweet. Fix: muddle until smooth before ice and spirit, or use syrup.
- **Shaking a Negroni.** Cloudy, foamy, over-diluted. Fix: build on ice and stir.
- **Uneven equal parts.** Sticky liquids stay in the jigger. Fix: let the jigger drain fully, or rinse it in water between drinks.
- **Too little ice.** A half-filled glass melts fast and the drink waters down. Fix: fill the glass with ice to the top.
- **Making a fruit salad.** Mashed orange and cherry in an Old Fashioned is not the IBA spec. Fix: follow the spec or house spec; ask if a guest wants it "muddled with fruit".

## Key takeaways

- IBA Old Fashioned: 45 ml bourbon or rye, 1 sugar cube, few dashes Angostura and water, muddled, ice, stirred; orange slice or zest and cherry.
- Syrup instead of the cube is common and faster (Difford's: 60 ml bourbon, 12.5 ml 2:1 syrup, 3 dashes).
- IBA Negroni: gin, Campari, sweet red vermouth, 30 ml each, built on ice in a chilled old fashioned glass, half orange slice.
- Sugar does not dissolve in cold spirit: dissolve it first.
- Build rounds one bottle at a time across all glasses.
