# 05.6 · Mojito and Moscow Mule

The Mojito and the Moscow Mule are long drinks: a spirit and lime stretched with a fizzy mixer over ice in a tall glass or a mug. They are some of the most ordered cocktails on a summer night and some of the slowest to make badly, because the Mojito needs muddled mint and the Mule needs ginger beer that still has its bite. Both are built straight in the serving glass, so speed comes from doing the steps in the right order. This lesson gives both IBA specs on full recipe cards, the main variations with their sources, how to muddle mint without shredding it, why copper mugs need care, and a timed build practice without alcohol.

## Why it matters

A group of six on a hot evening orders six Mojitos. If each one takes three minutes because you are tearing up mint and hunting for the soda, the last drink arrives after the first is finished. If you muddle the mint to a green paste, the drinks taste grassy and bitter and the guests spend the evening picking leaves out of their teeth. The Moscow Mule is quicker, but a flat ginger beer or a warm, half-filled glass turns it into sweet vodka. Long drinks are about rhythm, order and keeping the fizz.

## Core ideas

### The highball template with a twist

Both drinks follow the **highball / long** template ([lesson 05.1](lesson-01.md)): spirit + lime + a long fizzy mixer, built on ice in the glass ([lesson 04.2](../module-04/lesson-02.md)). A **highball glass** (tall, straight) or **Collins glass** (a little taller and narrower) holds them; the IBA Mojito page names no glass, so this course uses a highball, and the IBA Mule goes in a mule cup or a rocks glass.

### Muddling mint

**Muddling** means pressing ingredients in the glass to release juice or aroma ([lesson 04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md)). Mint's aroma sits in tiny oil glands on the leaf surface: a few gentle presses release it. Grinding and twisting tears the leaves into small dark bits that look bad, get stuck in straws, and many bartenders find make the drink taste grassy or bitter (professional judgment). Press, do not grind.

**Mint** (נענע, *nana*) comes in sprigs: a **mint sprig** is a small stem with its leaves. The IBA uses 6 sprigs; Difford's Guide counts 14 leaves. Choose fresh, bright mint and keep it in water in the fridge, like cut flowers.

### Keeping the fizz

You learned in [lesson 03.6](../module-03/lesson-06.md) that cold liquid holds CO2 better and that rough pouring knocks it out. So: cold soda and ginger beer, a glass full of ice, the fizzy mixer poured last and gently down the side or a bar spoon, and a short, light stir, never a hard one. The IBA Mojito starts with a splash of soda on the mint before the ice; that helps spread the sugar and mint, then most of the soda goes on top.

### Ginger beer and the copper mug

**Ginger beer** is a fizzy, spicy ginger soft drink; it is hotter and less sweet than **ginger ale**. Do not swap one for the other without telling the guest.

The Mule's copper mug is famous, but copper dissolves into acidic drinks. The U.S. FDA Model Food Code says copper should not touch foods with a pH below 6.0, and lime makes a Mule well below that. After a 2017 advisory from the Iowa Alcoholic Beverages Division, the accepted practice is: copper mugs **lined** inside with stainless steel or nickel are fine; solid, unlined copper is not used for the drink (Wikipedia, Moscow mule). A highball or rocks glass is always fine.

## Step by step

### Mojito

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| White rum | 45 | 1½ |
| Fresh lime juice | 20 | ⅔ |
| White cane sugar | 2 tsp | — |
| Mint | 6 sprigs | — |
| Soda water | to top (about 60–90 in a highball) | 2–3 |

- **Method:** in the glass, muddle the mint gently with the sugar and lime juice; add a splash of soda; fill the glass with ice; add the rum; top with soda; stir lightly, lifting from the bottom.
- **Glass and ice:** highball glass, filled with ice (cubes per IBA; crushed is common).
- **Garnish:** mint sprigs and a lime slice.
- **Profile and key technique:** fresh, minty, lime-sour and lightly sweet, very refreshing. The key is the mint: a few gentle presses, whole leaves, a fresh sprig on top for aroma.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Mojito](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/mojito/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Mojito](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/1341/mojito-cocktail) is 14 mint leaves, 60 ml light rum, 15 ml lime, 10 ml 2:1 syrup, churned with crushed ice in a Collins glass, topped with only 15 ml soda, mint sprig bouquet. The soda top "to top" in the IBA spec depends on the glass; about 60–90 ml fills a standard highball over ice (professional judgment).
- **Substitutions:** 10–15 ml 1:1 syrup for the sugar (dissolves at once); demerara sugar for a darker taste. No fresh mint: do not make it with dried mint; offer a different drink.
- **Home-practice version:** the full build with 45 ml cold water instead of rum. With real mint, lime, sugar and soda it is already a Virgin Mojito.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** Virgin Mojito: muddle mint, 20 ml lime, 2 tsp sugar (or 15 ml 1:1 syrup), ice, top with about 120 ml soda, stir lightly. A faithful, guest-ready drink.
- **Common mistakes:** shredded mint; undissolved sugar on the bottom; soda poured first and then muddled (it goes flat); half a glass of ice (watery fast); a sprig with brown leaves as garnish.

### Moscow Mule

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Vodka | 45 | 1½ |
| Ginger beer | 120 | 4 |
| Fresh lime juice | 10 | ⅓ |

- **Method:** fill the mug or glass with ice; pour the vodka and ginger beer; add the lime juice; stir gently once or twice.
- **Glass and ice:** mule cup (lined copper or stainless) or rocks glass, ice cubes.
- **Garnish:** lime slice.
- **Profile and key technique:** spicy, gingery, lightly sour, fizzy. The key is cold ginger beer poured gently on a full glass of ice, and a gentle stir that keeps the bubbles.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Moscow Mule](https://iba-world.com/cocktails/moscow-mule/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Moscow Mule](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/1366/moscow-mule) is 60 ml vodka, 15 ml lime, 10 ml 2:1 syrup, 90 ml ginger beer, in a copper mug or Collins glass two-thirds filled with cubed ice, topped with crushed ice, lime wheel and mint. Other spirits make other mules (Difford's lists a Mexican Mule with tequila).
- **Substitutions:** any neutral vodka; ginger ale only if the guest agrees (much milder and sweeter); a highball if no lined mug.
- **Home-practice version:** the full build with 45 ml cold water for the vodka.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** 10–15 ml lime and 150 ml ginger beer on ice with a lime slice. Close and guest-ready; it misses only the vodka's dryness.
- **Common mistakes:** warm or flat ginger beer; ginger ale used without telling the guest; hard stirring; unlined copper mug; forgetting the lime.

### Six Mojitos on one ticket

1. Line up six highballs. Put mint, sugar and lime in each (one ingredient at a time across all glasses).
2. Muddle each glass with 3–4 gentle presses.
3. A splash of soda in each, then fill all six with ice.
4. Rum into each, then top each with soda, gently.
5. Stir each lightly, garnish, serve together.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- Two highball glasses and one rocks glass or mug, ice, a jigger, a muddler (or the flat end of a wooden spoon), a bar spoon or long teaspoon.
- A bunch of fresh mint, 3–4 limes, white sugar, cold soda water, cold ginger beer, cold water.
- A kitchen scale, a phone timer, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: any tall glass and a wooden spoon handle; a fresh bottle of soda is enough for the fizz check.

> [!CAUTION]
> Muddle only in a thick, sturdy glass and press straight down: a thin glass can crack and cut your hand. Cut limes on a board ([lesson 01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md)).

### Time

50 minutes: 10 to set up, 20 for four Mojitos, 10 for three Mules, 10 for the fizz check and recording.

### Steps

1. Recite both specs. Check your cards.
2. **Mint test.** In one glass, press 6 sprigs' leaves gently 3–4 times. In a second glass, grind another 6 hard for 15 seconds. Smell both, then look at the leaves. Use the gentle one for the next step and throw the other away.
3. **Virgin Mojito (×4).** Full build with water instead of rum: time each from empty glass to garnish. Taste the first one: balanced or too sweet or sour?
4. **Mule (×3).** Full build with water instead of vodka. Time each.
5. **Fizz check.** Weigh a finished Mule right after building and again 5 minutes later; the weight lost is mostly CO2 leaving ([lesson 03.6](../module-03/lesson-06.md)). Then build a second one with the ginger beer poured fast from high and stirred hard; weigh it the same way.
6. Taste the last sip of each Mule: which one still prickles?
7. Run the simulation and pass both cards.

### What you should see

The gently pressed mint smells as fresh as the hard-ground mint, or more, and the leaves stay whole; the ground leaves are dark and torn. Your Mojito time should fall from around 2 minutes to about 60–80 seconds by the fourth one. A Mule takes 20–30 seconds. In the fizz check, the careful Mule loses less weight and still prickles at the end; the rough one loses more and tastes flat and sweeter.

### What this practice cannot teach

These virgin versions are true drinks, but they cannot show how much the rum or vodka changes the balance, or how to keep up a Mojito rhythm with a queue at the bar; real shifts and the Module 8 service simulation train that.

## Common mistakes

- **Shredded mint.** Spot it: dark green bits in the straw. Fix: 3–4 gentle presses, no twisting.
- **Flat soda or ginger beer.** Fix: keep mixers cold, open a fresh bottle, pour gently last, stir lightly.
- **Too little ice.** It melts fast and the drink goes watery and warm. Fix: fill the glass with ice.
- **Ginger ale for ginger beer.** Much sweeter and milder. Fix: use ginger beer, or tell the guest before swapping.
- **Unlined copper mug.** Fix: lined copper, stainless or glass only.
- **Sugar on the bottom.** Fix: muddle sugar with the lime until dissolved, or use syrup.

## Key takeaways

- IBA Mojito: 45 ml white rum, 20 ml lime, 6 mint sprigs, 2 tsp white cane sugar, soda to top; muddle, splash soda, ice, rum, top, stir lightly; mint and lime slice.
- IBA Moscow Mule: 45 ml vodka, 120 ml ginger beer, 10 ml lime, built in a mule cup or rocks glass, stirred gently; lime slice.
- Press mint gently; never grind it.
- Fizzy mixer last, cold and gentle; full glass of ice.
- Use lined copper, stainless or glass for a Mule; not solid unlined copper.
