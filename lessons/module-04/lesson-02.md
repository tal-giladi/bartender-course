# 04.2 · Building drinks and highballs

Building means making a drink directly in the glass it is served in, with no shaker and no mixing glass. It is the fastest method and the one you will use most on a busy night: gin and tonic, vodka soda, Cuba Libre, Moscow Mule, Spritz, and built short drinks such as the Negroni. Because it looks simple, it is where small habits make the biggest difference: the order of pouring, how full the glass is with ice, and how you treat the fizz. This lesson teaches a repeatable build and has you build ten alcohol-free highballs against the clock and the scale.

## Why it matters

Friday, 23:00, and a group of eight orders four vodka-sodas, two gin and tonics and two Cuba Libres. A bartender who builds well lines up eight glasses, fills them with ice, pours the spirits in one pass, tops them with mixers in a second pass and serves the round in under two minutes, every drink cold and fizzy. A bartender who does each drink start to finish, with half-filled glasses and the soda poured hard onto the ice, takes twice as long and serves flat, watery drinks. Highballs are a large share of what most bars sell (professional judgment), so a clean build is real money and real speed.

## Core ideas

### What "build" means

To **build** a drink is to pour its ingredients one after another into the serving glass, usually over ice, and mix them in the glass. There are three kinds of built drink:

| Kind | Examples (IBA) | How it is mixed in the glass |
|---|---|---|
| **Highball**: spirit plus a larger amount of carbonated mixer | Cuba Libre (rum, cola, lime), Moscow Mule (vodka, ginger beer, lime) | One gentle lift with the bar spoon |
| **Spritz**: wine-based, with sparkling wine and soda | Spritz (Aperol, prosecco, soda) in a wine glass full of ice | A gentle stir |
| **Built short drink**: no mixer, all strong ingredients | Negroni, Old Fashioned (rocks glass) | A proper stir in the glass, 10–15 seconds, to chill and dilute |

A **highball** (also the name of the tall glass it is served in; Hebrew speakers often say כוס גבוהה, *kos gvoha*, "tall glass", lesson [01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md)) is the simplest mixed drink there is. The IBA specs for Cuba Libre and Moscow Mule use the same frame: about 45–50 ml spirit, 10 ml fresh lime juice and 120 ml of mixer. Most bars have a house standard for "spirit and mixer", and you should learn it on your first shift.

**Mixer** means any non-alcoholic ingredient that lengthens a drink: soda water (סודה, *soda*), tonic (טוניק, *tonik*), cola, ginger beer or ginger ale, juices. **Top** or **top up** means filling the glass to its working level with the mixer.

### The build order

The order is not a matter of taste. Each step protects something:

1. **Glass first.** Clean, checked for chips (lesson [01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)), cold if possible.
2. **Ice second, to the top.** A full glass of solid cubes stays colder, so each cube melts more slowly and the drink is less watery (lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)). Ice also takes up volume, so the spirit-to-mixer ratio comes out right when the glass is full.
3. **Measured non-carbonated ingredients.** Spirit, juice, syrup and bitters go in with a jigger, poured over the ice.
4. **Carbonated mixer last.** Poured gently down the inside of the glass or down the bar spoon, not from a height onto the ice. Bubbles escape when the liquid is shaken about or hits rough surfaces (lesson [03.6](../module-03/lesson-06.md)).
5. **One lift.** Slide the bar spoon down the side to the bottom and draw it up once. Juice and syrup are heavier than soda and sink; the lift brings them up. Stirring round and round knocks the gas out.
6. **Garnish and serve straight away.**

Punch's highball guide, quoting bartender Garret Richard, adds two refinements for better bars: keep spirits and glasses cold so the ice "weeps" less water, and for highballs with juice and syrup, pre-mix the non-carbonated part so the soda needs almost no stirring. Both are optional; the basic order above is what every bar expects.

### How much ice and mixer

A 300 ml highball glass filled with cubes holds roughly 150–180 ml of liquid, because the ice takes up the rest (professional judgment; measure your own glass in the hands-on part). That is why a highball of 45 ml spirit and 120 ml mixer fits a full glass of ice. If you use half the ice, the same pours leave the glass looking half empty, and the guest feels short-changed even though the alcohol is correct.

Pour the mixer to a fixed visual line, about 1 cm below the rim, or measure it with a jigger until your eye is calibrated. Many bars use a soda gun (a hand-held dispenser connected to bag-in-box syrups and carbonated water); it pours fast and the gas is fresh, but the rules are the same: spirit first, mixer last, no stirring round.

### Building several drinks at once

When a round contains several built drinks, work in passes:

1. Line up all the glasses in one row.
2. Ice them all.
3. Pour all the spirits, left to right.
4. Add juices and syrups.
5. Top all the mixers, then lift each drink once.
6. Garnish all, then serve.

Passes save movement: you pick up each bottle once, not once per drink. Module 8 builds on this for full service sequencing.

### Built short drinks

A Negroni or an Old Fashioned built in the glass has no mixer to dilute it, so the glass needs a proper stir after the ingredients go in: 10–15 seconds with the bar spoon against the side, to chill the drink and add some water. The IBA Negroni is poured over ice in a chilled old fashioned glass and stirred gently; the IBA Old Fashioned starts by muddling a sugar cube with bitters and a little water in the glass (lesson 04.5). Lesson 04.3 teaches the stirring movement itself.

> [!NOTE]
> Highball proportions are taken from the IBA Cuba Libre (50 ml rum, 120 ml cola, 10 ml lime) and Moscow Mule (45 ml vodka, 120 ml ginger beer, 10 ml lime) pages, checked 2026-10-17. House standards in Israeli bars vary; follow the bar's spec.

## Step by step

### Build a highball

1. Pick up a clean highball glass by the base. Look at the rim for chips.
2. Scoop ice with the scoop, never with the glass, until the glass is full to the rim.
3. Hold the jigger over the glass and pour the spirit; then the juice, if any.
4. Open the mixer (or take the soda gun). Tilt the glass slightly or hold the bar spoon against the inside wall and pour the mixer down it, low and steady, to about 1 cm below the rim.
5. Slide the bar spoon down the side to the bottom and draw it up once.
6. Add the garnish (lesson 04.7), put the glass on a coaster, serve.

### Build a spritz

1. Fill a wine glass with ice.
2. Pour in the order the house spec gives (IBA lists prosecco, Aperol, then a splash of soda), pouring the sparkling wine gently down the side.
3. Add the splash of soda and stir gently once or twice.
4. Garnish and serve.

### Build a short drink and stir it in the glass

1. Chill the rocks glass (fill with ice and water, then empty) or take it from the fridge.
2. Fill with fresh cubes or one large cube.
3. Measure each ingredient over the ice.
4. Stir with the bar spoon against the side for 10–15 seconds, smoothly, without splashing.
5. Top up with a cube if the level dropped, garnish, serve.

## Hands-on

> [!CAUTION]
> Check every glass for chips before use and handle ice only with a scoop. If a glass breaks near the ice, empty and clean the ice container before you continue (lesson 01.6).

### What you need

- 10 highball glasses, or 2–3 glasses you empty and reuse.
- About 1.5 kg of ice cubes.
- 450 ml cold black tea (the "spirit"), 100 ml lemon or lime juice or water (the "juice"), 1.5 l cold soda water.
- A jigger, a bar spoon or long teaspoon, an ice scoop or a large clean cup used only for ice.
- A kitchen scale with 1 g steps, a phone stopwatch, a phone to film one build.
- The drill log: [labs/module-04/technique-drill-log.csv](../../labs/module-04/technique-drill-log.csv) (open in any spreadsheet) and your practice log ([template](../../templates/practice-log.md)).
- Low-cost alternative: tap water instead of tea; any carbonated water from the supermarket.

### Time

45 minutes: 5 to set up, 25 for the builds, 15 to film, clean up and log.

### Steps

1. **Measure your glass.** Fill one highball glass with ice, then pour water in up to 1 cm below the rim, using the scale (1 ml of water weighs about 1 g). Write how many ml of liquid it holds with ice in it. Empty it.
2. Set out the tea, juice and soda in the order you will use them, with the jigger and spoon next to the glasses.
3. **Builds 1–5, one at a time.** Put the empty glass on the scale. Start the stopwatch, ice the glass, then tare the scale. Pour 45 ml tea and read the weight (target 45 g, ±5% = 43–47 g). Add 10 ml juice, top with soda, lift once, and stop the clock. Write the tea weight and the time.
4. **Builds 6–10 as a round.** Line up five glasses (or work in pairs if you have fewer) and build them in passes: ice all, tea all, juice all, soda all, lift all. Time the whole round and divide by five.
5. **Fizz check.** Build two more glasses the same way. In one, do a single lift; in the other, stir round ten times. After 2 minutes, look at the bubbles and taste a small sip of each (it is only tea and soda).
6. Film one build from the side at bar-top height. Watch it: is the soda poured down the side or onto the ice? Is the lift one smooth stroke?
7. Enter each rep in the drill log (movement "build"), then write your average time, pours in tolerance and one fix in your practice log.

### What you should see

The glass full of ice should hold about 150–180 ml of liquid; if yours holds much more, it is a bigger glass or the ice was not filled to the top. Single builds usually take 20–30 seconds at first and the round of five 12–18 seconds per drink, because passes save movements (professional judgment). Most tea pours land within ±5% after lesson 03.2; misses are usually short pours from tipping the jigger slowly. The lifted drink keeps visibly more bubbles after 2 minutes and tastes livelier than the stirred one.

### What this practice cannot teach

A real soda gun, a crowded rail and guests watching you change the rhythm, and tea pours a little differently from spirits, so keep checking pours with a jigger when you start at a bar.

- [ ] At least eight of my ten "spirit" pours were within ±5%, or I logged the fix and will repeat.
- [ ] I compared a lifted and a stirred highball after 2 minutes.
- [ ] I filmed one build and noted one fix in my practice log.

## Common mistakes

- **Half a glass of ice.** The drink warms fast, dilutes more and looks short. Fix: fill the glass to the rim with ice every time.
- **Mixer before spirit.** The spirit floats on top and you cannot check the pour. Fix: measured ingredients first, mixer last.
- **Pouring soda from a height onto the ice.** The gas is knocked out before the guest gets the drink. Fix: low and gentle, down the side or the spoon.
- **Stirring a highball round and round.** It goes flat. Fix: one lift from the bottom.
- **Scooping ice with the glass.** The glass can chip into the ice, and that is a safety-critical failure in this course. Fix: always use the scoop.
- **Building a round drink by drink.** It doubles your movements. Fix: line the glasses up and work in passes.

## Key takeaways

- Build order: glass → ice to the top → measured ingredients → carbonated mixer last → one lift → garnish.
- A full glass of ice keeps the drink colder, less diluted and in the right proportions.
- IBA highballs use about 45–50 ml spirit, 10 ml lime and 120 ml mixer; follow the house standard where there is one.
- Spritzes get a gentle stir; built short drinks such as the Negroni get a proper 10–15 s stir in the glass.
- Build rounds in passes: ice all, spirit all, juice all, mixer all, lift all.
