# Instructor notes — 04.1 Choosing the method: build, stir, shake or blend

- **Objective:** learner picks the method from the ingredient list using four ordered questions and can say what each method does to chill, dilution, texture and clarity.
- **Likely confusion:** IBA builds the Negroni and the Old Fashioned in the glass, while many bars stir them in a mixing glass; the sort-card solution accepts both. Point out that the method family (no shaking, no air) is what matters.
- **Misconception:** "dilution is bad" or "25% is the ideal". Arnold rejects a single ideal; in the course model a shaken sour gains about 63% and a stirred drink about 37–44%.
- **Numbers:** all dilution figures come from `simulations/common/bar-math.js` `dilutionFraction` (shake 25% ABV, 12 s → 0.63; stir 32% ABV, 20 s → 0.37, 30 s → 0.44; throw 33% ABV, 10 s → 0.39; build 10% ABV, 30 s → 0.03, 300 s → 0.14; plain water: shake 12 s → 0.19, stir 20 s → 0.16, build 30 s → 0.02). Keep any spoken figures consistent with these.
- **Expected hands-on result:** cards 28–34/40 on a first try; water weight gains in the order shake > stir > build (for example +17%, +14%, +2%), a little under the model's water values because some liquid stays on the ice. Water gains far less than a cocktail because it cannot go below 0 °C.
- **Extension:** give the learner five drinks from Module 6 (for example a French 75 or a Ramos Gin Fizz) and ask them to predict the method, then check Difford's Guide.
