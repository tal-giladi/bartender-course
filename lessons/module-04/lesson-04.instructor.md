# Instructor notes — 04.4 Shaking, dry shakes and egg white

- **Objective:** learner seals, shakes 10–15 s, opens and strains a Boston shaker safely, and can make foam with a dry or reverse dry shake using pasteurised egg white or aquafaba.
- **Likely confusion:** dry shake (no ice first) versus reverse dry shake (ice first, then no ice). A table in the lesson sets them side by side.
- **Misconception:** "alcohol and citrus make raw egg safe". They do not reliably kill Salmonella; the course requires pasteurised egg white or aquafaba.
- **Numbers:** `dilutionFraction('shake', 0.25, t)`: 5 s 0.47, 10 s 0.61, 12 s 0.63, 15 s 0.65, plateau 0.66. Plain water at 12 s: 0.19 (learners weigh about 15–18%).
- **Expected hands-on result:** no leaks after the first few reps, opening under 3 s, spread ≤ 5 points; foam roughly wet < dry < reverse dry (for example 4, 9, 12 mm at 1 minute).
- **Safety-critical:** raw egg without a pasteurised/aquafaba alternative or warning is a safety-critical failure in the final assessment (outline §6). Egg and chickpea (legume) are allergens to declare (Module 9).
- **Extension:** time a full sour from ticket to glass (measure, ice, shake, strain, garnish) and aim for under 45 seconds without losing accuracy.
