# 04.8 · Technique drills: film, time and fix your movements

This is Module 4's practical piece. You put every movement from the module on camera, ten reps each, and measure them the way a trainer would: grams on a scale, seconds on a stopwatch, and a careful look at the film. Then you score yourself with the same technique rubric the final assessment uses in lesson 11.3, compare your film with written demonstrations, and write one concrete fix per movement. Nothing here needs alcohol; everything uses water, tea, syrup, citrus and ice. The aim is not a perfect score today but honest numbers you can beat next week.

## Why it matters

At a trial shift (משמרת ניסיון, *mishmeret nisayon*, lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)) a head bartender watches you for ten minutes and decides. They do not ask what a reverse dry shake is; they watch whether your jigger pours are full, whether your shake is safe, whether you stir quietly and whether your station is clean when you finish. Most beginners cannot see their own habits: the slow jigger tip, the wrist that moves when stirring, the twist expressed from too high. A phone camera and a scale show them to you in one evening. This drill is also a rehearsal for the practical technique part of the final assessment, worth 25% of the final grade, with a 75% minimum.

## Core ideas

### Measure what a trainer would watch

Each movement is checked on three things:

- **Accuracy**: is the amount right? Measured by weight. Error % = (measured − target) ÷ target × 100. The course tolerance is ±5% (lessons [01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md) and [03.2](../module-03/lesson-02.md)). Example: 28 g poured for a 30 ml target is (28 − 30) ÷ 30 × 100 = −6.7%, out of tolerance.
- **Consistency**: is every rep the same? For stirring and shaking, the weight gain of each rep is a fingerprint of your movement. The **spread** (highest gain minus lowest) shows how repeatable you are. A spread of 5 percentage points or less is the target.
- **Form and safety**: grip, order, noise, posture, hygiene and safety, which only the film shows.

Time matters too, but only once the movement is correct. Speed built on a wrong movement just makes the mistake faster.

### The rubric

The course uses one rubric for technique, in [templates/technique-rubric.md](../../templates/technique-rubric.md): ten lines (measuring, free pour, build, stir, shake, strain, muddle, garnish, station reset, safety), each scored 0, 1 or 2. **2** means the standard is met on every rep, **1** mostly, **0** not yet. The total is out of 20 and turned into a percentage. The drill sheet ([labs/module-04/technique-drill-sheet.md](../../labs/module-04/technique-drill-sheet.md)) turns your logged numbers into rubric scores, so the score comes from evidence, not from how the session felt. If you skip the optional free pour line, score out of 18.

Three faults are **safety-critical** in this course (course outline, final assessment): scooping ice with a glass, leaving broken glass in the ice, and unsafe knife handling. Any one of them fails the drill whatever the total; fix it and redo the drill.

### Filming that shows what matters

| Movement | Camera position | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Measuring, build | Side, at bar-top height, close | Jigger full and level, quick full tip, pour order |
| Stir | Side, at the height of the mixing glass, sound on | Spoon against the glass, still wrist, quiet ice |
| Shake | Front, at chest height, a step back | Two hands, small tin towards you, long straight movement, relaxed shoulders |
| Strain | Side, close to the glass | Hawthorne gate closed, fine strainer above the glass, clean finish |
| Muddle | Side, close | Tin held at the base, number of presses, leaves whole |
| Garnish | Above and in front of the glass | Coloured side down, height, placement, tongs or picks |
| Reset | Wide shot of the whole station | Every tool back in its place, surface wiped, bottles capped |

Prop the phone on a stack of books or a cheap phone stand. Film in landscape. Watch at half speed if your phone allows it.

### Comparing with a demonstration

For each movement, read the written demonstration named on the drill sheet (the Difford's Guide technique pages and the Module 4 lessons) or watch a demonstration video from a reputable bar-education source, then compare it with your reps 9 and 10. Look for differences in grip, angle, order and timing, not in style: two good bartenders can shake differently and both be right. Write the single change that would make the biggest difference.

> [!NOTE]
> The rubric standards (±5% measuring, 20–30 s stir, 10–15 s shake, 90 s reset) are this course's practice standards, set in the templates and lessons, checked 2026-10-17. Your self-score is not independently verified; ask a working bartender to watch your film if you can.

## Step by step

### Plan the session

1. Read the drill sheet and decide whether to do everything in one 90-minute session or in two sessions of about 45 minutes (A: measuring, build, stir, shake and strain; B: muddle, garnish, resets, scoring and comparison).
2. Set up the station as in lesson [01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md): tools in fixed places, ice in a clean container with a scoop, a bin, cloths, the scale near the working area.
3. Open the drill log ([labs/module-04/technique-drill-log.csv](../../labs/module-04/technique-drill-log.csv)) in a spreadsheet; the 80 rows for this lesson are already there.
4. Set up the phone for the first movement.

### Run each movement

1. Warm up with two unlogged reps.
2. Start filming. Do ten reps, logging after each one: measured weight, time, pass or fail, a short note.
3. Film reps 9 and 10 at least; film all ten if your phone has space.
4. Reset the station (timed) before the next movement and log the reset.
5. Every 20–30 minutes, stop for two minutes: drink some water, roll your shoulders, dry your hands.

### Score and fix

1. For each movement, count the passes and, for stir and shake, work out the spread of the weight gains.
2. Convert the counts to rubric scores with the "Score" column of the drill sheet.
3. Fill in the rubric and work out the percentage: total ÷ 20 × 100 (or ÷ 18 if you skipped the free pour).
4. Watch reps 9 and 10 of each movement next to the written demonstration and fill in the comparison table on the drill sheet.
5. Pick your **three** weakest movements. For each, write one specific fix ("tip the jigger fully in one movement", not "pour better") and plan a ten-rep re-test within a week.

## Hands-on

> [!CAUTION]
> This drill uses a knife, a peeler, glass and a lot of ice and water. Board on a damp cloth and claw grip for every cut; ice only with a scoop; check glasses for chips; wipe up spills on the floor at once. If a glass breaks near the ice, empty and clean the ice container before continuing (lesson 01.6).

### What you need

- Your home station from lesson 01.2: jigger, Boston shaker or jar, mixing glass or sturdy pint glass, bar spoon, Hawthorne and fine strainer (or tea strainer), muddler, Y-peeler, paring knife, board, damp cloth, ice scoop, a highball glass, a coupe or small wine glass, a rocks glass, tongs or picks.
- About 4 kg of ice cubes (two large bags), 2 l cold water, 600 ml cold black tea, 1 l soda water, 6 limes, 4 lemons or oranges, a small bunch of mint, 150 ml 1:1 sugar syrup.
- A kitchen scale with 1 g steps, a phone stopwatch, a phone and a stand.
- [Drill sheet](../../labs/module-04/technique-drill-sheet.md), [drill log](../../labs/module-04/technique-drill-log.csv), [technique rubric](../../templates/technique-rubric.md), [station checklist](../../templates/station-checklist.md), [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: if you have only one glass of each type, empty and reuse it between reps; tap water replaces tea and soda for every movement except the fizz part of the build.

### Time

90 minutes (or two sessions of 45): 10 to set up, 60 for eight movements at about 7 minutes each including resets, 20 to score, compare and plan fixes.

### Steps

1. Set up the station and the phone. Start the stopwatch for your first setup time if you want to compare with lesson 01.7.
2. **Measuring:** ten 30 ml water pours by jigger into the tared tin. Log grams and seconds.
3. **Build:** ten tea-and-soda highballs as in lesson 04.2. Log the tea weight and build time.
4. **Stir:** ten 25-second stirs of 90 ml water with julep strain, as in lesson 04.3. Log the weight gain.
5. **Shake and strain:** ten 12-second shakes of 90 ml water, each double strained into the coupe, as in lessons 04.4 and 04.5. Log weight gain, opening time and ice chips.
6. **Muddle:** ten muddles of 3 lime wedges with 10 ml syrup, then 4 mint leaves pressed gently, in the small tin. Log presses and whether the leaves stayed whole.
7. **Garnish:** ten twists cut, expressed and placed, as in lesson 04.7. Log time and width.
8. **Reset:** after each movement, reset the station against the station checklist and log the time (target 90 seconds).
9. Optional: run the three dilution presets and compare the model's prediction for a real cocktail with your water results; note why they differ (lesson 04.1).
10. Score, compare and plan fixes as in "Score and fix" above. Write the total, the three fixes and the re-test date in your practice log.

### What you should see

A first full drill typically scores between 55% and 80% (professional judgment). The usual low lines are measuring (short pours from a slow tip), stirring (spread above 5 points on early reps) and garnish (twists over 20 seconds). Shaking and building often score well because they are simpler to repeat. Watch for fatigue: if your accuracy drops in the last movements, that is useful information, since a real shift is long. A re-test of your three weakest movements a week later usually raises each by a point if you practised the specific fix.

### What this practice cannot teach

Water drills cannot tell you whether a real drink tastes right, and they cannot reproduce the pressure of guests waiting; your score shows clean, consistent movements, which is the foundation the recipe modules and the service simulation build on.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I did and logged ten reps of each of the eight movements, with times and measurements.
- [ ] I filmed at least reps 9 and 10 of each movement and compared them with a demonstration.
- [ ] I scored myself with the technique rubric using the drill sheet's evidence rules.
- [ ] I had no safety-critical fault (ice scooped with a glass, broken glass in the ice, unsafe knife use).
- [ ] I wrote three specific fixes and a re-test date in my practice log.

## Common mistakes

- **Scoring by feel.** "My stir felt good" is not evidence. Fix: score from the logged numbers with the drill sheet's rules.
- **Filming from the wrong angle.** You cannot see the spoon or the jigger. Fix: use the camera positions in the table above.
- **Skipping resets.** The station gets messy and the later movements suffer. Fix: reset and time it after every movement.
- **Vague fixes.** "Be faster" changes nothing. Fix: one physical change per weak movement, written as an instruction.
- **Chasing speed first.** Faster wrong movements are still wrong. Fix: accuracy and form first; time comes with repetition.
- **Practising through fatigue.** Accuracy collapses and injuries become likely. Fix: short breaks every 20–30 minutes; split the drill into two sessions if needed.

## Key takeaways

- Measure technique like a trainer: accuracy by weight, consistency by spread, form and safety by film.
- Error % = (measured − target) ÷ target × 100; the course tolerance is ±5%.
- Score with the technique rubric from evidence; 75% is the minimum for the final practical, and any safety-critical fault fails it.
- Compare your film with a demonstration and write one specific fix per weak movement.
- Re-test your three weakest movements within a week; progress comes from targeted repetition.
