# 04.6 · Blending, swizzling and throwing

Most drinks are built, stirred or shaken, but three more methods fill specific jobs. Blending turns a drink and crushed ice into a frozen slush, as in the Piña Colada. Swizzling spins a special stick in a glass packed with crushed ice until the outside frosts over. Throwing pours a drink in a long arc between two vessels to chill it and give it a fine, lively texture, without the cloudiness of a shake. This lesson explains what each method does, when to use it and how to do it safely, and in the hands-on part you throw ten water drinks over a tray, swizzle an alcohol-free swizzle and, if you have a blender, compare two frozen drinks.

## Why it matters

A summer afternoon at a beach bar in Tel Aviv: the blender runs non-stop, and the bartender who adds "a handful" of ice to each Piña Colada serves one drink that is watery and the next that is a solid lump the straw cannot get through. Two tables complain. A few streets away, in a cocktail bar, a guest orders a Manhattan "thrown", and the bartender who has never practised the movement splashes vermouth across the bar. These methods are less common than shaking and stirring, but they are visible, and when they go wrong everyone sees it. A few hours of practice with water makes them safe and repeatable.

## Core ideas

### Blending

**Blending** mixes the drink with crushed ice in an electric blender until the ice is part of the drink, giving a thick, frozen texture. The IBA Piña Colada is blended with ice and poured into a large glass with straws.

- **Ice amount sets everything.** Difford's Guide's frozen recipes typically use a 12 oz scoop (about 350 ml) of crushed ice per drink, and half that for some. Too much ice gives a bland, stiff slush; too little gives a thin, watery drink that separates. **Measure the ice** the same way you measure the liquid: one scoop, the same scoop every time.
- **Crushed, dry ice.** Crushed ice blends evenly. Ice straight from the freezer is colder and drier, so it freezes the drink more and waters it less than wet ice from a well (lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)).
- **Liquid first.** Put the liquids in the jug first, then the ice, so the blade turns freely. Start on low speed, then high, for 15–30 seconds until there are no lumps and the drink rolls in the jug.
- **Cold dulls taste.** Very cold drinks taste less sweet and less aromatic, so frozen recipes often carry more sugar and fruit than their shaken versions (professional judgment; lesson [03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md) on temperature and taste). Follow the frozen spec; do not just blend the shaken one.
- **Speed and noise.** Blending one drink at a time is slow and loud, so many busy bars batch frozen drinks or use a slush machine (professional judgment).

> [!WARNING]
> A blender blade is extremely sharp. Never put a hand, spoon or bar tool into the jug while the motor can run. Unplug or switch off at the base before clearing ice that is stuck, put the lid on before you start, and hold it down while blending. Never blend anything carbonated.

### Swizzling

**Swizzling** is mixing a drink in its own glass, packed with crushed ice, by spinning a stick between your palms. The traditional **swizzle stick** is a forked twig from a Caribbean tree with several short prongs at the bottom; modern ones are metal or plastic with blades set at right angles to the shaft. Difford's Guide's method:

1. Start with a chilled glass, the ingredients in it, and the glass half full of crushed ice.
2. Put the stick's blades into the drink, hold the shaft between the palms of both hands, and spin it fast by sliding your hands back and forth.
3. Add more crushed ice and keep swizzling until the outside of the glass frosts.

Swizzling mixes, aerates and dilutes the drink and chills the glass so hard that it frosts. It only works with **crushed** ice: cubes block the blades. A **bar spoon** works as a stand-in: spin its shaft between your palms the same way. Difford's Queen's Park Swizzle (rum, lime, sugar, mint and bitters, swizzled in a collins glass) is the classic example; the IBA Mint Julep uses a related idea, cracked ice stirred "until the cup frosts". Spinning a spoon or stick up and down in crushed ice without the palm-roll is often called **churning**.

### Throwing

**Throwing** means pouring a drink from one vessel held high to another held low, through a strainer, and back again, several times. Difford's Guide explains why bartenders do it:

- **Aeration without cloudiness.** Throwing draws in air and creates very fine bubbles. Stirring makes none, shaking makes larger ones. The thrown drink feels lighter and more open than a stirred one, but stays fairly clear.
- **Aroma.** It seems to release aromas, especially in drinks built on wine-based ingredients like vermouth and sherry. Difford's suggests comparing a stirred and a thrown Manhattan to taste the difference.
- **Dilution.** Difford's aims for about 30–35% dilution, usually 4–5 throws; with large, very cold cubes it can take six or seven.

In the course's educational model, a drink that starts at about 33% ABV gains about 25% water after 5 seconds of throwing, about 35% after 8 seconds and about 39% after 10 seconds, so 4–5 throws of about 2 seconds each land close to Difford's target.

**The movement.** Use two large tins (they pour more cleanly than a mixing glass). The drink and ice go in the top tin, about two-thirds full of ice, with a julep or Hawthorne strainer held in it at about 45°. Start with **both tins up high together**, then let the catching tin drop down as you pour, so the stream stretches into a long arc. Watch the catching tin, not the top one, and tilt it so the stream hits its inside wall. At the end, as the stream breaks into drops, straighten the catching tin. Pour back into the top tin and repeat. Difford's warns against starting with the tins at mid-height and trying to pull them apart: start high, let the lower hand fall. Practise with water until you are confident.

### Choosing among the three

| Method | Best for | Ice | Served in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blend | Frozen, creamy or fruity drinks (Piña Colada, frozen Daiquiri) | Crushed, measured scoop | Large glass, straw |
| Swizzle / churn | Rum swizzles, juleps, tall crushed-ice drinks | Crushed, packed | The glass it was swizzled in |
| Throw | Stirred-style drinks with vermouth or sherry; a lighter Martini or Manhattan; Bloody Mary ("rolling", gentler) | Cubes in the top tin, strained | Chilled coupe or on fresh ice |

## Step by step

### Blend a frozen drink

1. Chill the serving glass.
2. Measure the liquids into the blender jug.
3. Add one level scoop of crushed ice (measure your scoop in ml once, then always use the same one).
4. Put the lid on and hold it down. Blend on low for 3–5 seconds, then high for 10–20 seconds, until the drink rolls smoothly with no lumps.
5. Switch off and wait for the blade to stop before lifting the jug.
6. Pour into the glass, garnish, add a straw, serve. Rinse the jug at once; sugar sets as it dries.

### Swizzle a drink

1. Chill the glass (a collins or highball).
2. Measure the ingredients into the glass.
3. Half-fill the glass with crushed ice.
4. Put the swizzle stick or bar spoon in, blades at the bottom, shaft between your flat palms.
5. Rub your palms back and forth quickly so the stick spins, moving it slowly up and down through the drink.
6. Top with more crushed ice to a dome and swizzle briefly again until the outside of the glass frosts.
7. Garnish and serve with a straw.

### Throw a drink

1. Measure the drink into a large tin. Add cubes to about two-thirds.
2. Fit a julep or Hawthorne strainer into that tin at about 45° and hold it in place with your index finger. This is the top tin, in your dominant hand.
3. Hold the empty catching tin in your other hand, pinched near its rim so it can pivot.
4. Start with both tins together at about chin height.
5. Begin pouring and let the catching tin drop smoothly towards waist height; keep your eyes on the catching tin.
6. As the pour ends and the stream turns to drops, raise the catching tin back up to meet the top tin.
7. Pour the liquid back into the ice tin. That is one throw. Do 4–5 throws.
8. Strain into a chilled glass.

## Hands-on

> [!CAUTION]
> Practise throwing with water over a sink or a large tray, with nothing breakable nearby, and wipe the floor at once: water and ice on a hard floor make it slippery. If you use a blender, follow the warning above: lid on, hands and tools out of the jug, switch off before clearing.

### What you need

- Two large shaker tins (or two large plastic jugs), a julep or Hawthorne strainer, a jigger.
- A tray or baking sheet, a kitchen scale with 1 g steps, a towel, about 2 kg of ice cubes.
- For the swizzle: a collins or highball glass, a swizzle stick or bar spoon, about 400 g crushed ice (crush cubes in a clean towel with a rolling pin, lesson 01.6), 25 ml lime juice, 20 ml 1:1 syrup, 60 ml cold tea, a few mint leaves.
- Optional, for blending: a blender, 120 ml pineapple juice, 60 ml coconut milk or cream, 20 ml lime juice, 20 ml 1:1 syrup, and a measuring cup for crushed ice.
- The drill log: [labs/module-04/technique-drill-log.csv](../../labs/module-04/technique-drill-log.csv) and your practice log ([template](../../templates/practice-log.md)).
- Low-cost alternative: two large plastic jugs instead of tins for throwing (they make spills easier to see); a bar spoon instead of a swizzle stick.

### Time

60 minutes: 25 for throwing, 10 for the swizzle, 15 for the optional blending comparison, 10 to clean up and log.

### Steps

1. **Throwing, reps 1–10.** Weigh the dry tray and write it down. Stand over the tray. For each rep: put 90 ml water and ice in the top tin, do 5 throws, strain into a glass. After every rep, weigh the tray (water spilled on it is your spill) and dry it.
2. Write the spill in grams for each rep. Target by rep 10: 5 g or less. Note how high you managed to drop the catching tin without spilling.
3. Film reps 9 and 10. Check: both tins start high, the lower hand falls, eyes on the catching tin, the stream hits the inside wall.
4. Run the throw preset of the dilution simulation and compare its dilution at 5, 8 and 10 seconds with the 30–35% Difford's aims for.
5. **Swizzle.** Build the alcohol-free swizzle in the glass (lime, syrup, tea, mint pressed lightly), half-fill with crushed ice, swizzle, top with ice, swizzle again. Time how long it takes for the outside of the glass to frost.
6. **Optional blending.** Make the frozen drink twice with the same liquids: once with 175 ml crushed ice, once with 350 ml. Compare thickness, how easily a straw goes in, and (optional small sip) sweetness.
7. Add ten rows to the drill log for the throwing reps (movement "throw", spill in grams in the measured column) and log spill, frost time and the blending comparison in your practice log.

### What you should see

First throws usually spill 15–30 g, mostly at the start and the end of each pour; by the tenth rep most people are under 5 g with a drop of 20–30 cm (professional judgment). Spills at the start mean you pulled the tins apart instead of letting the lower one fall; spills at the end mean you did not straighten the catching tin as the stream broke. The swizzle glass usually frosts in 20–40 seconds of steady swizzling with dry crushed ice; wet ice frosts it more slowly. In the blending comparison, the double-ice version is stiffer, paler and noticeably less sweet; the single-scoop version is pourable and tastes fuller.

### What this practice cannot teach

Water shows your control, but only a real vermouth-based drink shows the aroma and texture difference that makes throwing worth doing, and only real fruit, cream and spirits show how a frozen spec balances; taste these later, optionally and in small amounts.

- [ ] My spill was 5 g or less on the last reps, or I logged the fix and will repeat.

- [ ] If I have a blender, I compared two ice amounts and noted texture and taste.

## Common mistakes

- **Guessing the ice in the blender.** Every drink comes out different. Fix: one measured scoop per drink.
- **Reaching into a blender jug.** Serious cuts. Fix: switch off at the base and wait for the blade to stop; use the jug's own tamper only if it is designed for it.
- **Swizzling with cubes.** The stick jams and nothing mixes. Fix: crushed ice only, packed in two stages.
- **Starting a throw at mid-height.** The stream breaks and splashes. Fix: start with both tins high and let the lower hand fall.
- **Watching the top tin.** You miss the catching tin. Fix: eyes on the tin that is catching.
- **Throwing too many times.** The drink over-dilutes and warms. Fix: 4–5 throws, then strain.

## Key takeaways

- Blend with a measured scoop of crushed ice; too much ice makes a bland slush, too little a watery one.
- Blender blades are dangerous: lid on, hands out, switch off before clearing.
- Swizzle by spinning a stick between your palms in crushed ice until the glass frosts.
- Throwing aerates finely and lifts aroma, best with vermouth or sherry; 4–5 throws aim at about 30–35% dilution.
- Start throws with both vessels high, let the catching hand drop, and watch the catching vessel.
