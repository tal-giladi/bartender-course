# Instructor notes — 04.5 Straining, muddling and layering

- **Objective:** learner picks the right strainer and double strains when needed, muddles herbs gently and citrus firmly in a safe vessel, and layers four liquids by density.
- **Likely confusion:** when double straining is needed. Rule of thumb in the lesson: served up, muddled, or egg/aquafaba → double strain; onto fresh ice or crushed ice → usually not.
- **Misconception:** "muddle harder for more flavour". For herbs, harder means bitter and grassy; only citrus and sugar need firm pressure.
- **Expected hands-on result:** single-strained smash shows chips and flecks, double-strained is clean; layering 2–3 layers on the first try and 4 by the third. The juice/tea boundary (about 1.04 vs 1.00 g/ml) is the hard one.
- **Safety:** muddling in thin glass is a real injury risk (Difford's reports a bad cut). Knife use for wedges follows 01.4. No flaming layered drinks: the course excludes fire tricks.
- **Extension:** test the layering order of two real liqueurs from the learner's shelf (optional, small amounts, no tasting needed) by reading sugar and ABV on the labels first, then pouring.
