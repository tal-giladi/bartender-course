# Instructor notes — 04.3 Stirring

- **Objective:** learner stirs quietly with the fingers for a fixed 20–30 s, strains cleanly into a chilled glass and keeps rep-to-rep weight gain within 5 percentage points.
- **Likely confusion:** the course standard (20–30 s, also in `templates/technique-rubric.md`) versus Difford's 30–45 s and Arnold's ~45 s. Explain that ice temperature and hardness change the right time; the model shows diminishing returns after ~30 s.
- **Misconception:** "the longer you stir, the better". After ~30 s each extra 15 s adds only a few percent of water in the model, and over-stirring with wet ice gives a thin drink.
- **Numbers:** from `bar-math.js` `dilutionFraction('stir', 0.32, t)`: 10 s 0.25, 20 s 0.37, 30 s 0.44, 45 s 0.48, plateau 0.51. Plain water (0% ABV) at 25 s: 0.18 in the model; learners usually weigh 12–16% because of holdback on the ice.
- **Expected hands-on result:** 40–60 turns in 25 s; spread falling from 6–10 points to 3–5 points over ten reps; film shows wrist movement on early reps.
- **Extension:** stir two mixing glasses at once with two spoons (common in busy cocktail bars), then compare the spread with single stirs.
