# 02.8 · Beer, cider, wine and alcohol-free drinks at a glance

On most shifts you sell more beer and wine than cocktails, and more and more guests ask for something without alcohol. This lesson is a quick map of those drinks: the two big families of beer, cider, how wines get their colour and bubbles, Israel's wine regions, and how to read an alcohol-free label honestly. Module 7 goes deeper into pouring, opening and recommending; here you learn to recognise and classify. You finish with a shelf survey in a real shop.

## Why it matters

A guest asks for "a light beer, not bitter". Another asks if the rosé is sweet. A pregnant guest asks whether the alcohol-free beer is "really zero". The first two need you to know styles; the third needs you to read the label exactly and not guess. These questions come every shift, and quick, correct answers keep the bar moving.

## Core ideas

### Beer: ales and lagers

Beer (בירה, *bira*) is fermented from malted grain (usually barley), flavoured with hops (a plant that adds bitterness and aroma). The yeast decides the family:

| Family | Yeast and fermentation | Typical character | Common styles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lager | Bottom-fermenting yeast, cool (about 7–13 °C), then cold storage | Clean, crisp, smooth | Pale lager (the everyday "beer"), pilsner, dark lager |
| Ale | Top-fermenting yeast, warmer (about 15–21 °C) | Fruitier, often fuller | Pale ale, IPA, wheat beer, stout, porter |

Five styles to know by name:

- **Pale lager / pilsner**: light gold, crisp, mild bitterness. Most draught beer (בירה מהחבית, *bira mehachavit*) in Israeli bars is in this family (professional judgment).
- **Wheat beer**: hazy, light, notes of banana, clove or citrus; low bitterness.
- **IPA (India pale ale)**: strong hop aroma (citrus, pine, tropical fruit) and clear bitterness; often stronger than lager.
- **Stout and porter**: dark, roasted, coffee and chocolate notes; not necessarily strong.
- **Sour beers**: tart, sometimes fruity; a niche but growing style (professional judgment).

Most everyday beers are about 4–6% ABV; strong styles can go well beyond (professional judgment; read the label). Serve lagers colder than ales.

### Cider

Cider (סיידר, *sayder*) is fermented apple juice, usually about 4–8% ABV (professional judgment), from dry to sweet. Pear cider is sometimes called perry. In Hebrew, "סיידר" also means sweet apple juice, so check whether a guest wants the alcoholic one.

### Wine: colour, sweetness and bubbles

Wine (יין, *yayin*) is fermented grape juice, usually about 11–15% ABV. The juice of almost all grapes is clear; colour comes from the skins.

| Type | Hebrew (transliteration) | How it is made |
|---|---|---|
| Red | יין אדום (*yayin adom*) | Red grapes fermented with their skins |
| White | יין לבן (*yayin lavan*) | Juice pressed off the skins before fermentation |
| Rosé | רוזה (*roze*) | Red grapes with short skin contact |
| Orange (skin-contact white) | יין כתום (*yayin katom*) | White grapes fermented with their skins |
| Sparkling | יין מבעבע (*yayin meva'bea*) | A second fermentation traps CO2 |

**Sweetness**: most wines are dry (no noticeable sugar). Rosé ranges from dry to sweet; ask or check the list. On sparkling wine labels, "brut" is drier than "extra dry", which surprises many guests.

**Bubbles**: in the **traditional method** (Champagne and many quality sparkling wines), still wine is bottled with a little sugar and yeast and ferments a second time in the bottle. In the **tank method** (most Prosecco), the second fermentation happens in a sealed pressure tank before bottling; it keeps fresh, fruity flavours.

Fortified and aromatised wines (port, sherry, vermouth) were covered in lesson 02.7.

### Israeli wine regions

For decades Israel used five registered wine regions, set in the 1960s and registered abroad: **Galilee** (גליל, including the Golan Heights as a sub-region), **Shomron** (שומרון), **Samson** (שמשון, the coastal plain and Judean lowlands), **Judean Hills** (הרי יהודה) and **Negev** (נגב). In September 2026 a new official map with eight regions and 18 sub-regions was launched; older bottles and lists will keep the five names for years. [Lesson 07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md) explains the new map, grapes and Israeli wineries.

### Alcohol-free and low-alcohol drinks

| Label wording | What it usually means | What to tell a guest |
|---|---|---|
| "0.0%" | No measurable alcohol | Safe for a guest avoiding alcohol completely |
| "Alcohol-free" / "נטול אלכוהול" with a number like 0.3% or 0.5% | A small amount of alcohol | Tell the guest the exact % and let them decide |
| "Low alcohol" / "light" | Reduced but real alcohol | Not suitable for a guest avoiding alcohol |

Rules differ by country. In the US, for example, beer may be labelled "non-alcoholic" only with the statement "contains less than 0.5% alcohol by volume", and "alcohol free" only if it contains no alcohol. Israeli labels show the alcohol content; always read the number rather than relying on the words. Module 9 covers guests who avoid alcohol and Module 7 covers zero-proof drinks.

## Step by step

### How to answer "something light" quickly

1. Ask: "Beer, wine, or something without alcohol?"
2. Beer → a pale lager or wheat beer. Wine → a crisp white or a dry rosé. No alcohol → a 0.0% option.
3. Name what you are offering and why in one line: "Our draught lager is crisp and not bitter."

### How to read a beer or wine label in 15 seconds

1. Category and style (lager, IPA; red, white, sparkling).
2. ABV (% vol).
3. Sweetness word on sparkling (brut, extra dry, demi-sec).
4. For imports in Israel: the Hebrew sticker with the importer's details (lesson 02.9).

## Hands-on

A shelf survey: in a supermarket or drinks shop, record 20 beers, ciders, wines and alcohol-free drinks by category, ABV and label details. You look and write; you do not need to buy anything.

### What you need

- Access to a supermarket or a drinks shop (any size), your phone for notes or photos of labels (ask the shop if photos are okay).
- The survey sheet [`labs/module-02/shelf-survey.csv`](../../labs/module-02/shelf-survey.csv) (open it in a spreadsheet or copy the columns into a notebook).
- Low-cost alternative: no shop nearby? Use an online Israeli supermarket or wine shop that shows product labels and ABV, and note "online" in the sheet.

### Time

45 minutes: 30 in the shop, 15 to tidy and analyse the sheet.

### Steps

1. Pick 20 products: at least 6 beers (include one lager, one ale such as an IPA or wheat beer, and one dark beer), 2 ciders if available, 8 wines (red, white, rosé, sparkling) and 4 alcohol-free or low-alcohol drinks.
2. For each, fill in: product type, style, ABV, country of origin, whether there is a Hebrew sticker or Hebrew label, any alcohol-free claim and its exact %, and for sparkling wine the sweetness word.
3. Note the Hebrew warning text on each alcoholic product and which of two wordings it uses (you will learn why in lesson 02.9).
4. At home, sort the sheet by ABV. Mark the lowest and highest in each group.
5. Answer three questions in your notes: Which alcohol-free product would you offer a guest who avoids all alcohol? Which beer is closest to "light, not bitter"? Which sparkling wine is driest?

### What you should see

Most beers cluster around 4–6% ABV, with IPAs and some dark or Belgian-style beers higher. Wines mostly sit at 11–15%. Alcohol-free products split into "0.0%" and products with a small number such as 0.3–0.5%; only the 0.0% ones suit a guest who avoids all alcohol. Imported products carry a Hebrew sticker with the importer's details. You may find "brut" and "extra dry" side by side and notice that the "extra dry" is the sweeter one.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying "lager means light".** Lagers can be dark and strong; "light" is about taste or calories. Fix: ask what the guest means.
- **Assuming "alcohol-free" means zero.** Some contain up to about 0.5%. Fix: read the exact % and tell guests who avoid alcohol completely.
- **Thinking "extra dry" is the driest sparkling wine.** Brut is drier. Fix: learn the order brut → extra dry → dry → demi-sec (driest to sweeter).
- **Assuming all rosé is sweet.** Many are dry. Fix: check the list or the label.
- **Confusing sweet apple juice with cider.** In Hebrew both can be "סיידר". Fix: ask "with alcohol or without?"

## Key takeaways

- Lagers use bottom-fermenting yeast at cool temperatures and taste clean and crisp; ales ferment warmer and taste fruitier.
- Wine colour comes from grape skins: red with skins, white without, rosé briefly, orange from white grapes with skins.
- Sparkling wine gets bubbles from a second fermentation: in the bottle (traditional method) or in a tank (most Prosecco).
- Israel's five long-standing wine regions are Galilee (with the Golan), Shomron, Samson, Judean Hills and Negev; a new eight-region map arrived in 2026 ([lesson 07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)).
- Read the exact ABV on alcohol-free products; only 0.0% suits a guest who avoids all alcohol.
