# Instructor notes — 07.1 Beer styles, temperatures and glassware

- **Objective:** learner sets a serving temperature and glass by style and strength, and checks glasses for beer-clean.
- **Likely confusion:** "colder is always better"; the difference between stored temperature (1–3 °C in a cold room) and serving temperature; the many names for tall slim glasses (pilsner, flute, vase, weizen).
- **Misconception:** a glass that looks clean is beer-clean. Rinse aid and fat are invisible; the sheeting and salt tests show them.
- **Expected hands-on result:** dishwasher glasses often fail the sheeting test and pass after hand-washing; fridge shelves around 3–7 °C, door warmer. Matching is open-ended: any reason based on aroma, foam, size or temperature is acceptable.
- **Extension:** ask the learner to write a one-line "glass and temperature" card for five beers on a real Israeli bar's tap list.
- **Note:** temperatures are converted from a US °F table and rounded; accept ±1 °C.
