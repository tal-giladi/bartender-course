# 07.3 · Wine basics: major grapes, styles and Israeli wine

Most guests do not ask for a grape or a region. They ask for "a dry white", "something not too heavy" or "the Israeli one". To answer, you need a small set of words for how wine tastes, the eight grape varieties that cover most wine lists, the facts on a label, and enough about Israeli wine to recommend it honestly. This lesson gives you all four, plus what kosher and mevushal mean for service in Israel. Israel launched a new official wine-region map in September 2026, so this lesson also explains what changed. You finish by analysing six real wine labels.

## Why it matters

A couple at the bar asks for "a red, but not heavy, something to go with fish". You have a Cabernet Sauvignon, a Merlot and a Pinot Noir by the glass. Another guest asks whether the house white is kosher and mevushal because an observant friend is joining. A tourist wants "something from the Golan". Each answer takes ten seconds if you know the basics, and each wrong answer costs trust.

## Core ideas

### Five words for how a wine tastes

You met sweet, sour, bitter and texture in [lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md). Wine uses the same ideas with its own words:

| Word | What it means | Guest language |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweetness** | sugar left after fermentation | dry (no sugar you notice), off-dry, sweet |
| **Acidity** | sourness; makes the mouth water | fresh, crisp, zesty / soft, round |
| **Tannin** | the drying, gripping feel from grape skins and seeds, mostly in red wine | soft / firm, grippy |
| **Body** | how heavy the wine feels; more alcohol and sugar mean more body | light, medium, full |
| **Oak** | flavour from ageing in oak barrels | toasty, vanilla, smoky, creamy |

Most wines are dry. A guest who says "dry" often means "not sweet and not sour"; ask "crisp and fresh, or soft and round?" (professional judgment).

### The eight principal grapes

The WSET (Wine & Spirit Education Trust) Level 2 wine course, a common entry-level qualification for wine service, names eight principal grape varieties (specification issue of April 2026). The typical styles below are general descriptions (professional judgment); climate, ripeness and winemaking change them, which is exactly what the WSET course teaches next.

| Grape | Colour | Typical style | Guest words |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cabernet Sauvignon | red | full body, firm tannin, high acidity; blackcurrant, often oak | "big, structured red" |
| Merlot | red | medium to full, softer tannin; plum, cherry | "smooth, round red" |
| Syrah / Shiraz | red | full, deep colour; dark fruit, pepper, spice | "spicy, rich red" |
| Pinot Noir | red | light to medium, low tannin, fresh; red cherry, strawberry | "light, elegant red" |
| Chardonnay | white | from crisp and unoaked to full and creamy with oak | ask: "fresh or creamy?" |
| Sauvignon Blanc | white | light to medium, high acidity; citrus, green notes, passion fruit | "zesty, aromatic white" |
| Riesling | white | high acidity, from dry to sweet; lime, green apple, flowers | "check the label for sweetness" |
| Pinot Grigio / Pinot Gris | white | light and neutral (Grigio) to rounder (Gris) | "easy, light white" |

Blends are common: a "Bordeaux blend" is Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot with other grapes. Rosé is usually made from red grapes with short skin contact (lesson 02.8) and is mostly dry. Sparkling wines use mainly Chardonnay and Pinot Noir (Champagne, traditional method) or Glera (Prosecco, tank method), as the WSET specification lists.

### Reading a wine label

A wine label (and the Hebrew importer sticker on imports, [lesson 02.9](../module-02/lesson-09.md)) tells you:

1. **Producer** (the winery).
2. **Grape** (varietal wine) or a blend name.
3. **Region** of origin, if stated.
4. **Vintage**: the harvest year. Most everyday white and rosé is best young (professional judgment).
5. **ABV** (% vol): a quick clue to body. 12% is usually lighter than 14.5%.
6. **Sweetness terms** on sparkling: brut is drier than extra dry (lesson 02.8).
7. **Kashrut** (כשרות) mark and the word **mevushal** (מבושל) if it applies.
8. Allergen notice: wine usually contains sulphites (lesson 06.6 and Module 9).

### Israel's wine regions: the 2026 map

Until 2026, Israeli labels and export registrations used five regions set decades ago: Galilee (with the Golan as a sub-region), Shomron, Samson, Judean Hills and Negev ([lesson 02.8](../module-02/lesson-08.md)). In September 2026 the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security, together with the Ministry of Health, launched Israel's first official wine-region map. According to the wine writer Adam Montefiore (The Jerusalem Post, 10 October 2026) and the Hebrew news site ice.co.il (6 September 2026), it has **eight regions** and **18 sub-regions**:

| Region | Hebrew (transliteration) | Example sub-regions |
|---|---|---|
| Golan | הגולן (*haGolan*) | Upper Golan, Lower Golan |
| Galilee | הגליל (*haGalil*) | Upper Galilee East and West, Lower Galilee |
| Valleys | העמקים (*ha'Amakim*) | Jezreel Valley |
| Carmel | הכרמל (*haCarmel*) | Nadiv Valley, Menashe Hills |
| Foothills | מורדות ההר (*mordot hahar*) | Judean Foothills |
| Coastal Plain | מישור החוף (*mishor hachof*) | — |
| Central Mountains | ההר המרכזי (*hahar hamerkazi*) | Judean Hills, Samarian Mountains |
| Negev | הנגב (*haNegev*) | Yatir, Arad Valley, Negev Highlands |

Three facts matter for service. Naming a region on the label is optional for wines sold in Israel. If a winery names one, at least 85% of the grapes must come from it. The map describes origin, not quality: a region name is not a ranking. Older bottles and lists will still show the old five names for years (professional judgment).

### Three Israeli wineries, one fact each

- **Carmel**: describes itself as Israel's first commercial winery, making wine since the late 19th century, with a historic winery in Zichron Ya'akov and about 108 grower families today.
- **Golan Heights Winery**: founded in 1983 by eight Golan communities; its first wine was a 1983 Yarden Sauvignon Blanc. Its brands are Yarden, Gamla, Golan and Mount Hermon, and it lists vineyards from about 400 m to about 1,100 m, mostly on volcanic basalt soils.
- **Recanati**: founded in 2000, based in the Upper Galilee. With researchers led by Dr. Elyashiv Drori of Ariel University, it released the first commercial wine from **Marawi** (also called Hamdani), a white grape local to the region, in 2015 (JTA). Similar local grapes are now made into wine by other producers too, including Palestinian ones such as Cremisan (press, 2015–2019; professional judgment on current availability).

The international grapes in the table are all grown in Israel: the Golan Heights Winery's website alone lists Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc and Riesling among others.

### Kosher and mevushal

Many Israeli wines are kosher (professional judgment; check the label). For **kosher** wine, the OU (a major kosher certifier) explains that from the crushing of the grapes onwards it must be handled only by Jews, under a supervisor (mashgiach). **Mevushal** (מבושל, "cooked") wine has been heat-treated; OU and Chabad explain that it stays kosher even when a non-Jew opens and pours it. Chabad adds that an opened non-mevushal kosher bottle touched by a non-Jew is no longer acceptable to an observant drinker, which is why kosher events and many restaurants use mevushal wine.

For a bartender this means (professional judgment): if a guest asks for kosher wine, show the bottle and the certification; if they ask whether it is mevushal, read the label rather than guess; if it is not mevushal and the guest is strict, bring the bottle sealed and ask how they would like it opened and poured. Fine non-mevushal wines are common, because heating can affect taste (OU).

## Step by step

### Answering "what wine do you recommend?"

1. Ask colour or occasion: "Red, white, rosé or bubbles?"
2. Ask one style question: white → "crisp and fresh, or rounder and creamy?"; red → "light and smooth, or big and structured?"
3. Match to a grape from the table and the bar's list.
4. Offer two choices with one guest-language line each: "Our Sauvignon Blanc is zesty and citrusy; the Chardonnay is softer with a little oak."
5. Offer a small taste if house policy allows (professional judgment).

### Reading a label in 20 seconds

1. Producer and wine name.
2. Grape or blend.
3. Region (and, on Israeli wines from 2026, one of the eight new region names or "Israel").
4. Vintage.
5. ABV.
6. Kosher certification; the word mevushal if present.

## Hands-on

A label analysis of six real wines and a grape-to-guest matching exercise. You look and write; buying or tasting is optional.

> [!WARNING]
> Tasting is optional. If you taste, you must be 18+, take a small sip and spit, keep the whole session under one standard drink, and never taste before driving.

### What you need

- Access to a supermarket, wine shop or an Israeli online wine shop that shows labels (front and back) and ABV.
- The sheet [`labs/module-07/wine-label-analysis.csv`](../../labs/module-07/wine-label-analysis.csv).
- The cards [`labs/module-07/grape-guest-cards.md`](../../labs/module-07/grape-guest-cards.md) and, after you finish, [`labs/module-07/grape-guest-cards-solution.md`](../../labs/module-07/grape-guest-cards-solution.md).
- Optional: [`templates/tasting-notes.md`](../../templates/tasting-notes.md) if you taste.
- Low-cost alternative: everything can be done online with producer websites and shop listings.

### Time

50 minutes: 30 for the labels, 20 for the cards.

### Steps

1. Choose six wines: at least three Israeli (one red, one white, one rosé or sparkling) and at least three made from grapes in the table of eight.
2. For each, fill the CSV: producer, wine name, grape(s), region as printed, vintage, ABV, kosher yes/no, mevushal yes/no, sweetness term if sparkling, and your one-line guest description.
3. For each Israeli wine, check whether the label uses an old region name (for example "Galilee", "Judean Hills", "Samson") or one of the eight new ones. Note it.
4. Sort the six wines from lightest to fullest using grape and ABV. Write your reasoning in one line each.
5. Do the grape-to-guest cards: twelve guest requests, each to be matched to one of the eight grapes with a reason. Then compare with the solution.
6. Optional: taste one wine you analysed and fill in the tasting notes template (sweetness, acidity, tannin, body, oak).

### What you should see

Most Israeli wines you find will show a kosher certification; some, but fewer, say mevushal. Most labels made before late 2026 use the old region names or a broad name such as "Galilee" or simply "Israel". ABV on still wines usually falls between about 12% and 15%; the higher ones are usually the fuller reds. Your lightest-to-fullest order will usually put Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc or Riesling first and Cabernet Sauvignon or Syrah last.

### What this practice cannot teach

Labels and descriptions tell you what a wine should taste like, not what this bottle tastes like today; only regular, small, careful tasting builds that memory, and only a real list teaches you what your bar actually pours.

## Common mistakes

- **Guessing whether a wine is mevushal.** An observant guest relies on your answer. Fix: read the label or ask the manager; never guess.
- **Calling every red "dry and heavy".** Pinot Noir is light; Merlot is soft. Fix: use the body and tannin words.
- **Assuming "dry" means "not sour".** Many guests mix them up. Fix: ask "crisp or round?"
- **Teaching guests the old five regions as the current system.** Fix: since September 2026 the official map has eight regions; older labels still show the old names.
- **Treating a region name as a quality grade.** The 2026 map describes origin, not quality. Fix: describe style, not rank.

## Key takeaways

- Describe wine with five words: sweetness, acidity, tannin, body, oak.
- Eight grapes cover most lists: Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling, Pinot Grigio/Gris.
- A label gives producer, grape, region, vintage, ABV and kosher or mevushal marks: read, do not guess.
- Israel's 2026 official map has eight wine regions and 18 sub-regions; naming one needs 85% of the grapes from it.
- Mevushal wine can be opened and poured by any staff member; for a strict guest and a non-mevushal bottle, bring it sealed and ask.
