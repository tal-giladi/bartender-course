# Instructor notes — 03.3 Taste and balance

- **Objective:** learner separates taste, aroma and texture, predicts sugar/acid/salt/cold effects, and finds a personal balance point with a one-variable test.
- **Likely confusion:** "taste" used for everything. Insist on taste (tongue) vs aroma (nose, mostly retronasal) vs texture (mouthfeel).
- **Misconception:** "bitterness is fixed with sugar". Salt (saline drops) suppresses bitterness selectively (Breslin & Beauchamp 1997); sugar only masks and adds cloying sweetness.
- **Expected hands-on result:** favourite in the syrup ladder usually 15–20 ml per 20 ml lemon in 100 ml water; salted tea less bitter, not salty; room-temperature sample tastes sweeter than iced; peel adds aroma not sourness.
- **Extension:** repeat the syrup ladder with lime instead of lemon, or with soda water instead of still water, and compare balance points (previews 03.6: carbonation reads as more sour).
- **Safety:** no alcohol; knife caution links to 01.4. Saline is 20% by weight, used by the drop.
