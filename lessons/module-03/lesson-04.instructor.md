# Instructor notes — 03.4 Syrups and juices

- **Objective:** learner makes 1:1 and 2:1 by weight, knows their Brix and density, swaps them (15 ml 1:1 ≈ 10 ml 2:1), measures juice yield and applies label/discard rules.
- **Likely confusion:** "2:1 is twice as sweet". By weight it is about 1.4× per ml (Difford's, by volume, 1.35×), because dissolved sugar takes up volume.
- **Misconception:** "fresher juice is always better". Arnold's blind tasting favoured about 4-hour-old lime juice; beyond the shift, aroma loss and food safety argue for discarding.
- **Expected hands-on result:** 1:1 ≈ 405 ml from 500 g (1.23 g/ml); 2:1 ≈ 225 ml from 300 g (1.33 g/ml); swap cups hard to tell apart; yields vary (record them for Module 10).
- **Extension:** make honey syrup (3 parts honey to 1 part warm water by weight) and find how much replaces 15 ml of 1:1 by tasting.
- **Sources caveat:** no official shelf-life figure for bar syrups was found; the 1–2 week (1:1) rule is labelled professional judgment. Module 9 should add any Israeli Ministry of Health guidance.
- **Safety:** hot syrup warning, knife caution, FDA untreated-juice guidance.
