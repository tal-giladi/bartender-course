# Instructor notes — 03.1 Units, ratios and scaling a recipe

- **Objective:** learner converts units (oz × 30, cl × 10, barspoon 5, dash 0.8), turns parts into ml, scales by N, and adds batch water only when servings are not ice-mixed.
- **Likely confusion:** "parts" read as ounces. Parts are a ratio; they become ounces only if one part is set to 1 oz.
- **Misconception:** "every batch needs 25% water". Batch water depends on the drink and on whether each serve will still meet ice; Arnold rejects a single figure. 40% in the lesson is an example; 03.5 replaces it with measured ranges.
- **Expected hands-on result:** worksheet 9–10/10 after correction; 4-serve batch reads 410–430 ml against 420 ml expected.
- **Extension:** batch 12 practice sours, add 20% water, chill, and compare with a freshly shaken serve by taste and temperature (previews 03.5 and Module 8 batching).
- **Consistency:** numbers match `simulations/common/bar-math.js` (`partsToMl`, `scale`, `batch` with water = base × dilution, `barOz` nearest ¼ oz on 30 ml).
