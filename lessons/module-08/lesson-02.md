# 08.2 · Mise en place: prep, storage and shelf life

Mise en place is French for "put in place": everything a shift needs, prepared, measured, labelled and placed where your hand expects it before the first ticket arrives. In lesson 03.4 you made syrup and juice and in lesson 04.7 you cut garnishes. This lesson turns those skills into a plan: how much to prep for a given night, in what order, how to store each item, and how long it may be kept. In the hands-on part you build a prep list from a forecast and then prep a small mise en place at home and check how it holds up.

## Why it matters

Friday, 21:30. The bar is three deep and the lime juice bottle sputters empty. Someone has to stop making drinks, wash limes, cut and press them for ten minutes while tickets pile up and the Mojitos wait. Or the opposite: two litres of juice were squeezed "to be safe" and most of it goes down the sink at close, with the cost of forty limes. Good mise en place means the right amount, made at the right time, kept the right way. It is the cheapest speed you will ever get, because every minute of prep before service saves several minutes during it.

## Core ideas

### What counts as mise en place

**Mise en place** (in Israeli kitchens and bars often just "mizanplas", מיזנפלס, or "the prep", הכנות, *hachanot*) covers:

- **Liquids you make:** citrus juice, syrups, any house mixes or batches (lesson 08.3).
- **Garnish:** wheels, wedges, twists, herbs, cherries, coffee beans, salt or sugar for rims ([lesson 04.7](../module-04/lesson-07.md)).
- **Bought products ready to use:** opened soda and tonic in the fridge, ginger beer, egg white or aquafaba, cream.
- **Equipment:** clean tins, jiggers, strainers, enough ice and enough clean glasses.

### The prep list: from forecast to amounts

A **prep list** says what to make and how much. It starts from a **forecast**: how many of each drink the bar expects to sell, usually based on the same night in previous weeks (the POS keeps these numbers). The calculation is the same one you used for scaling in [lesson 03.1](../module-03/lesson-01.md):

$$\text{amount needed} = \sum (\text{drinks forecast} \times \text{ml per drink}) \times (1 + \text{buffer})$$

The **buffer** covers spills, remakes and a busier night than forecast. This course uses **10%** as a starting point (professional judgment); a bar adjusts it once it sees what is left at close. Then turn juice into fruit with your measured **yield** from lesson 03.4:

$$\text{fruit needed} = \frac{\text{juice needed (ml)}}{\text{ml of juice per fruit}}, \text{ rounded up}$$

### A worked example

A forecast for a Friday (the numbers are in [labs/module-08/prep-forecast.csv](../../labs/module-08/prep-forecast.csv)): 12 Daiquiris, 20 Margaritas, 10 Whiskey Sours, 8 Cosmopolitans, 18 Mojitos, 15 Moscow Mules and 14 Espresso Martinis. The house uses 15 ml of 1:1 syrup in place of sugar in the Daiquiri and Mojito ([lesson 05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md) gives the swap). Lime juice per drink from the Module 5 cards: Daiquiri 20, Margarita 15, Cosmopolitan 15, Mojito 20, Moscow Mule 10 ml.

| Item | Calculation | Need | +10% buffer | Prep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lime juice | 12×20 + 20×15 + 8×15 + 18×20 + 15×10 | 1,170 ml | 1,287 ml | 1.3 L |
| Lemon juice | 10×25 | 250 ml | 275 ml | 300 ml |
| 1:1 syrup | 12×15 + 10×20 + 18×15 + 14×10 | 790 ml | 869 ml | 900 ml |

At 30 ml of juice per lime, 1.3 L needs 1,300 ÷ 30 = 43.3, so **44 limes**. At 37 ml per lemon, 300 ml needs **9 lemons**. For 900 ml of 1:1 syrup by weight, 900 × 1.23 g/ml ≈ 1,107 g, so about **553 g sugar and 553 g water** (density from lesson 03.4).

Juice is best fresh, so a bar with a long shift often presses half before opening and half mid-shift rather than all of it at 17:00 (professional judgment). Syrup keeps for days, so it is made ahead and only topped up from a new bottle, never poured onto old syrup.

### Order of prep

Make first what takes longest or must cool, and last what wilts or dries: **syrups first** (they need time to cool), then **juice**, then **cut garnish** and **herbs**. Cut enough garnish for the first two hours and refill from the fridge; a small tray that is refilled stays fresher than a big one cut at 17:00 (professional judgment).

### Storage

- **Cold:** juice, cut fruit, syrups, opened mixers, dairy, egg white and aquafaba, and opened vermouth and other wine-based aperitifs go in the fridge at 0–4 °C ([lesson 08.1](lesson-01.md)), covered and labelled.
- **On the station:** during service, juice and garnish sit on ice or in a cold rail, covered when not in use. The CDC says cut fruit should be back in the fridge within **2 hours**, or within **1 hour** above 32 °C (90 °F); a hot bar top in an Israeli summer counts.
- **Room temperature:** spirits and most liqueurs, out of direct sun. Cream liqueurs and anything with dairy follow the label.
- **FIFO in the fridge:** new prep goes **behind** the old, so the oldest is used first (FOODSAFE).
- **Allergens:** keep nut syrups such as orgeat ([lesson 06.4](../module-06/lesson-04.md)) and any dairy in clearly labelled bottles with their own pourer and tongs, away from other prep. Module 9 covers allergen rules.

### Labels

Every container gets a label: **name, date and time made, initials**, and for anything with a fixed limit, the **discard date**. Masking tape and a marker are enough. Unlabelled prep is thrown away, without exceptions.

### How long things keep, and who says so

Shelf life is where bars most often repeat numbers nobody checked. This table says what each limit is based on:

| Item | Store | Keep for | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh citrus juice | Fridge, covered | The shift; discard at close | Professional judgment (lesson 03.4); the FDA warns untreated juice can carry bacteria and must be kept cold |
| Cut citrus wheels, wedges | Fridge or on ice, covered | The shift; out of the cold no more than 2 h | CDC 2-hour rule; "discard at close" is professional judgment |
| Twists cut ahead | Covered, on a damp cloth, fridge | A few hours (they dry and lose oil) | Professional judgment |
| 1:1 syrup | Fridge, clean bottle | About 1–2 weeks | Professional judgment (lesson 03.4) |
| 2:1 syrup | Fridge, clean bottle | Longer than 1:1 | Professional judgment (lesson 03.4) |
| Mint and soft herbs | Stems in water or in damp paper, fridge | 2–3 days looking fresh | Professional judgment |
| Opened vermouth | Fridge, capped | About twice as long as at room temperature, where it lost flavour after about a month in Cook's Illustrated's test | Test reported by Tasting Table |
| Pasteurised egg white, cream, dairy | Fridge | As the label says after opening | Producer's label |
| Ready-to-eat food made in-house that needs cold holding | 5 °C (41 °F) or colder | Up to 7 days, date-marked; when two containers are combined, the older date counts | U.S. Food Code 3-501.17 (South Carolina guide) |

If a product shows any sign of spoilage (cloudy syrup, fizzing, mould, an off smell), it goes in the bin whatever its label says.

> [!NOTE]
> Temperature and time limits are from the CDC, FDA and FOODSAFE (BC), checked 2026-10-17. They are international guidance, not Israeli law; Module 9 covers Israeli requirements. Keep-for times marked professional judgment are starting points: follow your venue's written policy.

## Step by step

### Write a prep list

1. Get the forecast: drinks expected per menu item tonight (from the POS history, the manager or the booking sheet).
2. For each prepared item (each juice, each syrup, each garnish), multiply drinks × ml (or pieces) per drink and add the lines.
3. Add the buffer (10% to start) and round up to an amount you can measure and store.
4. Subtract what is already in the fridge **and still in date**. That is what you make.
5. Convert juice to fruit with your yield and syrup to grams of sugar and water.
6. Write the list in prep order: syrups, juice, garnish, herbs, equipment.

### Prep and store

1. Wash hands, clean and sanitise the board and tools ([lesson 01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)).
2. Make syrups by weight and leave them to cool, covered.
3. Wash, press and fine-strain citrus into clean bottles.
4. Cut garnish for the first two hours; keep the rest whole in the fridge.
5. Label every container: name, date, time, initials, and discard date if one applies.
6. Store in the fridge, new behind old. Put the station's share on ice, covered.
7. Tick the prep list and note anything you could not make.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- The forecast [labs/module-08/prep-forecast.csv](../../labs/module-08/prep-forecast.csv) and the worksheet [labs/module-08/prep-list-worksheet.md](../../labs/module-08/prep-list-worksheet.md), a calculator or spreadsheet.
- Your yield numbers from lesson 03.4 (or use 30 ml per lime and 37 ml per lemon).
- For the mini prep: 2 lemons, 1 orange, a small bunch of mint, 100 g sugar, water, a scale, a press, a fine strainer, a peeler, a paring knife, a board on a damp cloth, three small containers with lids, labels, a marker, a timer, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: limes or lemons only; jam jars with lids; masking tape for labels.

> [!CAUTION]
> Knife and peeler work: cut on a board that cannot slide, keep fingers curled away from the blade, cut away from your body, and never leave a knife in a sink or under a cloth ([lesson 01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md)).

### Time

75 minutes: 25 for the prep list, 35 for the mini prep, 15 for checks two hours later and notes.

### Steps

1. **Prep list.** Open the CSV and calculate lime juice, lemon juice and 1:1 syrup needed with a 10% buffer, as in the worked example. The worksheet lists what is already in the fridge; subtract what is still in date, then convert to fruit and to grams of sugar and water. Fill in the worksheet.
2. Add a garnish line: lime slices for Mojitos and Moscow Mules, lemon twists for Cosmopolitans, half orange slices for Whiskey Sours, mint sprigs. How many would you cut for the first two hours if half the drinks sell by 20:00?
3. **Mini prep, timed.** Make a mini mise en place for ten home-practice drinks in the right order: 100 ml of 1:1 syrup by weight; the juice of 2 lemons, strained; 6 orange half-wheels; 6 lemon twists; mint sprigs with their stems in a glass of water. Label everything.
4. Put three twists in a covered container on a damp paper towel in the fridge and leave three uncovered on the board. Do the same with the half-wheels.
5. **Two hours later**, compare covered and uncovered: colour, dryness, how much oil a twist still sprays when you squeeze it. Write it down.
6. Check your work against the solution and record times and findings in your practice log.

### What you should see

Your prep list should come to about 1.3 L lime, 300 ml lemon and 900 ml syrup before subtracting the fridge stock (the solution shows the rest). The most common slips are forgetting the syrup in the Mojito and Daiquiri, adding the buffer before multiplying, and not subtracting what is already prepped. In the mini prep, the first run usually takes 25–35 minutes (professional judgment). After two hours the uncovered twists are dry at the edges, curl and spray little oil; the covered ones look and smell almost fresh. Uncovered wheels darken and dry on the cut face. That is why garnish is cut in small amounts, covered, and refilled.

### What this practice cannot teach

A real prep list comes from a POS history and a manager's knowledge of the night, and real prep is done in litres while the bar fills up; you practise the calculation, the order and the storage, not the volume.

- [ ] I made a mini mise en place in the right order and labelled every container.

## Common mistakes

- **Prepping by habit, not by forecast.** You run out on a busy night and waste on a quiet one. Fix: write the prep list from numbers, and check leftovers at close to tune the buffer.
- **Cutting all the garnish at opening.** It dries and browns by 21:00. Fix: cut for the first two hours, keep the rest whole and covered.
- **Topping up old juice or syrup.** The mix takes the oldest date, and old contamination spreads into new prep. Fix: finish or discard, wash the bottle, refill.
- **Labels without a time.** "Lime, Friday" does not say whether it was made at 10:00 or 22:00. Fix: name, date, time, initials.
- **Quoting shelf lives as facts.** "Syrup keeps a month" may be someone's habit. Fix: know which limits are official and which are judgment, and follow the venue's written policy.

## Key takeaways

- Prep from a forecast: drinks × ml per drink, plus a buffer (10% to start), minus what is in date in the fridge.
- Convert juice to fruit with your own yield; make syrup by weight.
- Prep in order: syrups, juice, garnish, herbs; cut garnish for two hours at a time.
- Keep cold prep at 0–4 °C, covered, labelled with name, date, time and initials, new behind old.
- Know what each shelf-life limit is based on: official guidance, the producer's label or professional judgment.
