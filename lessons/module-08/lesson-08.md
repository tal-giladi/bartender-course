# 08.8 · The service simulation: a full rush

This is Module 8's practical piece. You plan a Friday shift, warm up on the simulation's practice shift, then run a full rush: twenty tickets in three waves, rounds of identical drinks, a server's table and bar guests arriving together, and a drink sent back. Afterwards you debrief with numbers, change one thing and run it again. Finally you take your hands back to the kitchen for a timed home mini-rush of six paper tickets. Everything from the module comes together: opening and prep, batches, stock, sequencing, equipment awareness and communication.

## Why it matters

Trial shifts in many bars are on a Thursday or Friday night, because that is when the manager learns most about a new bartender (professional judgment). Nobody expects you to be the fastest person behind the bar. They watch whether you keep the drinks correct when it gets busy, whether you finish a round together, whether you call out, ask for stock early, own your mistakes and keep your station clean between tickets. The rush shift in the simulation is the closest a home course can get to that pressure, and the debrief is how you turn one bad run into a better one, which is exactly the habit good bartenders keep for years.

## Core ideas

### The four service-simulation shifts

All four use the time table, the 30-second dead-drink rule and the scoring from [lesson 08.5](lesson-05.md): a drink is correct when glass, ice, every ingredient (within ±5 ml), method and garnish match the card and it is not dead; accuracy % = correct drinks served ÷ drinks ordered × 100; waste % = ml in binned, dead and remade drinks ÷ all ml poured × 100; ticket time runs from arrival to serve.

| Shift (preset) | Tickets | Drinks per ticket | Menu | Arrivals | Events | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tutorial` (08.5) | 5 | 1–2 | Daiquiri, Margarita, Negroni, Moscow Mule, Cuba Libre, Aperol Spritz | Next ticket when you serve the last; step hints on | — | Accuracy 100%, average ticket ≤ 90 s, waste 0% |
| `practice` (08.5) | 12 | 1–3 | The twelve Module 5 drinks | One every 75 s | — | ≥ 90%, ≤ 150 s, ≤ 5% |
| `stockout` (08.4) | 15 | 1–3 | The twelve Module 5 drinks | One every 70 s | Lime juice runs out around ticket 6; Aperol around ticket 11; end-of-shift count and order of 6 items | ≥ 90%, ≤ 180 s, ≤ 5%; no missing or unapproved ingredient; order list 6/6 |
| `rush` (this lesson) | 20 | 1–4, including rounds of 3–4 of one drink | The twelve Module 5 drinks plus Tom Collins, Paloma, Cuba Libre, French 75, draught beer and house wine | Three waves: 6 tickets in minutes 0–3, 6 in minutes 3–10, 8 in minutes 10–18 | A server's table ticket and bar guests' tickets arrive together; one guest sends a correct drink back and you remake it | ≥ 90%, ≤ 300 s, ≤ 5% |

The rush has a looser time target than practice because in the first wave more work arrives than one bartender can finish at once: tickets will wait. The skill is to keep that wait short and fair while every drink stays correct. The remade drink counts as waste (its ml go in the bin) but not as an incorrect drink, because the first one was made to spec. Module 6 cards ([lesson 06.3](../module-06/lesson-03.md) for the Tom Collins, Paloma and French 75, [lesson 06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md) for the Cuba Libre) apply; draught beer and wine are single quick steps in the simulation (Module 7 teaches the real pours).

### What the rush tests

- **Queue decisions:** oldest first, with quick wins (beers, wine, highballs) taken once each.
- **Rounds:** four French 75s or four Mojitos built in passes, two tins per shake, bubbles last.
- **Stock awareness:** levels drop faster in a rush; asking the barback early costs 3 seconds, fetching yourself 45.
- **Recovery:** a sent-back drink or a binned mistake is remade at once, logged, and the queue continues.

### Debrief: numbers first, then causes, then one change

After each run, fill in the [service debrief](../../templates/service-debrief.md) with the simulation's numbers before you think about how it felt. Then look for causes in this order (professional judgment):

1. **Incorrect drinks.** Which ones, and why: wrong spec, dead drink, missing garnish, wrong glass? Dead drinks point to sequence; wrong specs point to recall (back to the recipe drills of Modules 5 and 6).
2. **Slowest tickets.** Where did the time go: waiting in the queue (decision problem) or long work on the ticket itself (sequence problem: bottles picked up repeatedly, single shakes, ice too early)?
3. **Waste.** Binned drinks, dead drinks, remakes. Most waste comes from the same causes as incorrect drinks.
4. **Stock.** Did anything run out mid-ticket? When did you first see it was low?

Change **one** thing for the next run (for example "pour in passes on every ticket of three or more" or "ask the barback at the first low-stock warning") so you can see whether it worked. Changing five things at once teaches you nothing about which one helped.

### The home mini-rush

The simulation trains decisions; your hands need the same rules. The run sheet has six paper tickets (14 drinks) built from your home-practice versions. You make them in order of arrival with the quick-win exception, in passes, and time each ticket. It shows whether the sequencing you used in the simulation survives real jiggers, tins and ice.

> [!NOTE]
> Presets, targets and time rules are this course's educational model (lesson 08.5), set so that a well-sequenced run meets them; they are not industry standards. Specs follow the IBA as used in Modules 5 and 6 (checked 2026-10-17).

## Step by step

### Before the shift (planning, 15 minutes)

1. Read the rush menu and mark the drinks you are least sure of; review their cards in the recipe library or Modules 5 and 6.
2. Decide which drinks you would batch for a real Friday (08.3) and which prep would run out first (08.2, 08.4).
3. Write your three sequencing rules on a card next to the screen: oldest first plus quick wins; pours in passes, ice late; bubbles and garnish last.

### The runs

1. **Warm-up:** one `practice` shift. Note the numbers.
2. **Rush run 1.** Play it through without restarting, whatever happens.
3. **Debrief run 1** on the service debrief: numbers, causes, one change.
4. **Rush run 2** with that one change. Debrief again.
5. Optional: a `stockout` shift to practise early barback requests and the order list.

### The home mini-rush

1. Set up your home station (08.1) with ice, water, citrus, syrup, tea and soda.
2. Lay the six tickets face down; turn them over at the times on the run sheet.
3. Make each ticket with the 08.5 rules; start the timer when a ticket is turned over and stop it when its drinks are all on the "pass" (a tray).
4. Check one pour per ticket on the scale; reset between tickets.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A device with a browser for the simulation (a laptop or tablet is easier than a phone for the rush).
- The run sheet [labs/module-08/rush-run-sheet.md](../../labs/module-08/rush-run-sheet.md) and two copies of the [service debrief](../../templates/service-debrief.md).
- For the home mini-rush: your home station, ice, cold water, cold black tea, lemons or limes, 1:1 syrup, soda water, ginger beer or ginger ale, a few mint leaves, six to eight glasses or cups, a tray, a kitchen scale, a phone stopwatch, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: water and syrup only for every drink; cups instead of glasses; jars as tins.

> [!CAUTION]
> Glass and knives in a hurry: keep the board on a damp cloth, cut garnish before the mini-rush starts, never scoop ice with a glass, and if a glass breaks near your ice, stop and burn the ice before you continue ([lesson 01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)).

### Time

90 minutes: 15 planning, 10 practice warm-up, 15 per rush run plus 10 per debrief, 15 for the home mini-rush.

### Steps

1. **Plan** the shift on the run sheet (menu check, batch and prep decisions, your three rules).
2. **Warm up** with one practice shift.

[Simulation: Service shift — practice](../../simulations/service-sim/index.html?preset=practice)

3. **Rush run 1.** Twenty tickets in three waves, rounds of up to four drinks, a server's table and bar guests at the same time, one drink sent back. **Targets:** accuracy at least 90%, average ticket time 300 seconds or less, waste 5% or less.

[Simulation: Service rush — Friday night](../../simulations/service-sim/index.html?preset=rush)

4. **Debrief run 1** on the service debrief and the run sheet: numbers, the causes of every incorrect drink and of your two slowest tickets, and one change.
5. **Rush run 2** with the change; debrief again and compare.
6. Optional: one stock-out shift.

[Simulation: Service shift — stock-outs](../../simulations/service-sim/index.html?preset=stockout)

7. **Home mini-rush.** Make the six tickets on the run sheet as alcohol-free drinks, turning each ticket over at its time. Record each ticket's time and one weighed pour per ticket.
8. Write a five-line reflection: what you will do differently on a real trial shift.

### What you should see

A first rush run commonly lands around 80–90% accuracy and 300–400 seconds average ticket time, with most errors being dead drinks in the first wave and a missed garnish or wrong glass on Module 6 drinks (professional judgment). The second run, with one targeted change, usually improves one number clearly; if nothing improved, the change was not aimed at your biggest cause. In the home mini-rush, expect 60–120 seconds per ticket of two or three drinks, with the second wave of tickets waiting while you finish the first: that is a rush. Pours that drift off target under time pressure (more than ±5%) show where your jigger technique from lesson 03.2 still needs practice.

### What this practice cannot teach

The simulation cannot make you feel guests leaning over the bar, a sticky floor or the noise; the home mini-rush cannot give you real tickets from strangers. Together they train decisions and movements so that on a real shift your attention is free for the guests. Asking a bar for a few barback shifts after this module (as lesson 01.1 suggested) is the next step.

## Common mistakes

- **Restarting a bad run.** You never practise recovery. Fix: always finish the shift, then debrief.
- **Debriefing feelings, not numbers.** "It was chaos" does not tell you what to change. Fix: write the numbers first, then causes.
- **Changing everything at once.** You cannot tell what helped. Fix: one change per run.
- **Chasing time and losing accuracy.** Fast wrong drinks are remakes. Fix: accuracy first; time comes from sequence, not rushing.
- **Forgetting stock in a rush.** Fix: glance at levels after every ticket; ask the barback early.

## Key takeaways

- The rush preset: 20 tickets in three waves, rounds up to four, a table and the bar at once, one sent-back drink; targets ≥ 90% accuracy, ≤ 300 s average ticket time, ≤ 5% waste.
- Plan before service: weak specs reviewed, prep and batches decided, your three sequencing rules in view.
- Debrief with numbers, then causes (incorrect drinks, slow tickets, waste, stock), then one change.
- Accuracy first; speed comes from queue decisions, passes and ice-late sequencing.
- Take the same rules back to your hands with a home mini-rush.
