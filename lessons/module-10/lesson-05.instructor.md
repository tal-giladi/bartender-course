# Instructor notes — 10.5 Costing project: price your menu

- **Objective:** the module practical: cost and price the learner's 06.8 menu, sales-weighted menu pour cost, a week's reconciliation in ILS, short report, self-scored rubric (`labs/module-10/costing-project-brief.md`, pass 14/20).
- **Calculator contract (`menu` preset):** loads the 14 drinks of the course example menu with `price-list.csv`, VAT 18% on, target 20%, rounding 2 ILS; lets the learner edit lines, enter final prices and a sales mix (`pos-sales-week.csv`), shows pour cost and gross profit per drink, net sales, theoretical cost, menu pour cost (21.1% for the example at final prices) and average gross profit (37.62), and exports CSV via a `data:` link. Formula prices at 20% must match the "Price at 20%" column of the solution.
- **Feeds Module 11:** 11.6 (costing and operations, 10% of the final grade) uses the same formulas with the `exam` preset.
- **Likely confusion:** formula price vs final price. Insist on a written reason for every change.
- **Misconception:** "average of pour costs = menu pour cost". Quiz q2.
- **Expected result:** a menu pour cost of roughly 18–24% (professional judgment); for the example, theoretical 21.1%, actual 21.6%, variance value 168.90 ILS, flags on tequila, white rum, bourbon.
- **Open items:** prices are retail examples (Shufersal online, 2026-10-18) and assumptions; refresh for each cohort. Bars' trade prices are lower, so real pour costs would be lower than the course numbers.
- **Extension:** ask the learner to re-run the menu with trade prices 15% below the retail ex-VAT prices and see how much room that gives on the Hugo Spritz and the Mai Tai.
