# 06.5 · More classics and modern classics

This lesson adds eight drinks that a bartender is likely to be asked for, some old and some created after the year 2000. You will not learn them one by one from zero: each sits in a family you already know and differs from a known drink in one or two ways. The Boulevardier is a Negroni with whiskey, the Caipirinha is a Daiquiri built in the glass, the Hugo Spritz is a spritz with elderflower instead of Aperol. Six of the eight are IBA drinks; the Gimlet and the Hugo Spritz are not on the IBA list, so their cards use Difford's Guide and say so.

## Why it matters

Menus change, and guests bring drinks home from their travels. "Can you make a Penicillin?" "A Hugo, please." "A Caipirinha, but with vodka." A bartender who can answer from the family and one difference looks experienced on the first day; one who reaches for the phone for every request slows the bar down.

## Core ideas

### Eight drinks by family

| Drink | Family | One-line difference | Spec source | Created |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gimlet | sour | gin with sweetened lime cordial instead of sugar and fresh lime | Difford's Guide | an old British navy drink (Difford's Guide) |
| Caipirinha | sour | a lime sour built and muddled in the glass with cachaça | IBA | traditional Brazilian drink (professional judgment) |
| Penicillin | sour | Scotch sour with honey and ginger, a smoky Islay whisky floated on top | IBA | 2005, Sam Ross, Milk & Honey, New York (Difford's Guide) |
| Paper Plane | sour | four equal parts: bourbon, amaro, Aperol, lemon | IBA | 2007, Sam Ross, The Violet Hour, Chicago (Difford's Guide) |
| Gin Basil Smash | sour | gin sour with a handful of basil shaken in | IBA | 2008, Jörg Meyer, Le Lion, Hamburg (Difford's Guide) |
| Boulevardier | martini and Manhattan | a Negroni with bourbon or rye instead of gin, more base | IBA | first printed 1927, Harry MacElhone's *Barflies and Cocktails* (Difford's Guide) |
| Cuba Libre | highball | rum and cola with a little lime | IBA | early 20th century (professional judgment) |
| Hugo Spritz | spritz and sparkling | elderflower liqueur and mint in place of Aperol | Difford's Guide | 2005, Roland Gruber, South Tyrol, Italy (Difford's Guide) |

New words:

- **Lime cordial**: a sweetened, preserved lime juice concentrate (Rose's is the best-known brand). It is sweeter and less sharp than fresh lime.
- **Cachaça** (ka-SHA-sa): Brazilian spirit distilled from fresh sugar-cane juice ([lesson 02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)).
- **Amaro** (plural *amari*): a bittersweet Italian herbal liqueur; Amaro Nonino is a lighter, orange-scented one ([lesson 02.6](../module-02/lesson-06.md)).
- **Float**: a small amount of a liquid poured gently on top so it stays as a layer, at least for the first sips ([lesson 04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md)).
- **Elderflower liqueur**: a sweet liqueur flavoured with elder blossoms (St-Germain is a well-known brand).

### Muddling gently

Three of these drinks are muddled: lime and sugar for the Caipirinha, ginger for the Penicillin, basil (optionally) for the Gin Basil Smash. You learned in [lesson 04.5](../module-04/lesson-05.md) that muddling means pressing, not grinding. Press the lime wedges with the sugar to squeeze out juice and a little oil from the peel; grinding the white pith makes the drink bitter. Herbs only need a press or a hard shake; shredded basil leaves turn black and stick in the guest's teeth, so fine strain.

### Equal parts are easy and unforgiving

The Paper Plane (four × 30 ml) and the Boulevardier's cousin, the Negroni ([lesson 05.3](../module-05/lesson-03.md)), are easy to remember because the parts are equal or nearly so. But every pour shows: 5 ml too much amaro or Campari changes the balance at once. Measure each part; do not free-pour equal-parts drinks (professional judgment).

> [!NOTE]
> Specs checked on iba-world.com and diffordsguide.com on 2026-10-17. Creation dates and creators are as stated by Difford's Guide; other sources sometimes give a different year (for example 2007 or 2008 for the Paper Plane).

> [!WARNING]
> Several of these drinks are strong (the Boulevardier is almost all spirit; the Penicillin has 67.5 ml of whisky). If you taste real versions, make half sizes, sip and spit, and never before driving.

## Step by step

### Gimlet

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| London dry gin | 50 | 1⅔ |
| Lime cordial | 20 | ⅔ |
| Oude genever | 10 | ⅓ |
| Fresh lime juice | 5 | ⅙ |
| Fresh lemon juice | 5 | ⅙ |
| Rich sugar syrup (2:1) | 5 | ⅙ |

- **Method:** shake with ice, fine strain into a chilled coupe.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled coupe, no ice.
- **Garnish:** lime zest twist expressed and discarded; lime wedge.
- **Profile and key technique:** sharp and sweet at once, with a soft malty depth from the genever. The key is balancing cordial (sweet) with a little fresh citrus.
- **Spec source and variations:** not an IBA drink; card source [Difford's Guide — Gimlet (Difford's recipe)](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/831/gimlet-diffords-recipe). The same page says the Gimlet is classically equal parts gin and lime cordial, stirred in the serving glass, ice optional, and that cordial-heavy versions are very sweet, which is why its recipe adds fresh lime and lemon.
- **Substitutions:** leave out the genever and use 60 ml gin; if there is no lime cordial, many bars make a fresh-lime gin sour and call it a "gin sour", not a Gimlet (professional judgment).
- **Home-practice version:** 60 ml cold water + 20 ml lime cordial + 5 ml lime + 5 ml lemon, shaken, fine strained.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** lime cordial topped up with cold water and fresh lime, shaken, with a juniper or rosemary note if you have one. Close in taste; much lighter.
- **Common mistakes:** fresh lime and syrup only (that is a gin sour); equal parts cordial and gin with no fresh citrus when the house spec is Difford's (too sweet).

### Caipirinha

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Cachaça | 60 | 2 |
| Lime, cut into small wedges | 1 lime | — |
| White cane sugar | 4 teaspoons | — |

- **Method:** muddle lime and sugar gently in the glass; fill with cracked ice; add cachaça; stir gently.
- **Glass and ice:** double old fashioned glass, cracked (crushed) ice.
- **Garnish:** none in the IBA spec (the lime is in the drink).
- **Profile and key technique:** fresh, sharp lime with grassy cane spirit and sugar. The key is pressing, not grinding: juice and peel oil out, pith left whole.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Caipirinha](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/caipirinha/). The IBA page names its own variations: Caipiroska (vodka instead of cachaça), Caipirissima (rum) and Caipirão (Licor Beirão, a Portuguese liqueur).
- **Substitutions:** 20 ml 1:1 syrup for the sugar is faster but less traditional (professional judgment: 4 teaspoons of sugar is roughly 16 g).
- **Home-practice version:** muddle 1 lime and 4 teaspoons sugar, crushed ice, 60 ml cold water, stir.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** the same with soda water instead of water, a lime "caipi-soda". Fair; no cane spirit.
- **Common mistakes:** grinding the lime (bitter); cubed ice instead of cracked (less dilution, warm sugar left at the bottom); straining it (it is served with the lime in).

### Cuba Libre

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| White rum | 50 | 1⅔ |
| Cola | 120 | 4 |
| Fresh lime juice | 10 | ⅓ |

- **Method:** build in a highball filled with ice.
- **Glass and ice:** highball glass, ice cubes.
- **Garnish:** lime wedge.
- **Profile and key technique:** sweet cola, a lift of lime, light rum. The key is the lime: without it the drink is a "rum and cola" (professional judgment on naming).
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Cuba Libre](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/cuba-libre/). Many bars squeeze the lime wedge in and drop it into the glass instead of measuring the juice (professional judgment).
- **Substitutions:** gold rum; any cola.
- **Home-practice version:** 50 ml cold water + 10 ml lime + 120 ml cola, built over ice.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** cola with 10–15 ml fresh lime and a dash of vanilla. Close.
- **Common mistakes:** no lime; flat cola from an open bottle; a half-full glass of ice.

### Penicillin

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Blended Scotch whisky | 60 | 2 |
| Fresh lemon juice | 22.5 | ¾ |
| Honey syrup | 22.5 | ¾ |
| Fresh ginger | 2–3 quarter-size slices | — |
| Islay single malt (Lagavulin 16 in the IBA spec), to float | 7.5 | ¼ |

- **Method:** muddle the ginger in the shaker; add Scotch, lemon and honey syrup; shake with ice; fine strain into a chilled old fashioned glass with ice; float the Islay malt on top.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled old fashioned glass, ice (a large cube is common).
- **Garnish:** candied ginger.
- **Profile and key technique:** honeyed, spicy, sour, with a smoky nose from the float. The key is the float: pour it last, slowly over the back of a spoon, so the smoke is the first thing the guest smells.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Penicillin](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/penicillin/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Penicillin](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/2539/penicillin) (Simon Difford's 2009 version) is 45 ml blended Scotch, 15 ml ginger liqueur, 10 ml peated single malt, 20 ml lemon, 20 ml honey syrup (3 honey to 1 water by weight), all shaken and strained over ice; the page says Sam Ross's original used a honey-ginger syrup.
- **Substitutions:** any peated Islay malt for the float; honey syrup is honey thinned with warm water (house ratio, often 2:1 or 3:1 by weight; professional judgment).
- **Home-practice version:** muddle ginger, add 60 ml cold black tea, 22.5 ml lemon, 22.5 ml honey syrup, shake, fine strain over ice; float 7.5 ml strong lapsang souchong (smoked) tea.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** the home-practice version as written. Good and guest-ready.
- **Common mistakes:** shaking the Islay malt in (the smoke disappears into the drink); no fine strain (ginger fibres); thick honey not dissolving (use syrup).

### Paper Plane

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Bourbon whiskey | 30 | 1 |
| Amaro Nonino | 30 | 1 |
| Aperol | 30 | 1 |
| Fresh lemon juice | 30 | 1 |

- **Method:** shake well with ice, strain into a chilled cocktail glass.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass, no ice.
- **Garnish:** none in the IBA spec; a lemon twist or a small paper plane on the rim is common.
- **Profile and key technique:** bittersweet orange, herbs and bourbon, bright lemon. The key is four exact pours.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Paper Plane](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/paper-plane/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Paper Plane](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/3630/paper-plane) uses 22.5 ml of each, fine strained into a chilled coupe; the page says Sam Ross first made it with Campari and switched to a less bitter aperitivo.
- **Substitutions:** rye for bourbon; another light amaro will change the drink noticeably (tell the guest).
- **Home-practice version:** 30 ml cold black tea + 30 ml orange juice + 30 ml cold, strong chamomile tea with 10 ml syrup + 30 ml lemon, shaken. Trains four equal pours.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** an alcohol-free bitter aperitif (30 ml) with 30 ml orange juice, 30 ml cold black tea and 20 ml lemon, shaken. Fair; check the brand's ABV is 0.0%.
- **Common mistakes:** free-pouring "equal parts"; Campari instead of Aperol (much more bitter).

### Gin Basil Smash

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Gin | 60 | 2 |
| Fresh lemon juice | 22.5 | ¾ |
| Sugar syrup (1:1) | 22.5 | ¾ |
| Basil leaves | 10 leaves | — |

- **Method:** shake hard with ice (the shake tears the basil); fine strain into the chilled glass.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass (IBA); Difford's Guide uses a chilled old fashioned glass.
- **Garnish:** none in the IBA spec; a basil sprig is common.
- **Profile and key technique:** bright green, herbal, lemony. The key is fresh basil and a hard shake, then a fine strain.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Gin Basil Smash](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/gin-basil-smash/) (the IBA text says "pour"; fine straining is recommended, professional judgment). Variation: [Difford's Guide Gin Basil Smash](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/3282/gin-basil-smash) muddles 12 basil leaves (sprigs) in the shaker, adds 60 ml gin, 22.5 ml lemon, 10 ml 2:1 syrup, shakes and fine strains into a chilled old fashioned glass; basil sprig bouquet.
- **Substitutions:** none faithful for fresh basil (dried basil does not work).
- **Home-practice version:** 60 ml cold water + 22.5 ml lemon + 22.5 ml 1:1 syrup + 10 basil leaves, shaken hard, fine strained.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** the home-practice version with soda water added after straining. Close and guest-ready.
- **Common mistakes:** wilted basil; no fine strain (leaf bits); muddling basil to a paste (bitter, dark).

### Boulevardier

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Bourbon or rye whiskey | 45 | 1½ |
| Campari | 30 | 1 |
| Sweet red vermouth | 30 | 1 |

- **Method:** stir with ice in a mixing glass, strain into a chilled cocktail glass.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass, no ice (IBA); on a large cube is a common alternative.
- **Garnish:** orange zest (optionally lemon zest).
- **Profile and key technique:** rich, bittersweet, warming; rounder than a Negroni. The key is the stir: cold and silky, not watery.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Boulevardier](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/boulevardier/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Boulevardier](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/2955/boulevardier) is 45 ml bourbon, 22.5 ml sweet vermouth, 22.5 ml red bitter (2:1:1), stirred, strained over a large cube in an old fashioned glass, orange twist; the page says the drink first appears in Harry MacElhone's 1927 *Barflies and Cocktails*.
- **Substitutions:** rye for bourbon (spicier); another red bitter aperitif for Campari (tell the guest).
- **Home-practice version:** 45 ml cold black tea + 30 ml cold hibiscus tea with 5 ml syrup + 30 ml cold grape juice cut with tea, stirred and strained. Trains the stir and the pours.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** none that is faithful: the drink is all spirit and wine. Closest: an alcohol-free bitter aperitif with an alcohol-free whiskey alternative, stirred.
- **Common mistakes:** shaking it (cloudy); 30 ml whiskey as in a Negroni (it is 45); old, oxidised vermouth.

### Hugo Spritz

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh mint leaves | 8 leaves | — |
| Elderflower liqueur | 40 | 1⅓ |
| Soda water (chilled) | 60 | 2 |
| Prosecco (chilled) | 60 | 2 |

- **Method:** mint leaves into the chilled glass; fill with ice; add the other ingredients; stir briefly.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled wine glass, ice cubes.
- **Garnish:** lime wedge and a bouquet of mint sprigs.
- **Profile and key technique:** light, floral, minty, gently sweet. The key is cold ingredients and a brief stir so it stays fizzy.
- **Spec source and variations:** not an IBA drink; card source [Difford's Guide — Hugo Spritz](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/5039/hugo-spritz), which credits Roland Gruber, South Tyrol, 2005. Bars vary the elderflower amount a lot; follow the house spec.
- **Substitutions:** elderflower syrup or cordial (non-alcoholic) for the liqueur, about 20 ml, because it is sweeter (professional judgment); cava or another dry sparkling wine for Prosecco.
- **Home-practice version:** 8 mint leaves, ice, 20 ml elderflower cordial, 60 ml soda, 60 ml more soda (for the Prosecco), brief stir.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** elderflower cordial, soda and alcohol-free sparkling wine with mint and lime. Close and guest-ready.
- **Common mistakes:** muddling the mint hard (bitter); warm Prosecco; overfilling so it cannot be stirred.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A muddler (or the end of a rolling pin), a shaker, a mixing glass or jar, a jigger, a Hawthorne and a fine strainer, a bar spoon, two rocks glasses, a highball, a wine glass, cubed and crushed ice.
- 4 limes, 2 lemons, sugar, 1:1 syrup, fresh basil and mint, cola, soda water, elderflower cordial (or syrup), cold black tea.
- The eight cards above copied onto your own [recipe cards](../../templates/recipe-card.md), a phone timer and your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: skip the elderflower cordial and practise the Hugo's build with 20 ml of 1:1 syrup.

> [!CAUTION]
> Cut limes on a board, away from your fingers. Muddle in a sturdy glass or a metal tin, never in a thin glass: pressing hard can crack it.

### Time

60 minutes: 15 for the recall round, 35 for four drinks, 10 to taste and record.

### Steps

1. **Recall round.** For each of the eight drinks, write from memory: family, ml, method, glass, garnish and the one-line difference. Check with the cards and score 1 point per correct line (6 per drink, 48 total).
2. **Caipirinha style.** Cut a lime into 8 small wedges. Muddle with 4 teaspoons of sugar: four firm presses and a twist, no grinding. Fill with crushed ice, add 60 ml cold water, stir. Taste one with ground pith if you want to feel the difference (muddle a second lime hard).
3. **Cuba Libre style.** Build 50 ml water, 10 ml lime and 120 ml cola over ice; one lift. Time it: aim for under 30 seconds (professional judgment).
4. **Gin Basil Smash style.** Shake 60 ml water, 22.5 ml lemon, 22.5 ml syrup and 10 basil leaves hard for 12 seconds. Pour half through the Hawthorne only, and half through the Hawthorne and fine strainer. Compare.
5. **Hugo style.** Mint, ice, 20 ml elderflower cordial, 120 ml soda, brief stir.
6. Record recall score, times and notes. Run the simulation for the Module 6 cards.

### What you should see

A first recall round on drinks you met an hour ago usually scores 30–40 of 48. The common misses are the small amounts (Penicillin 22.5 and 7.5 ml, Gimlet's three 5 ml pours), the Boulevardier's 45 ml base (people write 30, as in a Negroni) and which drinks are not IBA. Repeat the missed cards over the next days with the Leitner boxes from [lesson 05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md).

The gently muddled Caipirinha tastes fresh and sharp; the hard-muddled one has a bitter, lingering finish from the pith. The Smash through the Hawthorne alone has green specks floating on top; the double strained one is clear green. The Cuba Libre build takes 20–30 seconds once the glass and lime are ready.

### What this practice cannot teach

The NA versions train movements and pours, but not the real flavours that make these drinks distinct (smoky Islay malt, bitter Campari, grassy cachaça); small, optional tastings at a bar fill that gap.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I can write all eight specs with family, ml, method, glass and garnish.
- [ ] I know which two are not IBA drinks and where their specs come from.
- [ ] I muddled lime gently and tasted the difference from hard muddling.
- [ ] I fine strained a herb drink and saw the difference.

## Common mistakes

- **Learning eight drinks from zero.** Fix: learn the family and the one-line difference first, then the amounts.
- **Grinding lime or herbs.** Bitter and messy. Fix: press, twist, stop.
- **Shaking the float in.** The Penicillin loses its smoky nose. Fix: float last, over a spoon.
- **Free-pouring equal-parts drinks.** Fix: jigger every part of a Paper Plane or Boulevardier.
- **Saying a non-IBA spec is "the official one".** Fix: Gimlet and Hugo Spritz specs come from Difford's Guide; house specs vary.

## Key takeaways

- Gimlet (Difford's): 50 gin, 20 lime cordial, 10 genever, 5 lime, 5 lemon, 5 rich syrup; shaken, coupe. Classically equal gin and cordial.
- Caipirinha (IBA): 60 cachaça, 1 lime in wedges, 4 teaspoons sugar; muddled and built on cracked ice.
- Cuba Libre (IBA): 50 white rum, 120 cola, 10 lime; built in a highball.
- Penicillin (IBA): 60 blended Scotch, 22.5 lemon, 22.5 honey syrup, ginger muddled; Islay malt 7.5 floated.
- Paper Plane (IBA): 30 each bourbon, Amaro Nonino, Aperol, lemon; shaken. Gin Basil Smash (IBA): 60 gin, 22.5 lemon, 22.5 syrup, 10 basil leaves; shaken.
- Boulevardier (IBA): 45 bourbon or rye, 30 Campari, 30 sweet vermouth; stirred. Hugo Spritz (Difford's): 8 mint leaves, 40 elderflower liqueur, 60 soda, 60 Prosecco; built.
