# 06.4 · Bloody Mary and Mai Tai

These two drinks look like opposites. The Bloody Mary is red, savory and spicy, a brunch drink built on tomato juice. The Mai Tai is golden, sweet-sour and nutty, the most famous drink of the tiki style. They share one thing that matters behind the bar: both are judged by how they are seasoned and finished, not only by the spirit. This lesson gives both IBA specs and their best-known variations, teaches you to season a Bloody Mary in small steps and to mix it gently, shows how a Mai Tai is shaken and served, and names the allergens in each, because Worcestershire sauce contains fish and orgeat is made from almonds.

## Why it matters

Sunday at noon, a table orders four Bloody Marys: "one extra spicy, one mild, one without Worcestershire, she's allergic to fish". If you know the spec as a base plus seasoning, all four are easy. If you only know "a splash of everything", you will guess, and one guess could cause an allergic reaction. The Mai Tai has the opposite trap: guests expect a big tropical serve, and a bar that cuts corners with bottled mix and no garnish loses the drink's point.

## Core ideas

### The savory family

**Savory** drinks get their main character from salt, spice, tomato or other non-sweet flavours. The Bloody Mary is the classic one: vodka gives strength without much flavour, tomato juice gives body, lemon gives freshness, and the seasonings do the rest:

| Seasoning | What it adds | IBA amount |
|---|---|---|
| Worcestershire sauce | savory depth, a little sweet and sour; contains anchovy (fish) | 2 dashes |
| Tabasco (hot pepper sauce) | heat and vinegar | to taste |
| Celery salt | salt and a green, celery aroma | to taste |
| Black pepper | warmth and aroma | to taste |

"To taste" is not "any amount". Season in small steps and taste each time ([lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md)); you can always add, never take out. Many bars write a house amount, for example "2 dashes Tabasco, 1 pinch celery salt, 2 grinds pepper" (professional judgment), so that every Bloody Mary is the same.

### Stir or roll, never shake

The IBA Bloody Mary is stirred gently with ice in a mixing glass and poured into a rocks glass, or into a highball with ice if the guest asks. Difford's Guide **rolls** it: tips the closed shaker over and over with ice instead of shaking it. Another common way to roll is to pour the drink between two tins a few times (professional judgment). Both mix and chill without beating air into the tomato juice. A hard shake makes tomato juice frothy and thin, and the drink separates as it stands (professional judgment). You practised a similar gentle movement, throwing, in [lesson 04.6](../module-04/lesson-06.md).

### Tiki and the Mai Tai

**Tiki** is a style of rum drink from American "Polynesian" restaurants of the mid-20th century: several rums, lime, syrups, crushed or cracked ice and generous garnishes. The **Mai Tai** is its best-known drink; Difford's Guide credits Victor "Trader Vic" Bergeron with creating it in 1944. Its ingredients:

- **Two rums.** The IBA names an amber Jamaican rum and a "Martinique molasses rhum", and notes that the Martinique rum Trader Vic used was made from molasses, not the sugar-cane-juice *rhum agricole* the island is known for today ([lesson 02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)).
- **Orange curaçao**, an orange liqueur often made on a brandy or rum base, close to triple sec ([lesson 02.6](../module-02/lesson-06.md)).
- **Orgeat** (say "or-zhah"), a sweet almond syrup, often with orange-flower water. It is non-alcoholic, gives the Mai Tai its nutty, round taste and contains tree nuts.
- **Fresh lime** and a little simple syrup.

The IBA says to shake and pour into a double rocks glass or a highball; Difford's Guide strains it over crushed ice. Garnish is a pineapple spear, mint and lime peel. Smack the mint sprig lightly against your hand to release its aroma ([lesson 04.7](../module-04/lesson-07.md)).

> [!CAUTION]
> Some tiki bars set garnishes on fire. This course never uses flame in drinks: burning alcohol and high-proof rum near guests and paper garnishes cause serious burns. Serve the Mai Tai with fresh mint and fruit only.

### Allergens: say them, do not guess

- **Worcestershire sauce** (Lea & Perrins, the original brand) lists anchovies (fish) and barley malt vinegar on its UK label. Other brands differ; read the bottle you use.
- **Orgeat** is made from almonds (tree nuts).
- **Celery salt** contains celery.

If a guest declares an allergy, leave out the ingredient (a Bloody Mary without Worcestershire is still a Bloody Mary, less deep) or offer another drink, and use clean tools. Never guess. Allergen rules and the guest conversation are covered in Module 9.

> [!NOTE]
> Specs checked on iba-world.com and diffordsguide.com on 2026-10-17; Worcestershire label checked the same day. Product recipes and labels change; the label on the bottle is always the authority.

## Step by step

### Bloody Mary

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Vodka | 45 | 1½ |
| Tomato juice | 90 | 3 |
| Fresh lemon juice | 15 | ½ |
| Worcestershire sauce | 2 dashes | — |
| Tabasco, celery salt, black pepper | to taste | — |

- **Method:** stir gently with ice in a mixing glass, pour into the glass (strain onto fresh ice if served on ice).
- **Glass and ice:** rocks glass; a highball with ice if the guest asks for it on ice.
- **Garnish:** celery stick, lemon wedge (optional).
- **Profile and key technique:** savory, fresh, peppery, with as much heat as the guest wants. The key is seasoning in small steps and mixing gently.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Bloody Mary](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/bloody-mary/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Bloody Mary](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/251/bloody-mary) is 60 ml vodka, 120 ml tomato juice, 15 ml lemon, 8 drops hot pepper sauce, 4 dashes Worcestershire, 2 grinds black pepper, a pinch of celery salt; rolled with ice, strained into an ice-filled Collins glass with a salt rim; celery stick and a skewer of cherry tomato, gherkin and cocktail onion.
- **Substitutions:** lime for lemon; any hot pepper sauce; sea salt and a few celery leaves if there is no celery salt; leave out Worcestershire for a fish allergy (tell the guest the taste will be lighter).
- **Home-practice version:** the Virgin Mary below, made with 45 ml cold water in place of the vodka, trains exactly the same pours and seasoning.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** Virgin Mary: 120 ml tomato juice, 15 ml lemon and the same seasoning, stirred or rolled. Faithful in taste; widely served.
- **Common mistakes:** shaking hard (frothy, thin); seasoning without tasting; a heavy hand with Tabasco on a "mild"; forgetting to ask about allergies.

### Mai Tai

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Amber Jamaican rum | 30 | 1 |
| Martinique molasses rum | 30 | 1 |
| Orange curaçao | 15 | ½ |
| Orgeat (almond) syrup | 15 | ½ |
| Fresh lime juice | 30 | 1 |
| Simple syrup (1:1) | 7.5 | ¼ |

- **Method:** shake with ice and pour into the glass (IBA); or strain over fresh crushed ice (Difford's Guide).
- **Glass and ice:** double rocks glass or highball, on ice.
- **Garnish:** pineapple spear, mint leaves (or a sprig) and lime peel.
- **Profile and key technique:** sweet-sour and nutty, with deep rum, bright lime and a fresh mint nose. The key is fresh lime and real orgeat, and a garnish placed so the guest smells the mint when drinking.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Mai Tai](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/mai-tai/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Mai Tai (Trader Vic's)](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/1219/mai-tai) is 60 ml aged rum, 15 ml dry curaçao, 22.5 ml lime, 10 ml 2:1 orgeat, 7.5 ml 2:1 syrup, optional 5 ml dark rum float; shaken, strained into a double old-fashioned glass of crushed ice; spent lime shell, mint sprigs, pineapple and cherry.
- **Substitutions:** one good aged rum (60 ml) if you do not have two; triple sec for curaçao. There is no faithful substitute for orgeat; for a nut allergy, offer a different drink rather than a Mai Tai without it (professional judgment).
- **Home-practice version:** 60 ml cold strong black tea with 2 drops of vanilla (the "rum"), 15 ml orange juice with 5 ml syrup (the "curaçao"), 15 ml orgeat, 30 ml lime, shaken and poured over crushed ice.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** 30 ml lime, 15 ml orgeat, 30 ml pineapple juice, 30 ml orange juice, 30 ml cold black tea, shaken, crushed ice, mint and pineapple. A good tropical sour; no rum depth.
- **Common mistakes:** a bottled "Mai Tai mix"; grenadine or pineapple juice added to the classic spec (that is a different drink); no orgeat; a limp, bruised mint garnish.

### Season a Bloody Mary to order

1. Ask: "How spicy, mild, medium or hot? Any allergies?"
2. Build vodka, tomato and lemon in the mixing glass. Add the house base seasoning (Worcestershire, celery salt, pepper) unless an allergy rules one out.
3. Add heat for the guest's level: for example 1 dash Tabasco for mild, 3 for medium, 5 for hot (professional judgment; set your own house scale).
4. Add ice, stir gently 10–15 seconds or roll 4–5 times.
5. Taste a drop with a clean straw. Adjust salt or heat once, in small steps.
6. Pour into the glass (on fresh ice if asked), garnish and serve.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A mixing glass or jar, a shaker, a bar spoon, a jigger, a strainer, two rocks glasses, one tall glass, cubed and crushed ice (crush cubes in a clean towel with a rolling pin).
- 500 ml tomato juice, 3 lemons, 3 limes, Worcestershire sauce (or a fish-free alternative), Tabasco, celery salt (or salt), black pepper, orgeat syrup (coffee-syrup brands sell it), orange juice, 1:1 syrup, cold black tea, vanilla, fresh mint, a slice of pineapple.
- Small paper cups and clean straws for tasting, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md) and [tasting notes](../../templates/tasting-notes.md).
- Low-cost alternative: skip the pineapple garnish; a lemon wedge is enough for practice.

> [!CAUTION]
> Use a board and a sharp knife for the pineapple and citrus, cutting away from your hand. Crush ice inside a folded towel, never in a glass.

### Time

50 minutes: 10 to set up, 20 for the Virgin Mary drills, 15 for the Mai Tai, 5 to record.

### Steps

1. Recite both specs, then check them.
2. **Seasoning ladder.** Mix 300 ml tomato juice with 50 ml lemon. Pour 60 ml into each of five cups. Leave cup 1 plain. Add to cup 2 one dash Worcestershire; to cup 3 the same plus a pinch of celery salt; to cup 4 all that plus a grind of pepper; to cup 5 all that plus 2 dashes Tabasco. Taste in order and write one line per cup.
3. **Stir vs shake.** Make two Virgin Marys from the card (45 ml water for the vodka). Stir one gently with ice; shake the other hard for 10 seconds. Pour both and compare the top and the texture after 2 minutes.
4. **Three heat levels.** Make a mild, a medium and a hot Virgin Mary using your own house scale. Write your scale down.
5. **Mai Tai.** Make the home-practice Mai Tai, pour over crushed ice, and garnish with a mint sprig (smacked once) next to the straw and a pineapple wedge. Smell before you sip.
6. Record results. Run the simulation for both cards.

### What you should see

The seasoning ladder shows each ingredient's job: Worcestershire makes the tomato taste deeper and rounder, celery salt makes it taste "more tomato" and less flat, pepper adds a warm smell, Tabasco adds heat and a little vinegar. The plain cup tastes thin by comparison.

The shaken Virgin Mary has a pink froth on top and looks paler; after 2 minutes it starts to separate into a watery layer. The stirred one stays deep red and smooth.

The Mai Tai style tastes sweet-sour and nutty; with the mint next to the straw you smell mint with each sip, which makes it taste fresher.

### What this practice cannot teach

The Virgin Mary teaches the seasoning exactly, but vodka adds warmth and rum adds deep, molasses flavour that tea cannot copy; a real guest's "spicy" may also be very different from yours, which only service teaches.

## Common mistakes

- **Shaking a Bloody Mary.** Frothy and separates. Fix: stir or roll.
- **Seasoning by eye.** Every drink differs. Fix: a written house amount and a taste with a clean straw.
- **Guessing about allergies.** Fix: know that Worcestershire contains fish and orgeat contains almond; read the label; leave the ingredient out or offer another drink.
- **A Mai Tai from a bottled mix.** Fix: fresh lime, real orgeat, curaçao and rum.
- **Fire in a tiki drink.** Fix: never; fresh mint and fruit only.

## Key takeaways

- Bloody Mary (IBA): 45 ml vodka, 90 ml tomato juice, 15 ml lemon, 2 dashes Worcestershire, Tabasco, celery salt and pepper to taste; stirred, rocks glass.
- Mai Tai (IBA): 30 ml amber Jamaican rum, 30 ml Martinique molasses rum, 15 ml orange curaçao, 15 ml orgeat, 30 ml lime, 7.5 ml syrup; shaken, double rocks or highball on ice.
- Savory drinks are stirred or rolled, not shaken.
- Season in small steps and write a house heat scale.
- Worcestershire contains fish; orgeat contains almond. Say it, never guess.
