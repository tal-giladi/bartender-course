# 06.2 · White Lady, Sidecar and Pisco Sour

These three drinks are all sours, the family from [lesson 06.1](lesson-01.md): a base spirit, lemon juice and something sweet, shaken and served cold without ice. What makes them worth learning together is the sweetener. In the White Lady and the Sidecar there is no syrup at all: an orange liqueur, triple sec, does the sweetening and adds flavour. In the Pisco Sour the sweetener is plain syrup, but an egg white turns it into a soft, foamy drink with bitters dotted on top. This lesson gives the three IBA specs and their main variations, shows how to swap a liqueur for syrup and back, and practises a safe foam with pasteurised egg white or aquafaba.

## Why it matters

A guest who loved the Margarita asks for "the same idea, but with gin". The answer is a White Lady, and it is a two-second change if you see the Margarita as "spirit + triple sec + citrus". Another guest asks for a Pisco Sour and wants "the real one, with egg". You need to know that the IBA spec uses egg white, that a bar must not use raw shell egg without care, and that a pasteurised white or aquafaba makes the same foam safely.

## Core ideas

### Triple sec as the sweetener

**Triple sec** is a clear, sweet orange liqueur, usually around 30–40% ABV ([lesson 02.6](../module-02/lesson-06.md)); Cointreau is the best-known brand. In a sour it does two jobs: it sweetens and it adds orange aroma. Because it is less sweet than syrup, the IBA uses as much triple sec as lemon, or more:

| | Base | Lemon | Triple sec | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| White Lady | gin 40 ml | 20 ml | 30 ml | more liqueur than lemon |
| Sidecar | cognac 50 ml | 20 ml | 20 ml | equal liqueur and lemon |
| Margarita ([lesson 05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)) | tequila 50 ml | lime 15 ml | 20 ml | more liqueur than lime |

So the White Lady is "a gin Margarita with lemon" and the Sidecar is "a cognac sour with triple sec". Learn the Margarita well and you have the shape of both. Modern triple secs vary in sugar; Difford's Guide notes that with some brands a few ml of syrup are needed to balance the White Lady. Taste and follow the house spec.

### The Sidecar's base: cognac

**Cognac** is a grape brandy from the Cognac region of France, aged in oak ([lesson 02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). It gives the Sidecar a rounder, fruitier, slightly woody taste than gin gives the White Lady. Any good grape brandy works as a substitute; say so to the guest if you use one, because "Sidecar" on a menu leads guests to expect cognac (professional judgment). Some bars rim the glass with sugar. Difford's Guide traces the sugar rim to a 1934 source and calls it out of fashion now; the IBA spec has no rim and no garnish.

### Pisco and the Pisco Sour

**Pisco** is an unaged grape brandy from Peru and Chile ([lesson 02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)). The Pisco Sour is the classic sour with egg white: shaken, it becomes a pale drink with a thick white foam on top, and the bartender drips a few dashes of **Amargo bitters** (a Peruvian aromatic bitters; Angostura is the usual substitute) on the foam as an aromatic garnish. You smell the bitters with every sip, then taste the sour drink under them.

The IBA spec uses lemon juice. Difford's Guide uses mostly lime with a little lemon, which is closer to how the drink is often made in Peru (professional judgment; check the house spec).

### Egg white, safely

> [!WARNING]
> Raw shell eggs can carry Salmonella bacteria. The IBA Pisco Sour lists a raw egg white; in this course, and in any bar without a written egg policy, use **pasteurised egg white** from a carton (heat-treated to kill bacteria) or **aquafaba** (the liquid from a can of chickpeas, a vegan foaming agent). Tell guests the drink contains egg (or chickpea), because egg is a common allergen. You learned both in [lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md).

One egg white is about 30 ml (professional judgment; eggs vary). Aquafaba is used 1:1 in place of egg white. Shake egg drinks harder and longer, or use a **dry shake** (shake without ice first, then with ice) or a **reverse dry shake** (shake with ice, strain the ice out, shake again without ice) for a thicker foam ([lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)). Fine strain into the glass.

### Bitters on a foam

Drop the bitters from just above the foam, 3–4 separate drops, so each sits on the surface. If you shake the dasher bottle from high up, it splashes and sinks. Some bartenders drag a cocktail pick through the drops to make a pattern; it is decoration only (professional judgment).

> [!NOTE]
> Specs checked on iba-world.com and diffordsguide.com on 2026-10-17. Brand ABVs and the availability of pisco and Amargo Chuncho in Israel change; check the bottle and your supplier.

> [!WARNING]
> Seal the shaker fully and hold both parts; an egg-white shake builds pressure, so break the seal away from your face and from guests. If you taste a real drink, make a half size, sip and spit, never before driving.

## Step by step

### White Lady

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Gin | 40 | 1⅓ |
| Triple sec | 30 | 1 |
| Fresh lemon juice | 20 | ⅔ |

- **Method:** shake well with ice, strain into a chilled cocktail glass (double strain to keep out ice chips).
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass, no ice.
- **Garnish:** none in the IBA spec; a lemon twist is common (professional judgment).
- **Profile and key technique:** dry gin, bright lemon and orange, lightly sweet. The key is the 30 ml of triple sec: it is the only sweetener, so measure it as exactly as the lemon.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — White Lady](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/white-lady/). Variation: [Difford's Guide White Lady](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/2091/white-lady) is 50 ml London dry gin, 22.5 ml triple sec, 22.5 ml lemon, 7.5 ml 2:1 syrup (optional), 15 ml pasteurised egg white (or a vegan alternative), optional saline and orange flower water; shaken with ice, strained back, dry shaken, fine strained into a chilled coupe, lemon twist.
- **Substitutions:** any triple sec (Cointreau is the classic); if the triple sec is less sweet, add 5 ml 1:1 syrup. Any London dry gin.
- **Home-practice version:** 40 ml cold water + 30 ml "triple sec stand-in" (20 ml fresh orange juice + 10 ml 1:1 syrup) + 20 ml lemon, shaken and double strained.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** an alcohol-free gin alternative (or chilled juniper-and-lemon-peel tea) with 25 ml orange juice, 10 ml 1:1 syrup and 20 ml lemon, shaken. Close in balance; lighter body.
- **Common mistakes:** adding syrup out of habit (too sweet); 30 ml lemon from Margarita muscle memory; warm glass.

### Sidecar

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Cognac | 50 | 1⅔ |
| Triple sec | 20 | ⅔ |
| Fresh lemon juice | 20 | ⅔ |

- **Method:** shake well with ice, strain into a chilled cocktail glass.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled cocktail glass, no ice.
- **Garnish:** none in the IBA spec; an orange or lemon twist is common.
- **Profile and key technique:** rich, fruity cognac with sharp lemon and orange. Equal triple sec and lemon makes it drier than the White Lady; do not "fix" it with syrup unless the house spec says so.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Sidecar](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/sidecar/). Variation: [Difford's Guide Sidecar](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/1773/sidecar-cocktail-diffords-spec) is 45 ml cognac, 15 ml triple sec, 15 ml Pineau des Charentes (a sweet, fortified grape aperitif), 15 ml lemon and 3 drops of saline; shaken, fine strained into a chilled coupe, orange twist. A sugar rim is an older serve (the page cites 1934) and now rare.
- **Substitutions:** another grape brandy for cognac (tell the guest); any triple sec.
- **Home-practice version:** 50 ml cold black tea (for colour and some tannin) + 20 ml triple sec stand-in (15 ml orange juice + 5 ml 1:1 syrup) + 20 ml lemon, shaken and strained.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** 50 ml cold rooibos or black tea with 3–4 drops of vanilla, 20 ml orange juice, 10 ml 1:1 syrup and 20 ml lemon, shaken. Fair in shape; no brandy warmth.
- **Common mistakes:** sugar rim all round the glass when not asked; a cheap, harsh brandy; under-shaking (it needs dilution to soften the cognac).

### Pisco Sour

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Pisco | 60 | 2 |
| Fresh lemon juice | 30 | 1 |
| Simple syrup (1:1) | 20 | ⅔ |
| Egg white (pasteurised) or aquafaba | 1 white (≈ 30) | 1 |

- **Method:** shake hard with ice and strain into the chilled glass (IBA). For a thicker foam, dry shake first or reverse dry shake; fine strain.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled goblet (a short stemmed glass) or coupe, no ice.
- **Garnish:** a few drops of Amargo bitters (or Angostura) on the foam.
- **Profile and key technique:** soft, sharp and fragrant: a silky body, a firm white foam and the spice of the bitters on the nose. The key is the foam: hard, long shake and a fine strain.
- **Spec source and variations:** [IBA — Pisco Sour](https://iba-world.com/iba-cocktail/pisco-sour/) (lists one raw egg white; this course uses pasteurised white or aquafaba). Variation: [Difford's Guide Pisco Sour](https://www.diffordsguide.com/cocktails/recipe/1563/pisco-sour) is 60 ml pisco, 15 ml lime, 7.5 ml lemon, 15 ml 2:1 syrup, 20 ml pasteurised egg white (or vegan alternative), optional 2.5 ml orange blossom water and 3 drops saline; shaken with ice, strained back, dry shaken, fine strained into a chilled coupe, 3 drops of Amargo Chuncho or Angostura.
- **Substitutions:** aquafaba 1:1 for egg white (vegan; tell the guest it contains chickpea); Angostura for Amargo; lime for part of the lemon. There is no faithful substitute for pisco; a young unaged grape brandy is closest (professional judgment).
- **Home-practice version:** 60 ml cold water + 30 ml lemon + 20 ml 1:1 syrup + 30 ml aquafaba, reverse dry shaken, fine strained, 3 drops of bitters (or a drop of strong cinnamon tea if you want it fully alcohol-free).
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** 60 ml white grape juice cut 1:1 with cold water, 25 ml lemon, 10 ml 1:1 syrup, 30 ml aquafaba, reverse dry shaken, cinnamon-tea drops on top. Close in texture; grape note without the brandy.
- **Common mistakes:** raw shell egg without a policy; a short shake (thin foam); bitters splashed and sunk; not telling the guest about egg.

### Reverse dry shake for a sour with foam

1. Measure everything, including the egg white or aquafaba, into the small tin.
2. Add ice, seal, shake hard 10–12 seconds.
3. Strain the drink into the other tin, throw away the ice.
4. Seal again (no ice) and shake hard 10 seconds; the foam builds fast because the drink is already cold.
5. Fine strain into the chilled glass; let it settle 10–20 seconds.
6. Drop the bitters from just above the surface.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A two-piece shaker (Boston or cobbler; a jar works for the first shake but leaks on a dry shake), a jigger, a Hawthorne and a fine (tea) strainer, three stemmed glasses, ice.
- 4 lemons, 2 oranges, 1:1 syrup, a can of chickpeas (for aquafaba) or a carton of pasteurised egg white, cold black tea, cold water, Angostura bitters (optional).
- A kitchen scale, a phone timer, a ruler, your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md) and three [recipe cards](../../templates/recipe-card.md).
- Low-cost alternative: aquafaba is almost free (it is the water from a can of chickpeas you would cook anyway).

> [!CAUTION]
> Cut citrus on a board with the knife moving away from your fingers. Hold stemmed glasses by the stem; cold, wet glass slips.

### Time

55 minutes: 10 to juice and set up, 30 for the three drinks (two of each), 15 to taste, measure and record.

### Steps

1. Write the three recipe cards from memory, then check them against the step-by-step section.
2. Juice and strain the lemons and oranges. Make the triple sec stand-in: 2 parts orange juice to 1 part 1:1 syrup.
3. **White Lady style (×2).** Shake 40 ml water, 30 ml stand-in, 20 ml lemon with ice, double strain. Time each drink from first pour to glass.
4. **Sidecar style (×2).** Shake 50 ml cold tea, 20 ml stand-in, 20 ml lemon, strain. Taste a White Lady and a Sidecar version side by side: which is sweeter, and why?
5. **Pisco Sour style (×2).** First version: wet shake only (ice, 15 seconds), fine strain. Second version: reverse dry shake as in the step-by-step. For each, measure the foam height in the glass with the ruler right away and after 5 minutes.
6. Drop 3 drops of bitters (or cinnamon tea) on each foam. Note which foam holds the drops on top.
7. Record times, foam heights and tasting notes in the practice log.
8. Run the simulation and pass all three cards.

### What you should see

The White Lady style tastes sweeter and more orange than the Sidecar style, because it has 30 ml of stand-in against 20 ml of lemon, while the Sidecar has equal amounts. Each shaken drink takes about 45–75 seconds once set up.

The wet-shake Pisco Sour style has a thin foam, a few millimetres, that fades within minutes. The reverse dry shake gives a much thicker, finer foam, often 1–2 cm, that still stands after 5 minutes; the bitters drops sit on it as dots. Aquafaba foam smells faintly of chickpea right after shaking; the lemon and bitters cover it in the finished drink.

White Lady style: 58 s and 49 s, "sweeter, orange-forward". Sidecar style: 52 s and 50 s, "drier, tea gives grip". Pisco Sour style with aquafaba: wet shake 4 mm foam, 1 mm after 5 minutes, drops sank; reverse dry shake 17 mm foam, 14 mm after 5 minutes, drops stayed as dots.

### What this practice cannot teach

Water and tea show balance and foam, but not how gin, cognac and pisco taste against triple sec, or how a strong spirit changes the foam; small, optional tastings and real service teach that.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding syrup to a triple-sec sour by habit.** Too sweet. Fix: the liqueur is the sweetener; add syrup only if the house spec says so.
- **Mixing up White Lady and Sidecar amounts.** Fix: remember "Lady 40-30-20, Sidecar 50-20-20".
- **Using raw shell egg.** A food-safety risk. Fix: pasteurised egg white or aquafaba, and tell the guest.
- **A weak foam.** Fix: shake longer and harder, use a dry or reverse dry shake, fine strain.
- **Bitters that sink.** Fix: drop them from just above the foam, and only after the foam has settled.
- **Ice chips in a drink served up.** Fix: double strain.

## Key takeaways

- White Lady (IBA): 40 ml gin, 30 ml triple sec, 20 ml lemon; shaken, chilled cocktail glass.
- Sidecar (IBA): 50 ml cognac, 20 ml triple sec, 20 ml lemon; shaken, chilled cocktail glass.
- Pisco Sour (IBA): 60 ml pisco, 30 ml lemon, 20 ml syrup, 1 egg white; shaken, chilled goblet, drops of Amargo bitters on the foam.
- In a sour, a liqueur can replace syrup; use about as much liqueur as citrus, or more.
- Use pasteurised egg white or aquafaba, shake for foam, and tell the guest about egg or chickpea.
