# Instructor notes — 06.6 Substitutions, adaptations and naming around the world

- **Objective:** substitute by role and adjust (2:1 = ⅔ of 1:1, sugar ↔ syrup); adapt for alcohol-free, lower alcohol and allergies; recognise naming differences and ask one clarifying question; know UK 25/35 ml measures and the 1 oz = 30 ml course convention.
- **Likely confusion:** "2:1 is twice as sweet". It is about 1.4× by weight (03.4), so use ⅔.
- **Misconception:** "a little of an allergen is fine" and "keep the classic's name after a swap". Both are wrong; remove or change the drink, and be honest about the name.
- **Expected hands-on result:** worksheet 6–7/8 (misses: scenario 5 nut allergy, 7 "straight up"); blind syrup test usually cannot tell 15 ml 1:1 from 10 ml 2:1; tea base gives more body than water.
- **Extension:** have the learner write an alcohol-free version of the Paper Plane and check that every product is 0.0% (most bitters and some "alcohol-free" aperitifs are not).
- **Note:** Israeli standard measure not found in this pass (stated as not verified); "straight up" ambiguity and lemon/lime 1:1 are labelled professional judgment.
