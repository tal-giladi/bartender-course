# 06.7 · Israeli bar menus: what is verified and what is judgment

Every country's bars have their own habits: which spirits sit in the well, which local serves guests expect, how menus are written. A new bartender in Israel is often told "everyone drinks X here" or "Israelis don't order Y". Some of that is true, some is one person's experience, and some is out of date. There is very little published data on what Israeli bars sell. This lesson does two things. It shows what can be backed by a named source today, including the two arak serves every Israeli bartender should know, and it teaches you to collect your own evidence from real menus and to label anything else honestly as professional judgment.

## Why it matters

In a job interview the manager asks: "What would you put on a summer menu for our bar?" Answering "arak with grapefruit is popular" is fine if you can say who reports it. Answering "Israelis only drink sweet cocktails" is a guess presented as a fact. Bars plan menus, orders and prices on these beliefs; a bartender who separates evidence from opinion makes better suggestions and earns trust.

## Core ideas

### Three kinds of statement

| Kind | What it means | Example from this lesson | How to write it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified | a named, checkable source supports it, with a date | an Israeli bar (Imperial Craft, Tel Aviv) was No. 17 on the 2015 World's 50 Best Bars list (JTA, 2015) | the claim, then the source and date |
| Professional judgment | experience or observation, not published data | "many Tel Aviv menus are in both Hebrew and English" | the claim, then "(professional judgment)" |
| Unknown | nobody you can name has measured it | "the best-selling cocktail in Israel" | say it is not known; do not guess |

Popularity, "what Israelis like" and sales are the claims most often stated without evidence. Course rule: no source, no fact.

### What can be backed by a source

**Arak is the local anise spirit, and it has a classic serve.** Arak is a grape spirit flavoured with aniseed ([lesson 02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)). The wine writer Adam Montefiore (2019) names Israeli producers and brands, including Kawar (distillery near Nazareth, opened 2010), its El Pasha brand, Elite, Alouf and Askalon. He describes the classic serve: one part arak to two parts water, ice added only after the water, in a small, narrow glass, with mint as an optional garnish, often as an aperitif or with mezze. He also suggests the simpler El Pasha for mixing with lemon or grapefruit juice.

**Arak with grapefruit juice.** Saveur, a US food magazine, describes a mix of arak and fresh grapefruit juice as a popular refresher on the beaches of Tel Aviv, and gives a 1 : 2 recipe (2 oz arak, 4 oz grapefruit juice, ice, a basil leaf). This is a single foreign magazine's description, not sales data, but it matches the Montefiore article's mixing suggestion.

**Tel Aviv has internationally ranked cocktail bars.** JTA reported in 2015 that Imperial Craft Cocktail Bar in Tel Aviv was No. 17 on the World's 50 Best Bars list published by Drinks International, the first Israeli bar on the list. Time Out Israel reported in 2017 that it placed No. 50 and won the Best Bar in the Middle East & Africa award. These are dated facts about one bar, not evidence of what every Israeli bar serves.

**There are Israeli craft spirits.** Time Out Israel lists Milk & Honey (M&H), a whisky distillery in Jaffa founded in 2012 ([lesson 02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). Local products give a menu a story; check what your supplier actually stocks.

**Limonana (mint lemonade) has an Israeli name.** Wikipedia's article on mint lemonade says the Hebrew name *limonana* blends the words for lemon (*limon*) and mint (*nana*), and attributes the claim that it is very popular in Israel to a single source (Food52). It is a natural alcohol-free option on an Israeli menu; the popularity claim is weakly sourced.

### What is professional judgment

These statements are common among Israeli bartenders but this course found no published data for them. Use them as starting points, not facts (all professional judgment, 2026-10-17):

- Many bar menus in Tel Aviv, Haifa and Jerusalem are bilingual, Hebrew and English, and cocktail names usually stay in their original language, transliterated in Hebrew (קוסמופוליטן, *Cosmopolitan*).
- Arak is often ordered as a long drink with grapefruit juice or with lemonade and mint, and as a shot.
- Shots (צ'ייסר, *chaser*, is the common Israeli word for a shot of spirit) are a large part of late-night sales in many bars.
- Some bars and most hotels hold kosher certification, which can affect which products they may stock and their opening hours on Shabbat and holidays.
- Prices on Israeli menus are shown including VAT; Module 10 checks the rule and the current rate on official sources.

### A small sample is not "the market"

When you survey menus, you learn what those bars offer, on that date. Three menus from Tel Aviv cocktail bars tell you nothing about a pub in Be'er Sheva. Write conclusions in the size of your evidence: "2 of the 3 menus I checked had an arak drink", not "Israeli bars love arak".

> [!NOTE]
> Sources checked on 2026-10-17. Rankings, brands, distilleries and menus change quickly; the Imperial Craft rankings are from 2015 and 2017. Re-check before you quote any of this in an interview or on a menu.

## Step by step

### Arak with water

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Arak | 30 | 1 |
| Cold water | 60 | 2 |
| Ice | a few cubes, added last | — |

- **Method:** build: arak first, then the water (it turns milky white), then the ice.
- **Glass and ice:** small, narrow glass; a few ice cubes added after the water.
- **Garnish:** a mint sprig (optional).
- **Profile and key technique:** dry, cool anise, softened by water. The key is the order: water before ice. The source does not say why; a common explanation is that ice added to neat arak can make the anise oils set into a film (professional judgment).
- **Spec source and variations:** [Adam Montefiore — Arak success story (2019)](https://adammontefiore.com/arak-success-story): 1 part arak to 2 parts water, or up to 1 : 5 for a longer drink, ice after water, small narrow glasses, mint optional. No IBA spec exists. The 30 ml pour is the course's example; the house pour decides (professional judgment).
- **Substitutions:** ouzo or raki behave similarly (see [lesson 02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)); tell the guest, they are different spirits.
- **Home-practice version:** 30 ml water with 2–3 drops of anise or fennel extract (or cold fennel tea), then 60 ml water, then ice. Trains the order and the pour.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** cold fennel or anise-seed tea over ice with mint. Fair in aroma; no louche (no milky effect).
- **Common mistakes:** ice first; a big pour served "neat on ice" when the guest wanted it with water; a sweet anise liqueur (sambuca) instead of arak.

### Arak and grapefruit

| Ingredient | ml | oz |
|---|---|---|
| Arak | 60 | 2 |
| Fresh grapefruit juice (chilled) | 120 | 4 |

- **Method:** build: pour grapefruit juice and arak into the glass, fill with ice.
- **Glass and ice:** chilled glass, filled with ice (Saveur names a chilled cocktail glass; a highball is a common bar serve, professional judgment).
- **Garnish:** a basil leaf.
- **Profile and key technique:** bittersweet, juicy grapefruit with cool anise. The key is fresh, cold juice.
- **Spec source and variations:** [Saveur — Arak and Grapefruit](https://www.saveur.com/article/Recipes/Arak-and-Grapefruit/) (2 oz arak, 4 oz chilled fresh grapefruit juice, ice, basil). No IBA spec exists. Montefiore (2019) suggests a simpler arak such as El Pasha for mixing with grapefruit or lemon juice. Many bars pour less arak (for example 40–50 ml) and top up with juice (professional judgment).
- **Substitutions:** pink or white grapefruit; ouzo if there is no arak (Saveur suggests it), and tell the guest.
- **Home-practice version:** 60 ml cold fennel tea + 120 ml grapefruit juice, ice, basil.
- **Alcohol-free analogue:** grapefruit juice with a splash of soda, a pinch of salt and mint or basil. Close in freshness; no anise.
- **Common mistakes:** bottled sweetened juice (too sweet); warm juice melting the ice fast; forgetting that this is a 60 ml pour of a strong spirit (arak is often around 45–53% ABV in the brands Montefiore lists).

### Turn a claim into a checked statement

1. Write the claim in one sentence.
2. Ask: who says so? Find a named source: a producer, a dated news report, an official body, the bar's own menu.
3. Check the date and what the source actually measured (one bar? one beach? sales?).
4. Rewrite the claim to the size of the evidence, with source and date.
5. If you find nothing, label it "professional judgment" or drop it.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A computer or phone, the CSV [labs/module-06/israeli-menu-survey.csv](../../labs/module-06/israeli-menu-survey.csv) (open it in any spreadsheet app) and the claims sort [labs/module-06/menu-claims-sort.md](../../labs/module-06/menu-claims-sort.md).
- For the optional builds: a glass, ice, grapefruit juice, fennel tea or anise extract, mint or basil.
- Low-cost alternative: paper and pen instead of a spreadsheet.

> [!WARNING]
> Arak is strong. If you taste a real arak serve, make a half size, sip slowly, and never before driving. The home-practice versions are enough for this lesson.

### Time

60 minutes: 15 for the claims sort, 35 for the menu survey, 10 for the optional builds and the write-up.

### Steps

1. **Claims sort.** Label each of the 12 statements in the lab file verified, professional judgment or unknown, then check [menu-claims-sort-solution.md](../../labs/module-06/menu-claims-sort-solution.md).
2. **Pick three bars** in Israel that publish a drinks menu online (their own website or social media page). Try to choose different kinds: a cocktail bar, a pub, a restaurant bar.
3. **Record each drink** on the menus in the CSV, one row per drink: bar, city, source URL, date accessed, drink name as written, language(s), family ([lesson 06.1](lesson-01.md)), base spirit, any Israeli product or local ingredient, alcohol-free (yes/no).
4. **Count.** How many drinks per family? How many menus have an arak drink, an Israeli product, an alcohol-free cocktail?
5. **Write three sentences** of findings, each sized to your evidence ("2 of 3 menus…").
6. Optional: make the home-practice arak serves in the right order (water before ice).

### What you should see

The claims sort usually goes quickly for clear cases; the hard ones are claims that sound obvious ("arak is popular") but where the only sources are a magazine description or a writer's experience. Those are partly supported: say exactly what the source said.

In the survey, expect cocktail bars to list 8–15 signature drinks plus classics, often with names in English and descriptions in Hebrew or both, and pubs to list few cocktails (professional judgment; your sample decides). Many menus mix families; sours and highballs usually dominate. Write down what you found, including surprises.

### What this practice cannot teach

Menus show what bars offer, not what guests actually order; sales data stays inside the bar's till, and you only learn it by working there or asking the manager.

## Common mistakes

- **Turning one article into a national fact.** Fix: say who, where and when: "Saveur describes it as popular on Tel Aviv beaches".
- **Quoting old rankings as current.** Fix: give the year; check the current list.
- **Treating your own experience as data.** Fix: label it professional judgment.
- **Ice before water in arak.** Fix: arak, water, then ice.
- **A survey without dates or links.** Nobody can check it. Fix: URL and access date on every row.

## Key takeaways

- Every claim about Israeli menus is verified (named source and date), professional judgment, or unknown; popularity claims need a source.
- Arak with water: 1 part arak to 2 parts water, ice after the water, small narrow glass (Montefiore, 2019).
- Arak and grapefruit: about 1 : 2 arak to fresh grapefruit juice over ice (Saveur).
- Dated facts: Imperial Craft, Tel Aviv, No. 17 (2015) and No. 50 (2017) on the World's 50 Best Bars; M&H whisky distillery in Jaffa since 2012.
- Your own menu survey is evidence about those bars on that date; write findings to that size.
