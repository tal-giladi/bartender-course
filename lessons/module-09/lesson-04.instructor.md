# Instructor notes — 09.4 Responsible service: intoxication and refusing service

- **Objective:** signs by speech, balance, coordination, behaviour; green-amber-red with actions; grams and standard drinks; eight-step refusal; strong-drink requests; drivers; alcohol poisoning (101); Penal Law s. 193(c).
- **Likely confusion:** standard drinks vs glasses. Many learners count three cocktails as three drinks; the arithmetic shows five or six.
- **Misconception:** "refusing is the manager's job". Penal Law s. 193(c) names workers as well as owners; the bartender pouring the drink is responsible. Refuse with the manager's support, not instead of it.
- **Expected hands-on result:** red cards sorted correctly; hesitation on amber (card 2, cheerful shot-buyer; card 9). Card 6 tests that disability or slow speech is not assumed to be alcohol. All five calculated drinks exceed one standard drink. First video takes usually include "you're drunk" or backing down at the second push.
- **Extension:** run refusal situation 1 with the partner escalating to anger; the learner practises safety (bar between them, manager, 100).
- **Note on sources:** no Israeli official guide to recognising intoxication was found (2026-10-18); NSW official guidance is used as a practical standard and labelled as such. Standard drink: gov.il uses the U.S. 14 g figure; no official Israeli definition found.
- **Safety-critical:** continuing to serve a clearly intoxicated guest is a safety-critical failure (11.1, 11.5); the `intoxicated` and `strong` simulations flag it.
