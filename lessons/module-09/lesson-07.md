# 09.7 · Guest scenarios: seven situations, practised

This is Module 9's practical piece. Seven situations every bartender meets, sooner or later, come together here: a guest who may be under 18, a guest who has had too much, an order that makes no sense, a declared allergy, a complaint, a request for an unusually strong drink, and a request for something without alcohol. For each one you first run a branching conversation in the guest-scenarios simulation, then write your own response in full, and score it with a rubric. The point is not to memorise lines; it is to make the safe, professional choice automatic before a real guest is in front of you.

## Why it matters

On a trial shift, the manager may not ask you to make a single complicated cocktail, but they will watch what you do when a young-looking group walks in, when someone slurs an order, or when a guest says "I'm allergic". Three of the safety-critical failures in this course's final assessment (lesson 11.1) live in these situations: serving a possible minor without an ID check, continuing to serve an intoxicated guest, and ignoring a declared allergy or guessing ingredients. The strong and alcohol-free requests hide safety traps too (over-pouring, hidden alcohol), and every one of the seven decides whether guests trust you. Lesson 11.5 repeats all seven in random order as an exam; this lesson is where you practise them.

## Core ideas

### How the simulation scores you

The guest-scenarios simulation is an educational model. Each scenario is one conversation with four decision points; at each point you choose one of three replies or actions. Each choice scores 2 (safe and professional), 1 (safe but weak) or 0 (poor). Some 0-choices are also flagged **safety-critical**.

- Score % = points ÷ 8 × 100.
- A scenario passes with **at least 70% (6 of 8 points) and no safety-critical choice**.
- After each scenario you see your path, the model path and why.

### The seven situations

| Preset | Situation | Main rule | Type | Safety-critical choices |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `underage` | young-looking guest orders two beers; a friend vouches; first "ID" is a phone photo | no sale or serving to under-18s; presumption unless ID shown (Penal Law s. 193A); ID required to drink (licence condition) ([09.5](lesson-05.md)) | statutory + licence condition | serving without a physical ID showing 18+; serving the friend's second beer to the young guest |
| `intoxicated` | regular guest, slurring, swaying, wants another round and shots for the table | no supply to a drunk person or encouraging them (Penal Law s. 193(c)) ([09.4](lesson-04.md)) | statutory; signs from official guidance | serving any alcohol to him; letting him drive; leaving him alone if he becomes unwell |
| `unclear` | "a Martini, not too strong, sweet but dry, vodka or whatever" | repeat, name the conflict, two options, check back ([09.2](lesson-02.md)) | professional judgment | none |
| `allergy` | guest with a tree-nut allergy orders a Mai Tai | never guess; check, tell, clean set ([09.3](lesson-03.md)) | professional standard; packaged-label rules statutory | guessing; serving orgeat; using an unwashed shaker after orgeat |
| `complaint` | Margarita sent back as "too sour" | listen, apologise, solve, thank ([09.2](lesson-02.md)) | professional judgment | none |
| `strong` | sober guest wants "the strongest thing you have, triple everything" | house measures; never encourage heavy drinking ([09.4](lesson-04.md)) | house policy + statutory if intoxicated | pouring above house spec; encouraging fast or heavy drinking; serving if signs of intoxication appear |
| `alcohol-free` | guest says she is pregnant and wants "something festive without alcohol" | Ministry of Health: avoid alcohol in pregnancy; say the ABV of anything not 0.0% ([07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)) | official guidance | serving a drink with alcohol (including bitters or a 0.5% beer) without telling her |

### The written response and the rubric

For each situation you also write what you would say (in quotation marks) and do, step by step. Score each written response with the [scenario rubric](../../labs/module-09/scenario-rubric.md):

| Criterion | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safety and law | the safe choice, with the right rule | safe but rule unclear or late | unsafe or illegal |
| Guest care | polite, private, no blame, offers an alternative | polite but no alternative | rude, public, or blaming |
| Accuracy | correct facts (spec, allergen, ABV, age) | minor slip | wrong fact that matters |
| Team | manager involved where needed, log written | partly | ignored |
| Clarity | short, specific words a guest understands | wordy or vague | confusing |

A written response passes with **at least 7 of 10 and a 2 in Safety and law**. A 0 in Safety and law is a safety-critical failure whatever the total.

> [!NOTE]
> The scoring model and the scenarios are this course's educational model (professional judgment), not an official standard. Legal references were checked on 2026-10-18 (Penal Law on Nevo, updated to 2 July 2026; Ministry of Health allergen guide, 29 June 2025; Ministry of Health pregnancy page). House policies on IDs, doubles and comps vary.

## Step by step

### For each of the seven situations

1. Read the situation card on the [response sheet](../../labs/module-09/scenario-response-sheet.md).
2. Write your response first, before the simulation: exact words in quotation marks, then actions, in order.
3. Run the simulation preset for that situation.
4. Compare your written answer with the simulation's model path; improve your written answer if needed (mark what you changed).
5. Score the written answer with the rubric; record the simulation score.
6. Move to the next situation.

### After all seven

1. Compare with the [model answers](../../labs/module-09/scenario-model-answers-solution.md).
2. Pick your two weakest situations.
3. Rerun those two presets and rewrite those two answers.
4. Record everything in your practice log and the summary table on the response sheet.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- The [scenario response sheet](../../labs/module-09/scenario-response-sheet.md), the [scenario rubric](../../labs/module-09/scenario-rubric.md) and, after you finish, the [model answers](../../labs/module-09/scenario-model-answers-solution.md).
- A computer or phone for the simulation, paper or a notes app, a timer, and your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Optional: a partner to play the guests from the cards, a phone to film yourself, and for the alcohol-free situation a jug, ice, soda water, citrus and a syrup to build the drink you write.
- Low-cost alternative: if the simulation is not available, do the written responses and role-play the cards with a partner, scoring with the rubric only.

8. **Score and compare.** Score each written answer with the rubric, fill in the summary table, then read the model answers.

10. **Optional role-play.** With a partner reading the guest's lines, say three of your answers aloud and film them; check tone, length and whether you offered an alternative.

### What you should see

A first run typically passes the clear cases (complaint, unclear order) and loses points on the safety ones through small slips: accepting the phone photo, serving the friend's beer that goes to the minor, using the Mai Tai shaker, or forgetting that bitters contain alcohol (professional judgment). Any safety-critical flag means that scenario fails even with 7 of 8 points: rerun it until you pass cleanly. Target: all seven passed in the simulation and all seven written answers at 7/10 or more with a 2 in Safety and law. If you pass everything on the first attempt, do the role-play with a partner who argues back.

### What this practice cannot teach

A simulation and a written sheet cannot reproduce a real person's face, voice, pressure or anger, the noise of a full bar, or the moment you must decide alone; they make the right choice familiar so that on a real shift you can spend your attention on the guest, and you should still ask your manager whenever a situation goes beyond what you practised.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the simulation as a quiz to click through.** Fix: write your own answer first; the writing is what you will say on a shift.
- **Passing on points but failing on safety.** A safety-critical flag fails the scenario. Fix: in every situation, ask first "what is the unsafe choice here?"
- **Forgetting the alternative.** Refusing without offering water, a soft drink or another cocktail feels like rejection. Fix: every refusal ends with an offer.
- **Missing hidden alcohol.** Bitters, a 0.5% beer or a liqueur rinse in an "alcohol-free" drink. Fix: say the ABV of anything that is not 0.0%, or leave it out ([lesson 07.5](../module-07/lesson-05.md)).
- **Not involving the manager.** Fix: minors with doubtful ID, intoxicated guests, allergy reactions and money questions all go to the manager, and into the log.

## Key takeaways

- Seven situations, one method: notice, check the rule, act safely, offer an alternative, involve the team, log it.
- Five situations carry safety-critical choices: possible minor, intoxicated guest, allergy, and (through over-pouring or hidden alcohol) the strong and alcohol-free requests.
- A scenario passes only with at least 70% and no safety-critical choice; a written answer needs 7/10 and a 2 in Safety and law.
- Know which rules are statutory (minors, drunk guests), which are licence conditions (ID to drink, signs) and which are house policy (ID age, doubles, comps).
- Practise your two weakest situations again until the safe answer is automatic; lesson 11.5 tests all seven in random order.
