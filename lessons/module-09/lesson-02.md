# 09.2 · Complaints, substitutions and unclear orders

Sooner or later a guest will push a drink back across the bar and say "This is wrong." How you react in the next thirty seconds decides whether they leave angry or come back next week. This lesson gives you a calm, four-step way to handle complaints, a simple rule for when to adjust, remake or replace a drink, the honest way to substitute when something has run out, and a method for orders that do not make sense as spoken. In the hands-on part you write responses to eight cases, compare them with model answers, and practise the complaint and unclear-order conversations in the guest-scenarios simulation.

## Why it matters

A guest sends back a Margarita: "Way too sour." One bartender says, "That's the classic recipe," and the guest feels stupid. Another says, "Sorry about that, let me fix it. Would you like it a little sweeter, or should I make you something else?" Same drink, same spec, completely different evening. Complaints are also information: a run of "too sour" may mean your lime juice is fresher than usual or your syrup was made wrong ([lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)). Handling the guest well and checking the cause are both part of the job.

## Core ideas

### Why guests complain

| Type | Examples | Usually caused by |
|---|---|---|
| Taste | too sour, too sweet, too strong, "can't taste the alcohol" | spec not to the guest's taste, wrong measure, bad syrup or juice |
| Temperature and texture | warm, watery, flat | drink waited too long, wrong ice, old soda ([lesson 08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md)) |
| Wrong drink | modifier missed, wrong spirit, wrong glass | no check-back ([lesson 09.1](lesson-01.md)) |
| Wait | "we've been waiting 20 minutes" | queue, not acknowledged |
| Bill | wrong item, wrong tab | not rung in at once |
| Something unsafe | glass chip, foreign object, allergy concern | stop at once (see below) |

### Listen, apologise, solve, thank

A simple four-step model used in hospitality (professional judgment; many venues teach a version called LAST):

1. **Listen** to the end. Do not interrupt or explain the recipe. Ask one question if you need to: "Too sour, or too strong?"
2. **Apologise** for the experience, not as an admission that the guest is right about the recipe: "Sorry it's not what you hoped for." In Hebrew: סליחה, אני מצטער / מצטערת (*slicha, ani mitzta'er / mitzta'eret*, sorry, I'm sorry, said by a man / woman).
3. **Solve** with a clear offer, usually two choices: "I can make it a little sweeter, or make you something else."
4. **Thank** them for telling you: "Thanks for letting me know." Then check in again after a few sips.

Never argue, never say "nobody else complains", and never blame a colleague in front of a guest. Problems inside the team are discussed after service ([lesson 08.7](../module-08/lesson-07.md)).

### Adjust, remake or replace

| Situation | What you do | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| Made correctly, guest wants it a bit sweeter or sourer | **Adjust**: remake with a small change (for example +5 ml syrup), not by topping up their glass | you |
| Made wrong (missed modifier, wrong measure, dead drink) | **Remake** to spec, at once, at no charge | you |
| Guest simply does not like the drink | **Replace** with something they choose; whether it is charged follows house policy | manager or house rule |
| Guest asks for a refund, a discount or a free round | Fetch the manager; do not promise | manager |
| Guest says "too weak" and wants more alcohol free | Explain politely that you pour the house measure; offer a double at the double price if they are not intoxicated (lesson 09.4) | house rule |
| Something unsafe: glass chip, foreign object, possible allergen | Remove the drink, apologise, tell the manager, check the batch, glassware or ice; for any reaction follow lesson 09.3 | you, then manager |

Remake into a clean glass with fresh ice, never by adding to the old drink. Bin the old one and log it in the waste log ([lesson 08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md)) with the reason. Taste-check the remake with a straw before serving ([lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md)), never from the guest's glass.

> [!WARNING]
> A chip in a glass means the missing piece may be in the drink or the ice. Remove the drink, check the guest's mouth is not cut (offer first aid if needed), and burn the ice well if there is any chance the glass broke over it ([lesson 01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)).

### Substitutions: honest and before you pour

When an ingredient runs out (an **86**, [lesson 08.7](../module-08/lesson-07.md)) or a guest asks for a change, substitute by the ingredient's role ([lesson 06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md)) and tell the guest first:

- "We're out of Cointreau. I can make your Margarita with another triple sec; it's a little sweeter. Is that OK?"
- "No Prosecco tonight. I can make your spritz with cava, a drier Spanish sparkling wine, or if you'd like something bitter without bubbles, a Negroni."

Three rules: never substitute silently; never substitute an ingredient in an allergy order without checking the new product's label ([lesson 09.3](lesson-03.md)); and if no faithful substitute exists, say so and offer a different drink. A drink that changes its main spirit or character is a different drink and should be named as such (professional judgment).

### Unclear and contradictory orders

Some orders cannot be made as spoken: "a Martini, sweet but dry"; "something strong that doesn't taste strong"; "a Mojito without mint"; "a whiskey sour, no sour". Others have two meanings, such as "Martini" ([lesson 06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md)). In Israel, a guest asking for מרטיני (*martini*) may mean the cocktail or a glass of Martini-brand vermouth on ice (professional judgment). The method:

1. **Repeat** what you heard, without correcting the guest's words.
2. **Name the conflict** gently, or the two meanings: "Dry usually means very little vermouth, so less sweet. Do you mean a softer, less boozy Martini?"
3. **Offer two concrete options**, one line each: "I can make a Martini with more vermouth, softer and a bit sweeter, or a vodka-based French Martini, fruity and sweet."
4. **Check back** the final choice before you pour.

"Sweet", "dry", "strong" and "light" mean different things to different guests; the table in [lesson 07.6](../module-07/lesson-06.md) helps you translate them. If the guest cannot decide, make a small taste of each element only if house policy allows (professional judgment).

### Staying safe with an angry guest

Most complaints are calm. If a guest raises their voice, insults staff or threatens anyone: keep the bar between you, lower your own voice, do not touch them, and call the manager or security. Do not serve more alcohol to a guest who is aggressive and possibly intoxicated (lesson 09.4). In an emergency, Israel Police is 100 (professional judgment: well-known public number).

> [!NOTE]
> The four-step complaint model, the adjust/remake/replace table and the Hebrew phrases are professional judgment; venues set their own rules on comps, refunds and charging for replacements. Specs follow the IBA list (checked 2026-10-18).

## Step by step

### Handle a complaint about a drink

1. Stop what you are doing, face the guest and listen to the end.
2. Ask one clarifying question if needed ("too sour, or too strong?").
3. Apologise for the experience.
4. Offer two solutions (adjust or something else; remake if it was wrong).
5. Bin the old drink; remake into a clean glass with fresh ice; taste-check with a straw.
6. Serve, thank the guest, and check in after a few sips.
7. Log the waste and reason; tell the manager if it was a quality problem (juice, syrup, glassware).

### Substitute when something is out

1. Choose a substitute with the same role (base, sweetener, acid, modifier).
2. Tell the guest what changes and how it tastes, before you pour.
3. If an allergy is involved, read the substitute's label first.
4. If there is no faithful substitute, offer a different drink by name.

### Clarify an unclear order

1. Repeat what you heard.
2. Name the conflict or the two meanings.
3. Offer two concrete drinks, one line each.
4. Check back the final choice.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- The case sheet [labs/module-09/complaint-cases.md](../../labs/module-09/complaint-cases.md): eight complaints, substitutions and unclear orders. Model answers are in [labs/module-09/complaint-cases-solution.md](../../labs/module-09/complaint-cases-solution.md); do not open them until you have written yours.
- Paper or a notes app, a phone to record yourself, and your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- For the optional practical part: a shaker or jar, jigger or measuring spoons, water, lemon or lime juice and 1:1 sugar syrup.
- Low-cost alternative: skip the practical part and do the written and spoken parts only.

### Time

45 minutes: 20 to write the eight responses, 10 for the simulation, 15 for the spoken practice and the optional adjustment drill.

### Steps

1. **Write.** For each case, write exactly what you would say (in quotation marks) and what you would do, using listen, apologise, solve, thank for complaints, and repeat, name, two options, check back for unclear orders.
2. **Compare** with the model answers. Mark each of your answers on three points: did you avoid arguing or blaming; did you offer a clear solution or two concrete options; did you route money decisions (refunds, free drinks) to the manager?
3. **Simulate.** Run the complaint and unclear-order scenarios. Aim for at least 6 of 8 points in each.

[Simulation: Guest scenarios — complaint](../../simulations/guest-scenarios/index.html?preset=complaint)

[Simulation: Guest scenarios — unclear order](../../simulations/guest-scenarios/index.html?preset=unclear)

4. **Speak.** Record yourself saying your answers to cases 1, 4 and 6 out loud, as if to a guest. Play it back: is your tone calm and friendly, and is each answer under about 20 seconds?
5. **Optional adjustment drill.** Shake a non-alcoholic sour base: 60 ml cold water, 20 ml citrus juice, 15 ml 1:1 syrup. Taste with a straw. Then make it again with +5 ml syrup, and again with +5 ml citrus instead. Note how much a 5 ml change shifts the balance ([lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md)). This is the size of change you offer a guest who finds a drink "too sour".
6. Log your simulation scores and one thing you will say differently next time.

### What you should see

Most first drafts explain the recipe to the guest ("that's how it's made") or promise a free drink without asking the manager; the model answers show the difference (professional judgment). In the adjustment drill, 5 ml of syrup or citrus is clearly noticeable in a short drink: that is why you remake with a small change rather than topping up the old glass. If your simulation score is below 6 of 8, look at which step you skipped: usually the clarifying question or the check-back.

### What this practice cannot teach

Writing a calm answer at home is easy; staying calm when a real guest is loud, the queue is long and you think they are wrong takes practice on real shifts, ideally first with a colleague or manager nearby.

## Common mistakes

- **Defending the recipe.** "That's the classic spec" makes the guest wrong. Fix: apologise for the experience and offer to adjust or replace.
- **Topping up the old drink.** Adding syrup or spirit to a returned glass is unhygienic and imprecise. Fix: bin it, remake in a clean glass, log the waste.
- **Promising money you cannot give.** Fix: say "Let me get my manager" for refunds, discounts and free rounds.
- **Silent substitutions.** Fix: tell the guest what changes before you pour; with allergies, read the label first.
- **Guessing an unclear order.** Fix: repeat, name the conflict, offer two concrete drinks, check back.
- **Arguing with an angry guest.** Fix: lower your voice, keep the bar between you, call the manager.

## Key takeaways

- Complaints are handled in four steps: listen, apologise, solve, thank, then check in again.
- Adjust or remake into a clean glass; never top up the returned drink; log the waste.
- Refunds, discounts and free drinks are the manager's call.
- Substitute by role and always tell the guest first; with an allergy, read the new label.
- For unclear orders: repeat, name the conflict or meanings, offer two concrete options, check back.
