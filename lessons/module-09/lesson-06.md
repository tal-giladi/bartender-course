# 09.6 · Food safety and workplace safety

A bar is a food business and a workplace at the same time. The ice, juice, garnish and glasses you handle reach guests' mouths, and the glass, knives, wet floors, hot water, chemicals and gas around you can hurt you and your colleagues. Earlier lessons taught the habits (hygiene and reset in lesson 01.7, ice in 01.6, prep and storage in 08.2, equipment in 08.6). This lesson adds what Israeli rules say: the Ministry of Health conditions written into food-business licences, the employer's legal duty to train you about workplace risks (updated two days before this lesson's research date), and how to report incidents. In the hands-on part you audit your home station against the licence conditions, log a fridge's temperature and write an incident report.

## Why it matters

A health inspector visits on a Friday afternoon. She opens the garnish fridge (8 °C), asks for the temperature records (there are none for last month), and watches a bartender with a bandaged, ungloved cut slicing limes. Each of those is a licence condition, not a matter of taste. The same afternoon a barback slips on melted ice carrying a rack of glasses. Food safety and workplace safety are not paperwork: they are what keeps guests from getting ill and staff from getting hurt, and a bar's licence depends on them.

## Core ideas

### Where Israeli food-hygiene rules for bars come from

A bar's business licence comes with conditions from each authority that approves it ([lesson 09.5](lesson-05.md)). For a restaurant or bar-restaurant that serves food and alcohol (licensing item 4.2), the published uniform specification contains a Ministry of Health chapter, and it requires the business to follow the Ministry's guide to food handling in large kitchens (May 2008), attached as an annex. These are **licence conditions**: binding on the licensed business. For a pub whose main activity is serving alcohol (item 4.8), this course read the police conditions; it did not find a separate Ministry of Health chapter in that document (checked 2026-10-18), so ask your venue which conditions its licence carries. The numbers below are the 4.2 conditions; they match the international guidance used in Module 8.

### Temperatures and records

| What | Condition (item 4.2, Ministry of Health chapter) |
|---|---|
| Chilled food | kept at **+5 °C or below** |
| Frozen food | **−18 °C or below** |
| Hot food | **+65 °C or above** |
| Chilled deliveries | arrive at +5 °C or below; frozen at −18 °C; temperature checked and written on the delivery note |
| Fridges and freezers | an indicating thermometer and a recording thermometer; temperature records kept **at least three months** |
| Storage | food covered and protected in the fridge; air must circulate; doors closed |
| Water | drinking-water quality for use in the business; where ice is used with food, it is made from drinking-quality water |

For a bar: juice, syrups, cut garnish, dairy and egg white in fridges at 5 °C or below; a thermometer in every fridge; readings written in the temperature log at opening and closing ([lesson 08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) and the log kept for at least three months. Ice is food ([lesson 01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)): scoop only, never a glass, never hands.

### Personal hygiene, illness and wounds

The same chapter sets conditions for food workers (licence conditions):

- clean work clothes, changed at least daily; hair tied back and covered in food-preparation areas;
- clean, short nails without polish; no jewellery except a wedding ring;
- hand washing with soap and water before work, after the toilet and when moving between tasks;
- **no work with food while sick with an infectious disease, or with open or infected wounds**; return after an infectious illness only with a doctor's approval; go for a medical check if the Ministry requires it;
- workers trained in food handling, and at least one senior worker who completed food-quality training (Ministry of Health programme or HACCP, a system for finding and controlling food-safety hazards).

Behind a bar this means (professional judgment applying the conditions): tell your manager before the shift if you have vomiting, diarrhoea or fever; cover a small cut with a waterproof plaster and a glove, and do not cut garnish or handle ice with an open wound; wash your hands at the hand-wash sink, not the glass sink, every time you return to the bar ([lesson 01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md)).

### Cross-contamination at the bar

**Cross-contamination** is the transfer of bacteria, allergens or chemicals into food or drink from somewhere they should not be. The main routes behind a bar:

| Route | Control |
|---|---|
| Hands after money, phones, bins, the toilet | wash hands; tongs for garnish |
| Raw food (kitchen) next to garnish | separate boards and containers; garnish covered and labelled |
| Allergens | clean set for allergy orders ([lesson 09.3](lesson-03.md)); orgeat, dairy and egg labelled and separate |
| Chemicals | stored below and away from ice, glass and garnish; never in drink bottles; never mixed |
| Dirty glassware | glasswasher at the right temperature; inspect every glass ([lesson 08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)) |
| Broken glass | burn the ice well; never scoop with a glass ([lesson 01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)) |

### Workplace safety: what the employer must do

Israeli law puts the duty to keep a workplace safe on the employer and the person in charge of the workplace (the "holder"). One regulation every new worker should know about is the **Work Supervision Organisation (Information and Training of Workers) Regulations 1999** (statutory):

- the holder of the workplace must give workers information about the risks at work and training on how to prevent them, and record it in a **training log** (פנקס הדרכה, *pinkas hadracha*);
- under an amendment published on 14 July 2026 and in force from **16 October 2026**, the training must normally be **face to face, in a language the worker understands**, given by a qualified professional who signs a declaration; online or e-learning training is allowed only where a safety officer has been appointed and approves it; the training log now records each trained worker's ID number;
- notices about workplace safety must be posted in Hebrew and in another language understood by most of the workers.

On your first shift (professional judgment): ask to be shown the glasswasher, ice machine, CO2 and gas, chemicals and their safety data, the first-aid kit, fire exits and extinguishers, and how to report an injury; sign the training log when you are trained; and do not use equipment you have not been shown ([lesson 08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)).

### Everyday hazards, briefly

| Hazard | Control (from earlier lessons) |
|---|---|
| Wet, sticky floors | non-slip shoes, mats, mop at once, "wet floor" sign |
| Glass and knives | call-outs, cutting board on a damp cloth, broken-glass routine ([lessons 01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md), 01.6, [08.7](../module-08/lesson-07.md)) |
| Hot water and steam | open the glasswasher and step back |
| Chemicals | gloves and eye protection as labelled; never mixed |
| CO2 and cellar gas | alarm procedure; never enter a cellar with the alarm sounding ([lesson 08.6](../module-08/lesson-06.md)) |
| Heavy loads, kegs | trolley, two people, roll not lift |
| Aggressive guests | bar between you, manager, security, police 100 ([lesson 09.4](lesson-04.md)) |
| Going home late | safe transport; leave with a colleague if you can (professional judgment) |

### Guest safety and incidents

Keep exits and passages clear, keep the number of guests within the licensed occupancy shown on the sign at the entrance (a police licence condition for pubs, [lesson 09.5](lesson-05.md)), clean spills and broken glass on the floor at once, and watch for guests who are unwell. Report every injury, near miss (something that nearly caused harm), food-safety problem and emergency to the manager and write it in the [incident log](../../labs/module-09/incident-log.md). Emergency numbers in Israel: Magen David Adom 101, Police 100, Fire and Rescue 102 (professional judgment: well-known public numbers).

> [!NOTE]
> Licence conditions are from the uniform specification for item 4.2A on gov.il (Ministry of Health chapter and its 2008 annex) and the police specification for item 4.8; both copies are undated, so check the current version for your venue. The training regulations amendment is from Kovetz HaTakanot 12471 (14 July 2026, in force 16 October 2026). Checked 2026-10-18. Applications to a bar station are professional judgment.

## Step by step

### Food-safety check at opening

1. Read every fridge and freezer thermometer; write the readings, time and your initials.
2. Anything above 5 °C: tell the manager before using the contents.
3. Check dates and labels on juice, syrups, dairy and egg white; discard anything out of date.
4. Clean hands, clean clothes, hair tied, no jewellery except a wedding ring.
5. Hand-wash sink stocked with soap and paper towels.
6. Ice well clean, scoop in its holder, no glass near the ice.
7. Chemicals stored below and away from food and glass.

### Cut yourself during service

1. Stop and step away from the ice and garnish.
2. If any blood or glass may be in food, drink or ice: discard it and burn the ice.
3. Wash, cover with a waterproof plaster, put on a glove; tell the manager.
4. Deep cut or one that will not stop bleeding: first aid and 101.
5. Write it in the incident log.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- The audit sheet [labs/module-09/safety-self-audit.md](../../labs/module-09/safety-self-audit.md) (20 checks with the reason for each) and the printable [incident log](../../labs/module-09/incident-log.md).
- A fridge thermometer (a cheap one costs a few shekels; a kitchen probe thermometer also works), your home practice station ([lesson 01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md)), and your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: if you have no thermometer, do the audit and the incident report, and read your fridge's own display if it has one (note that built-in displays can be inaccurate).

### Time

45 minutes over three days: 20 minutes for the audit, 1 minute twice a day for the fridge log, 15 minutes for the incident report and 10 to review.

### Steps

1. **Audit.** Set up your home station as for a practice session. Go through the 20 checks; mark each pass or fail and write the fix for every fail.
2. **Fix and re-check.** Correct every fail you can (move cleaning spray away from the ice, label the syrup, put the thermometer in the fridge).
3. **Fridge log.** Put the thermometer in the middle shelf of your fridge. For three days, read it in the morning and evening, before opening the door for anything else, and write time, reading and initials. Note any reading above 5 °C and what you did.
4. **Incident report.** Write an incident-log entry for this case: during a busy practice session you cut your finger on a lime knife, a drop of blood fell on the cutting board next to the garnish tray, and you had to stop.
5. Compare your report with the model below and log the results.

### What you should see

Most home stations fail on the same few checks: cleaning products near the ice or glasses, unlabelled syrups and juices, no hand-washing routine between tasks, and jewellery (professional judgment). Home fridges often read 5–8 °C on the upper shelves or when the door is opened often; if yours reads above 5 °C, turn the dial colder and recheck the next day. A good incident report is short, factual and says what you did with the food.

<summary>Model incident report</summary>

"18/10, 19:40, home practice station. Cut left index finger with paring knife while slicing lime; small cut, bleeding stopped after 2 minutes of pressure. A drop of blood landed on the cutting board next to the garnish tray. Stopped work, washed and covered the cut with a waterproof plaster and a glove. Discarded all cut garnish and the lime on the board; washed and sanitised the board and knife; washed hands. Told: (at work: shift manager). Outcome: continued with a glove after 15 minutes. Cause: cutting towards my fingers on a wet board; next time board on a damp cloth and claw grip (lesson 01.4)."

### What this practice cannot teach

A home kitchen has no inspector, no commercial glasswasher, no cellar and no crowded floor; the audit builds the habits, but your employer's training, signed in the training log, is where you learn the real equipment, chemicals and emergency routes of each venue.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I audited my home station against 20 checks and fixed the fails I could.
- [ ] I logged my fridge temperature twice a day for three days and acted on any reading above 5 °C.
- [ ] I wrote an incident report for a cut and compared it with the model.
- [ ] I know what training to ask for on my first shift and that it should be recorded in the training log.

## Common mistakes

- **No thermometer, no records.** "The fridge feels cold" is not a record. Fix: a thermometer in every fridge, readings at opening and closing, kept three months.
- **Working sick or with an open cut.** Fix: tell the manager before the shift; cover small cuts with a plaster and glove; no garnish or ice with an open wound.
- **Chemicals next to the ice.** Fix: below and away from ice, glass and garnish, in labelled original containers.
- **Using equipment nobody showed you.** Fix: ask for training first and sign the training log.
- **Not reporting a near miss.** Fix: log it; near misses show where the next injury will happen.

## Key takeaways

- Licence conditions for Israeli food businesses set chilled food at 5 °C or below, frozen at −18 °C, hot at 65 °C or above, and temperature records kept at least three months.
- Food workers keep clean clothes, covered hair, short unpolished nails, no jewellery except a wedding ring, washed hands, and do not work with food while infectious or with open wounds.
- Prevent cross-contamination: tongs, clean sets, separate labelled storage, chemicals away from food and ice.
- The employer must train you about workplace risks and record it; from 16 October 2026 training is normally face to face in a language you understand.
- Report every injury, near miss and food-safety problem in the incident log; emergency numbers 101, 100, 102.
