# 09.3 · Allergens and ingredient questions

"Is there anything with nuts in this?" is one of the most important questions a guest can ask you, because the wrong answer can put them in hospital. This lesson teaches the 14 allergens that Israeli labelling now follows, where they hide in bar drinks and garnishes, what Israeli law does and does not require of a bar today, and a step-by-step routine for every allergy order: ask, check, tell, make it clean, and say "I can't confirm that" when you cannot. It ends with what to do if a guest has a severe reaction. In the hands-on part you build an allergen matrix for twenty classic drinks, check real labels at home, and practise the allergy scenario in the simulation.

## Why it matters

A guest says, "I'm allergic to nuts, what can I have?" and points at the Mai Tai. A rushed bartender says "Sure, no nuts in that" and shakes it with orgeat, an almond syrup ([lesson 06.4](../module-06/lesson-04.md)). Another bartender rinses a shaker that just held orgeat and uses it for the guest's "safe" Daiquiri. Both mistakes are invisible in the glass. A serious allergic reaction can start within minutes. Ignoring a declared allergy or guessing ingredients is a safety-critical failure in this course's final assessment (lesson 11.1), and it should be in your head on every shift.

## Core ideas

### Allergy, intolerance, preference

- A **food allergy** is a reaction of the immune system to a food protein. Even a trace can cause a reaction, and a severe one, **anaphylaxis**, can be life-threatening.
- An **intolerance** (for example lactose intolerance) is not an immune reaction; it can be very unpleasant but usually depends on the amount. **Coeliac disease** is an immune reaction to gluten and needs strict avoidance.
- A **preference** (vegan, no sugar, does not like coffee) is a choice.

You do not decide which one the guest has. Treat every declared allergy as serious. In Hebrew: אלרגיה (*alergia*, allergy), רגישות (*regishut*, sensitivity or intolerance).

### The 14 allergens

Israel has adopted the EU food-information regulation (No 1169/2011) under section 3A of the Protection of Public Health (Food) Law 2015, and its Annex II list of allergens and intolerance-causing substances (Ministry of Health guide, version of 29 June 2025):

| # | Allergen | Hebrew | Where it hides at a bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cereals with gluten (wheat, rye, barley, oats) | דגנים המכילים גלוטן (*dganim hamechilim gluten*) | beer; malt vinegar in some Worcestershire sauces; bar snacks |
| 2 | Crustaceans | סרטנאים (*sartana'im*) | rare; seafood garnishes |
| 3 | Eggs | ביצים (*beitzim*) | egg-white sours: Whiskey Sour (optional), Pisco Sour |
| 4 | Fish | דגים (*dagim*) | Worcestershire sauce (anchovy) in a Bloody Mary |
| 5 | Peanuts | בוטנים (*botnim*) | bar snacks; hands and scoops after snacks |
| 6 | Soybeans | סויה (*soya*) | soy milk; some snacks |
| 7 | Milk (including lactose) | חלב (*chalav*) | cream liqueurs, cream, milk drinks |
| 8 | Tree nuts (almond, hazelnut, walnut, cashew and others) | אגוזים (*egozim*) | orgeat (almond) in a Mai Tai; nut liqueurs |
| 9 | Celery | סלרי (*seleri*) | celery salt and celery sticks in a Bloody Mary |
| 10 | Mustard | חרדל (*chardal*) | some savoury drink mixes |
| 11 | Sesame | שומשום (*shumshum*) | tahini or sesame snacks; occasional modern syrups |
| 12 | Sulphur dioxide and sulphites (above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/l) | סולפיטים (*sulfitim*) | wine, sparkling wine, vermouth; some cordials and syrups (check the label) |
| 13 | Lupin | תורמוס (*turmus*) | rare at a bar |
| 14 | Molluscs | רכיכות (*rachichot*) | rare; clam juice in some savoury drinks |

The Ministry of Health guide adds that Israel has a special labelling rule for **fava beans** (פול, *ful*), because sensitivity to fava beans is common in Israel; it is labelled differently from the 14.

Three points matter for drinks:

- **Distillation exemptions.** Annex II exempts cereals, whey and nuts "used for making alcoholic distillates". A vodka distilled from wheat or a spirit distilled with nuts does not have to declare them. Anything added after distillation (a nut flavouring, cream, an egg) is not exempt (professional judgment on how this applies to a given bottle: check the producer).
- **Fining agents.** Fish gelatine or isinglass used to clarify beer and wine is exempt from the fish allergen. This matters to vegans more than to allergic guests.
- **No ingredient list on many bottles.** Under Article 16(4) of the same regulation, drinks above 1.2% ABV do not have to carry a list of ingredients, but allergens must still be declared. That is why a wine label says "contains sulphites" but a liqueur may list nothing else. If the label is silent and the producer's information does not answer the question, you cannot confirm the product is safe.

### What Israeli law requires of a bar today

| Product | Requirement | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Packaged food and drinks (bottles, cans, snacks) | Allergens from the list highlighted in the ingredient list (for example in bold); "may contain" (עלול להכיל, *alul lehachil*) for possible cross-contact is the producer's decision | statutory (EU rule adopted under Food Law s. 3A); the "may contain" approach is official guidance |
| Non-prepacked food and drinks (what you make or pour at the bar) | The EU rule requires allergen information, but in Israel this part is **deferred to 1 January 2032** by a temporary provision in the law | statutory deferral (MoH guide, 2025) |

So as of 2026-10-18, this course found no Israeli statutory duty for a bar to label or list allergens for the drinks it mixes. That does not make guessing acceptable. A bar that serves a guest something they declared they cannot eat risks the guest's health and its own reputation, and venues set written allergen procedures for exactly this reason (professional judgment). Older Israeli labels may list allergens in a separate "contains" (מכיל, *mechil*) line rather than in bold: read the whole label (professional judgment).

> [!NOTE]
> Allergen labelling rules in Israel are changing (EU-style labelling adopted from 2025; requirements for non-prepacked food from 2032). Checked on the Ministry of Health guide (version 2, 29 June 2025) and EUR-Lex on 2026-10-18. Product recipes change too: always read the current label.

### Cross-contact

**Cross-contact** means an allergen gets into a drink that does not contain it, through tools, ice, hands or garnish. At a bar the usual routes are:

- a shaker, jigger, bar spoon, muddler or strainer just used for orgeat, egg or cream, only rinsed in the dump sink;
- a garnish tray where nuts, celery salt or citrus sit side by side;
- the ice scoop or the bartender's hands after serving peanuts;
- a blender used for a cream drink.

The fix is a **clean set**: tools washed and rinsed (or fresh from the glasswasher), fresh ice from the well with a clean scoop, a garnish from a closed container, and washed hands.

### Allergy, honesty and the guest's choice

You can tell a guest what is in a drink, what the label says and how you made it. You cannot promise "100% safe" for products whose labels do not answer the question or for a busy shared bar. The honest answer is sometimes: "I can't confirm that bottle is nut-free, so I'd rather not make it. I can make you a Daiquiri with a clean shaker, or open a sealed bottle of something you can read yourself." Letting the guest read the label is part of the answer.

### When a reaction happens

> [!CAUTION]
> Signs of anaphylaxis include swelling of the throat or tongue, fast or difficult breathing, wheezing, difficulty swallowing, feeling faint, confusion or drowsiness, pale or blue lips, and a raised, itchy rash; it usually starts within minutes (NHS). Call Magen David Adom on **101** at once and say you think it is anaphylaxis. If the guest has an adrenaline auto-injector, help them use it. Lay them down with legs raised, or sit them up slowly if breathing is hard; do not let them stand or walk. If there is no improvement after 5 minutes, a second auto-injector may be used. Tell the manager, keep the drink and its bottles for checking. This course does not teach first aid: take a certified first-aid course.

## Step by step

### Every allergy order

1. **Stop** what you are doing and give the guest your full attention.
2. **Ask** exactly what they cannot have and how sensitive they are ("all tree nuts, or just almonds? Is a trace a problem?").
3. **Check** every ingredient of the drink against the recipe card (Modules 5 and 6), and read the label of every bottle, syrup and garnish involved.
4. **Tell** the guest what you found, including what you cannot confirm.
5. **Decide** together: a drink with no listed allergen and no doubtful product, or no drink.
6. **Make it clean**: wash hands, clean tools, fresh ice, garnish from a closed container.
7. **Mark** the ticket or tell the server ("allergy, nut-free, seat 3") so it is not mixed up at the pass.
8. **Serve** it yourself if you can, name it, and check in.

### Substituting in an allergy order

1. Leave out the allergen only if the drink still works (a Bloody Mary without Worcestershire) ([lesson 06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md)).
2. Read the label of any substitute before using it.
3. If the substitute's label is silent or unclear, do not use it.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- The worksheet [labs/module-09/allergen-matrix.csv](../../labs/module-09/allergen-matrix.csv) (twenty classic drinks with their spec ingredients and an empty column for allergens) and the instructions in [labs/module-09/allergen-worksheet.md](../../labs/module-09/allergen-worksheet.md). Reference answers: [labs/module-09/allergen-matrix-solution.md](../../labs/module-09/allergen-matrix-solution.md).
- A spreadsheet app (or paper), and three to five packaged products from your kitchen or a shop: for example a bottle of wine or vermouth, a beer, a syrup or cordial, a packet of nuts or crackers, a liqueur.
- For the clean-set drill: a shaker or jar, jigger, bar spoon, strainer, two bowls, washing-up liquid, a timer, and your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).
- Low-cost alternative: use product photos and ingredient lists from producers' websites instead of real bottles.

### Time

50 minutes: 20 for the matrix, 10 for the labels, 10 for the clean-set drill, 10 for the simulation and logging.

### Steps

1. **Matrix.** For each of the twenty drinks, write which of the 14 allergens are present from the spec alone, and which depend on the brand (mark "check label"). Use the table in Core ideas.
2. **Compare** with the solution. For every miss, write where the allergen was hiding.
3. **Labels.** For each product you collected, find: the allergens highlighted or listed, any "may contain" line, the ABV, and whether there is an ingredient list at all. Note one product where the label does not let you confirm a nut-free or gluten-free answer.
4. **Clean-set drill.** Put a teaspoon of a sticky syrup (or orgeat, if you have it) through your shaker, jigger and bar spoon as if making a drink. Then prepare a clean set as you would for an allergy order: wash, rinse, dry, wash your hands. Time it. Repeat once and try to be faster without skipping a step.
5. **Simulate.** Run the allergy scenario and aim for at least 6 of 8 points with no safety-critical choice.

[Simulation: Guest scenarios — allergy](../../simulations/guest-scenarios/index.html?preset=allergy)

6. Log the matrix score, your clean-set time and your simulation result.

### What you should see

Most learners spot egg and almond and miss celery salt, Worcestershire (fish), sulphites in vermouth and sparkling wine, and gluten in beer (professional judgment). At least one product you check will have no ingredient list (most spirits and liqueurs above 1.2% ABV), which is exactly the case where you must say "I can't confirm". A clean set at home usually takes 1–2 minutes; in service that is the cost of doing it right, so tell the guest it will take a moment.

### What this practice cannot teach

A worksheet cannot show you a guest's face when they are worried, or the pressure to "just make it" when the bar is full; and nothing at home prepares you for a real allergic reaction, which is why a certified first-aid course is worth taking before your first job.

## Common mistakes

- **"It should be fine."** Guessing is the most dangerous answer. Fix: check the spec and the labels; if you cannot confirm, say so and offer a drink you can.
- **Forgetting the hidden ones.** Celery salt, Worcestershire, vermouth, beer. Fix: learn the bar list in the table and the matrix.
- **Rinsing instead of cleaning.** A dump-sink rinse does not remove orgeat or egg. Fix: a washed or fresh set, fresh ice, clean hands.
- **Treating an allergy as a preference.** Fix: every declared allergy gets the full routine, even if the guest says "only a little".
- **Silent substitutes in an allergy order.** Fix: read the substitute's label first.
- **Not passing it on.** The server mixes up two similar drinks at the pass. Fix: mark the ticket and say "allergy" when you hand it over.

## Key takeaways

- Israel now follows the EU list of 14 allergens; packaged products must highlight them, and Israel adds a special rule for fava beans.
- For drinks made at the bar, Israel has deferred the EU allergen-information rule to 2032; you still never guess.
- Bar allergens hide in orgeat, egg white, cream liqueurs, Worcestershire, celery salt, wine, vermouth, sparkling wine and beer.
- Every allergy order: stop, ask, check, tell, decide, make it clean, mark, serve.
- In a severe reaction call 101 at once, help with the guest's auto-injector, keep them lying down, and tell the manager.
