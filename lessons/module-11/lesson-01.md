# 11.1 · How the final assessment works and is graded

This lesson explains the course's final assessment before you sit any part of it. You will see the five scored components and their weights, the minimum each one needs, the safety rule that can fail you on its own, how retakes work, and how to turn your results into one number and one decision. You will practise the arithmetic on three example candidates and then plan your own exam weeks. Read it once now, and come back to it in lesson 11.7 when you fill in your final report.

## Why it matters

On a trial shift (משמרת ניסיון, *mishmeret nisayon*: an unpaid or paid try-out shift before a bar hires you), the manager is not adding up points. They watch a few things at once: are the drinks right, are you fast enough, do you keep the station clean, and would they trust you alone with a drunk guest or an allergy question? One serious safety mistake ends the trial, however good your Negroni was. The final assessment is built the same way: a balanced score across skills, a floor under each skill, and a short list of mistakes that fail you whatever the total.

## Core ideas

### What the final assessment is, and what it is not

The final assessment (הערכה מסכמת, *ha'arakha mesakemet*) is six exam parts and a report. It checks whether you can do what Modules 1–10 taught, under exam conditions, and it gives you an honest picture of where you stand.

It is **not** a licence, certificate or accredited qualification. Lesson 01.1 said this, and it is still true at the end: the course's legal research (Module 9, checked 2026-10-18) found no personal licence for bartenders in Israeli law, and this course cannot grant any qualification. Your result is a self-assessment that helps you, and that you can describe truthfully to an employer (lesson 11.7 shows how).

### The five components

| Component | Weight | Where it comes from | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written knowledge | 20% | Module 11 quiz, your lesson-quiz average in Modules 1–10, and the guest scenarios exam (11.5) | 70% |
| Recipe recall | 25% | [11.2](lesson-02.md): recipe drill, exam preset: 20 random cards, each marked on ml (±5 ml), method, glass and ice, garnish | 80% |
| Practical technique | 25% | [11.3](lesson-03.md): a filmed exam set scored with the technique rubric (measuring within ±5%, build, stir, shake, strain, muddle, garnish, station reset, safety); optional review by a working bartender | 75% |
| Simulated service | 20% | [11.4](lesson-04.md): service simulation, exam preset: accuracy, ticket time and waste | 70% |
| Costing and operations | 10% | [11.6](lesson-06.md): costing and inventory assignment, checked with the costing calculator's exam preset | 70% |

The weights say what a bar cares about most: getting drinks right from memory and with clean technique is half of the grade. Costing is the smallest part because a new bartender rarely sets prices, but you should understand them.

### The written knowledge score

The written component blends three results:

$$\text{written} = 0.50 \times \text{Module 11 quiz} + 0.25 \times \text{lesson-quiz average} + 0.25 \times \text{guest scenarios exam}$$

- **Module 11 quiz:** the module quiz at the end of this module, ten scenario questions from the whole course. It is also an ordinary Academy quiz with the Academy's 70% pass mark.
- **Lesson-quiz average:** the average of your lesson-quiz scores in Modules 1–10, as the Academy shows them. If some are missing, take the ones you missed now; do not estimate.
- **Guest scenarios exam:** the score from lesson 11.5's simulation run (points out of 56, as a percentage).

### The overall decision

$$\text{weighted total} = 0.20\,W + 0.25\,R + 0.25\,T + 0.20\,S + 0.10\,C$$

where W, R, T, S, C are your five component scores in percent. Keep one decimal.

You **pass** the final assessment when all three are true:

1. The weighted total is **80.0 or more**.
2. **Every** component meets its minimum (70, 80, 75, 70, 70).
3. There is **no safety-critical failure** anywhere.

A high total does not rescue a component below its minimum. A bar cannot use someone who is excellent at costing but cannot remember specs.

### Safety-critical failures

Any one of these, in any part, means remediation and a retest of that part, whatever your total:

- Serving or recommending alcohol to a possible minor without an ID check.
- Continuing to serve a clearly intoxicated guest.
- Ignoring a declared allergy, or guessing what is in a drink.
- Scooping ice with a glass, or leaving broken glass in the ice.
- Unsafe knife handling.
- A raw-egg drink without a pasteurised egg white or aquafaba alternative, or without a warning.

These are the mistakes that hurt people or break the law (Module 9 covers the Israeli law; Modules 1 and 4 the physical safety). Lesson 11.5 is pass/fail on the guest items; lesson 11.3 checks the physical ones on film.

### Exam conditions, retakes and honesty

**Exam conditions** apply to every part:

- Closed book: no recipe library, notes or earlier answers open. The one exception is the costing assignment (11.6), where you may use the formulas and the price list, as a bar manager would.
- One sitting per part, with a timer.
- No restarting a simulation run: the first complete run of an exam preset counts.

**Retakes** are allowed for any part after at least one logged remediation session (practice aimed at what went wrong), with a new seed in the simulations so you meet new cards and tickets. The latest attempt taken under exam conditions counts. Write every attempt, with its date, on the [exam score sheet](../../labs/module-11/exam-score-sheet.md).

**Self-scoring is unverified.** You mark most parts yourself, with rubrics designed to make marking concrete (a rubric is a scoring guide that says what each score looks like). Research on professionals shows why this matters: a systematic review of doctors' self-assessments (Davis and colleagues, 2006) found that self-ratings often did not match external measures, and the least skilled were among the most likely to overrate themselves. So mark from evidence: weights on a scale, times on a stopwatch, film, the simulation's numbers. Where you can, ask a working bartender to look at your technique film. Nobody checks your score, which is exactly why it is only worth something if you are strict.

> [!NOTE]
> The weights, minimums, the 80% pass mark and the written-score blend are this course's grading model (course outline, checked 2026-10-18), not an industry or government standard. The simulations calculate the same numbers; the formulas are shown in each exam lesson.

## Step by step

### Working out your result

1. For each part, write the raw result on the score sheet (points, rubric total, the simulation's numbers).
2. Turn each raw result into a percentage: points ÷ maximum × 100 (for example 66 / 80 = 82.5%).
3. Calculate the written score with the 50/25/25 blend.
4. Multiply each component by its weight and add the five products. Round to one decimal.
5. Check each component against its minimum.
6. Check the safety-critical list for every part.
7. Decide: **pass** if all three conditions hold; otherwise name exactly which part needs remediation and a retest.

### A worked example

Dana scored 80% on the Module 11 quiz, has a lesson-quiz average of 82% and scored 68% on the guest scenarios exam with no safety-critical choice. Her other results: recall 68 / 80 (85%), technique 16 / 20 (80%), service 79, costing 32 / 40 (80%).

- Written = 0.50 × 80 + 0.25 × 82 + 0.25 × 68 = 40 + 20.5 + 17 = **77.5%** (minimum 70: met).
- Weighted total = 77.5 × 0.20 + 85 × 0.25 + 80 × 0.25 + 79 × 0.20 + 80 × 0.10 = 15.50 + 21.25 + 20.00 + 15.80 + 8.00 = **80.6**.
- Every minimum is met and there is no safety failure: **pass**, narrowly. Her report in 11.7 would still name the guest scenarios and service as areas to improve.

### Planning your exam weeks

1. Revise first: one recall session per day for a week (lesson 05.1's spaced repetition) and one technique drill (04.8).
2. Do the parts in the module's order, one or two per day, so a bad day does not spoil everything.
3. Book a quiet hour with your station set up for 11.3, and a laptop or tablet for 11.4.
4. Leave a week at the end for retakes before you write the report.

## Common mistakes

- **Averaging the five scores instead of weighting them.** Recall and technique count 25% each, costing only 10%. Fix: multiply each by its weight.
- **Treating the total as the only test.** A component below its minimum fails the assessment whatever the total. Fix: check minimums separately.
- **Forgetting the safety rule.** One scoop of ice with a glass fails the technique part. Fix: check the safety list for every part.
- **Restarting a simulation until the score looks good.** That measures luck, not skill. Fix: first complete run counts; retake later with a new seed after remediation.
- **Generous self-marking.** "Almost 30 ml" is not within ±5%. Fix: mark from the scale, stopwatch, film and simulation numbers.

## Key takeaways

- Five components: written 20%, recall 25%, technique 25%, service 20%, costing 10%.
- Written = 0.50 × Module 11 quiz + 0.25 × lesson-quiz average + 0.25 × guest scenarios exam.
- Pass = weighted total ≥ 80.0, every minimum met (70, 80, 75, 70, 70) and no safety-critical failure.
- Retake any part after logged remediation; the latest attempt under exam conditions counts.
- The assessment is self-scored and unverified, and it is not a licence or certificate.
