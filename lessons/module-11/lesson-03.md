# 11.3 · Practical technique exam

This is the practical technique part of the final assessment, worth 25% of your grade. You film yourself making the same exam set twice, with water, tea, juice and syrup: measured pours, a built highball, a stirred and strained drink, a shaken and double-strained drink, a muddled drink, garnishes, and a station reset after each round. Then you turn what the scale, the stopwatch and the film show into scores on the course technique rubric. You need 75%, and any safety-critical failure fails this part whatever the score. If you can, a working bartender watches the film and scores it too.

## Why it matters

On a trial shift the manager watches your hands before they taste anything. A level jigger, a quiet stir, a tight seal on the tins, a clean double strain and a station that looks the same at midnight as at seven tell them you have practised. A wobbling jigger, ice chips in a coupe or a glass dipped into the ice well tell them the opposite in seconds. This exam puts your technique on film so you can see what they would see.

## Core ideas

### The exam set

You did every movement in the [04.8 technique drills](../module-04/lesson-08.md), ten reps each. The exam is shorter and closer to service: the same set of drinks twice, in order, with a reset between. Each round takes about 12–15 minutes.

| Step | What you make | What is checked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Measuring | 4 jigger pours (30, 30, 15, 15 ml) weighed on a tared tin | each within ±5% (30 ml: 28.5–31.5 g; 15 ml: 14.3–15.7 g) |
| 2 Build | highball: 45 ml cold tea, 10 ml citrus, 120 ml soda | order glass → ice → tea → juice → soda → lift; tea within ±5% |
| 3 Stir | 70 ml tea and water stirred 20–30 s, strained into a chilled coupe | spoon on the glass, quiet, no splash, time on the clock |
| 4 Shake | 60 ml water, 20 ml citrus, 15 ml 1:1 syrup, 10–15 s | sealed, no leak, two hands, tins open in under 3 s |
| 5 Strain | the shaken drink, double strained | no ice chips, no drips |
| 6 Muddle | lime wedges and syrup, then mint pressed gently, ice, soda | pressing not shredding, leaves whole, sturdy vessel |
| 7 Garnish | lemon or orange twist on the stirred drink, lime slice on the highball | twist in 20 s or less, oil expressed, placed the same way both rounds |
| 8 Free pour (optional) | two 4-count pours of 30 ml | within ±10% |
| 9 Station reset | after each round | complete in 90 s or less |
| 10 Safety | throughout | scoop, knife, glass, no raw egg |

The full instructions, the evidence log and the scoring table are on the [exam sheet](../../labs/module-11/technique-exam-sheet.md).

### From evidence to rubric

The course has one [technique rubric](../../templates/technique-rubric.md): ten lines scored 0, 1 or 2, out of 20 (or 18 if you skip the optional free pour). In the exam, a score comes from evidence across both rounds:

- **Measuring:** 2 if all 8 weighed pours are in tolerance, 1 if 6–7, 0 if 5 or fewer.
- **Build, stir, shake, strain, muddle, garnish:** 2 if both rounds meet the standard, 1 if one round does, 0 if neither.
- **Station reset:** 2 if both resets are complete in 90 seconds, 1 if one is, 0 if neither.
- **Safety:** 2 for no fault, 1 for one minor fault corrected at once, 0 for a repeated minor fault.

Score = total ÷ 20 × 100 (or ÷ 18). **Minimum: 75%**, which is 15 / 20 or 14 / 18.

### Safety-critical failures on film

Separate from the score, three things fail this part outright: scooping ice with a glass, leaving broken glass in the ice, and unsafe knife handling (cutting towards your hand, a board that slides, a knife left near the edge or dropped into a sink). A raw-egg foam without pasteurised egg white or aquafaba would also fail it; the exam set uses no egg, so if you add a foam, use aquafaba. Watch both films specifically for these before you add up any points.

> [!CAUTION]
> Knives and glass: work on a board on a damp cloth, cut away from your hand, put the knife down flat away from the edge, check glasses for chips, and use an ice scoop only. If a glass breaks near your ice, stop, empty and clean the ice container, and start the round again ([lesson 01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)). Never muddle in a thin glass.

### Optional review by a working bartender

Self-scored technique is the least reliable part of the assessment, because you are judging your own film. A review by someone who works behind a bar is the best check this course can offer. Ask a bartender you know, or a bar where you have done a barback shift, to watch the two films and fill in the [reviewer form](../../labs/module-11/reviewer-form.md). Where their score and yours differ, record the lower one. It is informal: a review is not a certificate, and the course does not claim it is.

> [!NOTE]
> The standards (±5% measuring, 20–30 s stir, 10–15 s shake, 90 s reset) are this course's practice standards from lessons 01.3, 03.2 and Module 4 (checked 2026-10-17), not an industry certification standard.

## Step by step

### Setting up (15 minutes, not filmed)

1. Set up your station as in [lesson 01.7](../module-01/lesson-07.md): ice well (a bowl), scoop, tins, mixing glass, jigger, bar spoon, strainers, board on a damp cloth, knife, peeler, glasses, scale, stopwatch.
2. Prepare the ingredients: cold tea, water, 1:1 syrup, lemons or limes juiced, lime wedges and slices cut, mint in water, soda or ginger ale cold.
3. Chill two coupes. Put the phone on its stand and check the frame.

### Each round (filmed, one continuous take)

1. Say "Round 1" and the time. Start the stopwatch.
2. Measuring: tare the tin, pour 30 ml, read the scale out loud, empty; repeat for 30, 15 and 15.
3. Build the highball, weighing the tea pour. Garnish with a lime slice.
4. Stir and strain into a chilled coupe. Cut, express and place a twist.
5. Shake and double strain into the second coupe.
6. Muddle and build the third highball.
7. Optional: two free pours of 30 ml on the scale.
8. Station reset: start a lap time, reset, stop the lap.
9. Repeat everything for round 2.

### Scoring (after both rounds)

1. Watch both films once only for safety-critical failures and tick the checklist.
2. Watch again and fill in the evidence log: grams, seconds, and pass or fail per step per round.
3. Convert to rubric scores with the table on the exam sheet; add up; turn into a percentage.
4. Record the score on your exam score sheet. If a reviewer scores the film later, update to the lower total.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- Your home station from [lesson 01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md): Boston shaker or two jars, mixing glass or jar, jigger, bar spoon, Hawthorne or julep strainer and a fine (tea) strainer, muddler or rolling pin end, peeler or paring knife, board, damp cloth, ice scoop or a large metal spoon.
- Two coupes or small wine glasses, two highballs or tall glasses, a tin or sturdy glass for muddling.
- Ice, cold black tea, water, 1:1 syrup, 3–4 lemons or limes, a few mint leaves, soda water or ginger ale.
- A kitchen scale (1 g), a phone stopwatch, a phone stand.
- The [exam sheet](../../labs/module-11/technique-exam-sheet.md), the [technique rubric](../../templates/technique-rubric.md), the [station checklist](../../templates/station-checklist.md), the [exam score sheet](../../labs/module-11/exam-score-sheet.md); optional [reviewer form](../../labs/module-11/reviewer-form.md).
- Low-cost alternative: jars instead of tins, a tea strainer for the fine strain, cups instead of coupes, a stack of books as a phone stand.

### Time

75 minutes: 15 to set up, 30 for two filmed rounds, 30 to watch, log and score.

### Steps

1. Set up and prepare as in "Setting up" above.
2. Film round 1 and round 2 as in "Each round", without stopping the camera inside a round. If something goes wrong (a spill, a missed step), keep going: recovery is part of the exam.
3. Safety check first: watch both films for the four safety-critical items and tick them on the exam sheet.
4. Log the evidence and convert it to rubric scores.
5. Record the total and percentage on your score sheet.
6. Write your two weakest rubric lines and one specific fix for each; they go into your performance report.
7. Optional: send the films to a working bartender with the reviewer form.

### What you should see

After the 04.8 drills and a few weeks of Modules 5–8, most learners score between 70% and 90% (professional judgment). Measuring and garnish are the usual low lines: 15 ml pours are harder to get within ±5% than 30 ml pours (0.7 g is a small margin), and twists slow down when you are filmed. Shake, build and reset usually score 2. A failed reset almost always means tools were scattered during the round, not that the reset itself was slow. If you score above 90% on your own but a reviewer scores you lower, believe the reviewer.

### What this practice cannot teach

Water does not taste wrong when you over-dilute it, and your kitchen is not a crowded bar with a server waiting. The exam shows that your movements are clean and consistent; real drinks, real guests and a real trial shift show the rest.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I filmed two complete rounds of the exam set in one session.
- [ ] I checked both films for safety-critical failures before scoring.
- [ ] I scored every rubric line from logged evidence, not from memory.
- [ ] I scored at least 75% with no safety-critical failure, or I wrote a remediation plan and a retake date.
- [ ] I recorded the score (and a reviewer's score, if any, taking the lower) on my score sheet.

## Common mistakes

- **Stopping the camera after a mistake.** You lose the evidence and the recovery. Fix: keep going; recovery is part of the job.
- **Scoring from memory.** "It felt good" is not evidence. Fix: grams on the scale, seconds on the stopwatch, the film.
- **Reading the scale without taring.** Every pour is wrong by the tin's weight. Fix: tare before each pour and say the number out loud.
- **A scattered station.** The reset fails because tools were left everywhere during the round. Fix: put each tool back as you finish with it.
- **Missing a safety failure on your own film.** Fix: watch once only for safety, before you think about points.

## Key takeaways

- Two filmed rounds of the same exam set, with a station reset after each, using water, tea, juice and syrup.
- Score the technique rubric from evidence: 2 = both rounds, 1 = one round, 0 = neither (measuring by count of pours in tolerance).
- Minimum 75% (15 / 20 or 14 / 18); technique is 25% of the final grade.
- Ice scooped with a glass, broken glass left in ice, unsafe knife handling or unsafe raw egg fails this part whatever the score.
- A working bartender's review is the best check; record the lower score.
