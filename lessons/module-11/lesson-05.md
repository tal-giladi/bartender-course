# 11.5 · Guest scenarios exam

This is the guest part of the final assessment. You run all seven guest situations from Module 9 in the simulation's exam preset, in random order, and then answer three new cases in writing: a possible minor at a birthday table, an intoxicated guest who wants "one for the road", and a guest with an egg allergy who orders a drink made with egg white. The simulation score counts towards your written knowledge component. The part itself is pass or fail on safety: one safety-critical choice anywhere, in the simulation or in writing, means remediation and a retest, however good the rest was.

## Why it matters

Most mistakes behind a bar cost money: a remade drink, a wasted pour, a slow ticket. A few cost far more. Serving a 17-year-old can lead to a police closure order for the bar and a criminal case; serving a drunk guest who then drives can end a life; guessing about an allergen can send a guest to hospital. A bar can train you to be faster. It cannot afford a bartender who gets these wrong even once, which is why this part is pass or fail on safety.

## Core ideas

### Two parts

| Part | What you do | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Simulation exam | all seven situations in random order: possible minor, intoxicated guest, unclear order, allergy, complaint, strong drink request, alcohol-free request | a score (points out of 56, as a percentage) that feeds the written component; a list of any safety-critical choices |
| Written cases | three new situations answered in writing, closed book | a rubric score per case; a check that your safe choices transfer to situations you have not practised |

### How the simulation exam is scored

It is the same model as [lesson 09.7](../module-09/lesson-07.md): each situation has four decision points, each choice scores 2 (safe and professional), 1 (safe but weak) or 0 (poor), and some 0-choices are flagged safety-critical. In the exam preset the seven situations come in random order with no review between them; the end screen shows each path, the model path, the total and any safety-critical choices.

$$\text{guest scenarios exam \%} = \frac{\text{points}}{56} \times 100$$

This percentage goes into your written knowledge score with a weight of 25% (lesson 11.1).

### How the written cases are scored

Each response is scored with the [scenario rubric](../../labs/module-09/scenario-rubric.md) from Module 9: Safety and law, Guest care, Accuracy, Team and Clarity, 0–2 each. A response passes with at least 7 of 10 **and** a 2 in Safety and law. A 0 in Safety and law is a safety-critical failure.

### Pass or fail on safety

The guest part is **passed** when there is no safety-critical choice in the simulation and no 0 in Safety and law in any written case. The safety-critical items it checks are the ones from lesson 11.1:

- serving or recommending alcohol to a possible minor without an ID check;
- continuing to serve a clearly intoxicated guest;
- ignoring a declared allergy or guessing ingredients;
- a raw-egg drink without a pasteurised or aquafaba alternative or a warning (case C).

A low but safe score is not a failure of this part: it lowers your written component and becomes an area to improve in your report. An unsafe choice is a failure whatever the score.

### The rules behind the cases

The cases rest on the rules taught in Module 9, checked there on 2026-10-18:

| Situation | Rule | Type | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Possible minor | no sale or serving of alcohol to anyone under 18; you are presumed to have known unless the guest showed an ID saying they were adult (Penal Law s. 193A); bars' licence condition: anyone who wants to drink shows ID | statutory + licence condition | [09.5](../module-09/lesson-05.md) |
| Intoxicated guest | no supply of alcohol to a drunk person, or encouraging them to drink, by the owner or a worker (Penal Law s. 193(c)) | statutory | [09.4](../module-09/lesson-04.md) |
| Allergy | egg is one of the 14 allergens; never guess, prevent cross-contact with a clean set | statutory list for packaged food + professional standard | [09.3](../module-09/lesson-03.md) |
| Raw egg | pasteurised egg white or aquafaba only | course rule | [04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md) |

> [!NOTE]
> Laws change. The legal references are from Module 9's research on 2026-10-18 (Penal Law on Nevo, consolidated to 2 July 2026). The simulation and the rubric are this course's educational model, not an official standard, and house policies on ID and refusals vary between bars. This is not legal advice.

## Step by step

### Simulation exam

1. Close your notes and the Module 9 lessons.
2. Open the guest scenarios simulation with the exam preset and write down the seed.
3. Answer all seven situations in the order they come. Take your time: there is no clock, and in real life you would also stop and think before refusing someone.
4. At the end, copy the results block: points, percentage, and any safety-critical choices.

### Written cases

1. Open [guest-exam-cases.md](../../labs/module-11/guest-exam-cases.md). Set a 30-minute timer.
2. For each case, write what you would say in quotation marks and what you would do, step by step, and the rule behind your main decision.
3. When the timer stops, score each response with the scenario rubric, Safety and law first.
4. Only then read the model answers and correct your scores if you were generous.

### Deciding the result

1. Any safety-critical choice in the simulation, or a 0 in Safety and law in any case? Then this part is **not passed**.
2. Otherwise it is **passed**. Record the simulation percentage on your score sheet for the written component.
3. If not passed: re-read the Module 9 lesson for the situation you failed, rerun its single-situation preset until you pass it twice, rewrite the failed case, then retake the exam preset with a new seed and three cases you have not seen (ask someone to write a new case, or rewrite one of the Module 9 situations with different details).

## Hands-on

### What you need

- A device with a browser for the guest scenarios simulation.
- [guest-exam-cases.md](../../labs/module-11/guest-exam-cases.md), the [scenario rubric](../../labs/module-09/scenario-rubric.md) and your [exam score sheet](../../labs/module-11/exam-score-sheet.md).
- Low-cost alternative: if you cannot use the simulation, answer the seven situations in writing with the [scenario response sheet](../../labs/module-09/scenario-response-sheet.md), score each with the rubric, and use the total rubric points (out of 70) as your percentage instead of the simulation score.

### Time

60 minutes: 20 for the simulation exam, 30 for the written cases, 10 to score, compare and record.

### Steps

1. Run the simulation exam, all seven situations, once.

[Simulation: Guest scenarios — final exam](../../simulations/guest-scenarios/index.html?preset=exam)

2. Record the points, percentage and any safety-critical choices on your score sheet.
3. Answer the three written cases in 30 minutes, closed book.
4. Score each case with the rubric, then compare with [guest-exam-cases-solution.md](../../labs/module-11/guest-exam-cases-solution.md).
5. Decide the result: passed, or remediation and retest.
6. Write one sentence for your performance report: the situation you handled best and the one you would most like to practise on a real shift.

### What you should see

After Module 9, most learners make no safety-critical choice and score between 75% and 95% in the simulation (professional judgment). The points usually go on the softer decisions: checking back an unclear order, offering an alternative, involving the manager, writing the incident log. In the written cases, the common slips are accepting a student card or a friend's word in case A, offering a "small one" or a soft drink "with just a splash" in case B, and promising aquafaba is safe without asking about traces or where it was made in case C. Any of these is a 0 in Safety and law.

<details>
<summary>Example result</summary>

Simulation: 48 / 56 = 85.7%, no safety-critical choice (lost points: no check-back on the unclear order, no incident log after the intoxicated guest, a weak alternative for the strong-drink guest). Written cases: A 10/10; B 9/10 (manager told only at the end); C 8/10 (asked about traces but forgot to mark the ticket; one vague sentence). Result: passed. 85.7% goes into the written component.

</details>

### What this practice cannot teach

Choosing the right answer on a screen or on paper is easier than saying it to a group of friendly, insistent people at 01:00. Practise the exact words out loud, and on your first shifts, watch how experienced staff refuse service; the words matter less than staying calm, private and firm.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I ran the guest scenarios exam once, closed book, and recorded the score and seed.
- [ ] I answered all three written cases in 30 minutes, closed book.
- [ ] I scored every response with the rubric, Safety and law first, before reading the model answers.
- [ ] I had no safety-critical choice, or I wrote a remediation plan and a retest date.

## Common mistakes

- **Accepting a friend's word, a phone photo or a student card.** Fix: a physical, valid document with a date of birth, or no alcohol.
- **"Just one more" or "a small one".** Any alcohol to a clearly intoxicated guest is a failure. Fix: water, food, a taxi, the manager.
- **Promising a safe swap without checking.** Fix: ask about traces, check where the alternative was made, clean set, mark the ticket; if you cannot be sure, say so.
- **Refusing rudely or in public.** Safe but costly in Guest care. Fix: private, calm, no labels, an alternative.
- **Scoring generously before reading the model answers.** Fix: score Safety and law first, strictly, then compare.

## Key takeaways

- Two parts: the seven situations in the simulation's exam preset, and three new written cases.
- The simulation percentage (points ÷ 56) is 25% of your written knowledge score.
- The part is pass or fail on safety: any safety-critical choice, or a 0 in Safety and law, means remediation and a retest.
- The rules: no alcohol to under-18s without a valid ID (s. 193A), none to drunk guests (s. 193(c)), never guess an allergen, safe egg only.
