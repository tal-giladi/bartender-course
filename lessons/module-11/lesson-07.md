# 11.7 · Your final performance report

This is the last lesson of the course and the practical piece of Module 11. You bring together everything you scored in lessons 11.2 to 11.6 and the Module 11 quiz into one performance report: your five component scores, the weighted total, the safety check, your result, three strengths, three areas to improve and a remediation plan with dates. Then you turn the report into two practical things: an honest sentence you can say to a bar manager, and a list of next steps towards your first shift. The report is yours; nobody checks it, so it is only useful if it is true.

## Why it matters

Sooner or later a bar manager will ask, "So what can you do?" A vague answer ("I did a course, I know cocktails") sounds like everyone else. A specific one ("I can make thirty classics to spec, my measured pours are within 5%, I've run a twenty-ticket simulated rush at 88% accuracy, and I haven't worked a real shift yet") sounds like someone who knows where they stand, which is exactly what managers want from a beginner. The performance report is how you find that answer.

## Core ideas

### The report

The course's [performance report](../../templates/performance-report.md) template has five parts:

| Part | What goes in | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Scores | the five component scores, minimums, weighted points and total | your [exam score sheet](../../labs/module-11/exam-score-sheet.md) |
| Safety-critical items | six checkboxes | 11.3 film, 11.5 simulation and written cases, every other part |
| Result | pass, or remediation needed (and which part) | the three conditions of lesson 11.1 |
| Strengths and areas to improve | three each, with evidence | error logs and debriefs from 11.2–11.6 |
| Remediation plan | area, practice, frequency, retest date | your areas to improve |

### Deciding the result, once more

From [lesson 11.1](lesson-01.md): you pass when the weighted total is at least 80.0, every component meets its minimum (written 70, recall 80, technique 75, service 70, costing 70) and there is no safety-critical failure. Use the latest attempt of each part taken under exam conditions, and keep the attempt history: it shows what you fixed, which is often more convincing than the final number.

If you have not passed yet, the report is still worth writing: it tells you exactly which part to remediate and retake, and the remediation plan becomes your next two or three weeks.

### Evidence-based strengths and areas

A strength or an area to improve is only useful when it is specific and points to evidence:

| Vague | Specific, with evidence |
|---|---|
| "Good at cocktails" | "All twelve Module 5 drinks fully correct in the recall exam" |
| "Need to be faster" | "Two dead drinks in wave 1 of the service exam: I build before I shake" |
| "Customer service" | "Guest scenarios 68%: I skip the check-back on unclear orders" |
| "Measuring" | "15 ml pours: one of four out of tolerance in each technique round" |

The best places to look: the error log from the recall exam (11.2), the lowest rubric lines from the technique exam (11.3), the service debrief (11.4), the points you lost in the guest scenarios (11.5), and the points you lost in the costing assignment (11.6).

### A remediation plan that works

For each area to improve, write one specific practice, how often, and a retest date: "20 weighed 15 ml pours, four times a week, re-film on 8 November" rather than "practise measuring". Plan two to three weeks, not two months. Retests follow the same exam conditions and use a new seed; update the report when you have the new score.

### Describing your result honestly

The course is not a licence, a certificate or an accredited qualification, and your scores are self-assessed. Research on self-assessment shows that people often rate themselves differently from external measures, and the least skilled tend to overrate themselves the most (Davis and colleagues, 2006). So describe what you did and how you measured it, not a title:

- **Do say:** what the course covered, roughly how many hours, how you practised (water drills, filmed technique, simulated service), your self-assessed result, and that you have not yet worked a real shift.
- **Do not say:** "certified bartender", "licensed", "diploma", or a score without saying it was self-assessed.
- **If someone reviewed your technique film,** you can say so, but not that they certified you.

Managers hire beginners for attitude and reliability as much as skill (professional judgment). An honest, specific description is the strongest thing you can bring.

### What home practice could not give you

Lesson 01.1 listed what this course cannot teach, and it is worth repeating at the end: real draught foam, a full rush with real guests, a commercial glasswasher, reading a crowded room, and the physical stamina of an eight-hour shift. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that bartenders typically learn through a few weeks of on-the-job training, and that some start as servers or as bartender helpers (barbacks) and move up (checked 2026-10-18). That is your next step.

> [!NOTE]
> The grading model and the report template are this course's (checked 2026-10-18). Job titles, hiring practices and pay vary between bars and change over time; statements about hiring are professional judgment.

## Step by step

1. **Collect your results.** Open your exam score sheet and make sure every part has a latest attempt under exam conditions, with its date.
2. **Fill in the scores table** of the performance report: score, minimum, met or not, weighted points; add them up to one decimal.
3. **Tick the safety-critical items** honestly, looking back at the 11.3 film and the 11.5 results.
4. **Write the result:** pass, or remediation needed for a named part.
5. **Write three strengths and three areas to improve,** each with its evidence.
6. **Write the remediation plan:** practice, frequency, retest date for each area.
7. **Write your honest CV line** (two sentences).
8. **Write your next steps:** at least two bars to ask for barback or junior shifts, and how you will prepare for a trial shift.
9. **Set a date** to update the report after your retests.

## Hands-on

### What you need

- The [performance report](../../templates/performance-report.md) template and your filled [exam score sheet](../../labs/module-11/exam-score-sheet.md).
- Your error logs, rubrics and debriefs from lessons 11.2 to 11.6.
- The [worked example](../../labs/module-11/performance-report-example-solution.md), to compare after you finish.
- Low-cost alternative: the template copied onto paper.

### Time

60 minutes: 15 to collect and calculate, 25 for strengths, areas and the remediation plan, 10 for the CV line and next steps, 10 to compare with the example and correct.

### Steps

1. Fill in the performance report, following "Step by step".
2. Check your weighted total with the formula from lesson 11.1, by hand or in a spreadsheet.
3. Compare your report with the worked example. Check three things: does every strength and area name evidence; does every remediation line have a frequency and a date; does your CV line say "self-assessed" and say you have not yet worked a real shift (if you have not)?
4. Optional: show the report and your technique film to a working bartender and ask what they would add to your areas to improve.
5. Put your retest dates in your calendar and log the session in your [practice log](../../templates/practice-log.md).

### What you should see

A complete report fits on two pages. Most learners find that their strengths are in the parts they practised most often (recall and the movements of Module 4) and their areas to improve are in the parts that are hardest to practise alone (guest conversations and the first wave of a rush). If your report shows no area scoring below 85%, look again at your lowest rubric lines and debriefs: there is always something specific to improve, and a bar will appreciate that you know what it is.

### What this practice cannot teach

A report cannot tell you how you will feel when the bar is three deep and a guest is shouting an order. It tells you what you can rely on when that happens, and what to keep practising until it does.

### Completion checklist

- [ ] I filled in every part of the performance report from my score sheet.
- [ ] I decided my result with all three conditions.
- [ ] I wrote three strengths and three areas to improve, each with evidence.
- [ ] I wrote a remediation plan with practice, frequency and retest dates.
- [ ] I wrote an honest CV line and at least two next steps.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying scores without dates or attempts.** Fix: keep the attempt history; it shows what you fixed.
- **Vague strengths and areas.** Fix: a number, a drink, a step or a situation for each.
- **A remediation plan without dates.** Fix: practice, frequency and a retest date for every line.
- **Overclaiming.** "Certified bartender" is not true. Fix: describe what you did and say it was self-assessed.
- **Stopping at the report.** Fix: write the next steps and ask a bar for a barback shift this month.

## Key takeaways

- The performance report collects five scores, the weighted total, the safety check and the result.
- Pass = total ≥ 80.0, every minimum met, no safety-critical failure; the latest exam-condition attempt counts.
- Strengths and areas need evidence; the remediation plan needs practice, frequency and dates.
- Describe your result honestly: self-assessed, what you practised, and no certificate claim.
- The next step is real shifts, starting as a barback or junior bartender.
